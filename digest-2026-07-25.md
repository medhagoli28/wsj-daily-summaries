# WSJ Deep Digest — 2026-07-25

*Today's stories, researched from non-WSJ sources: SpaceX flies its first Starship since the IPO while the stock sags, Alphabet's blowout quarter gets punished for a $200 billion capex bill, and a week whipsawed by AI spending fears and $100 oil finally steadies as crude retreats on peace talks. (The day's remaining headlines carried over from the July 24 digest and are covered there.)*

## 💻 Tech

**1. SpaceX Launches Another Starship, and This Time the Market Is Watching** — SpaceX flew Starship Flight 13 from Starbase, Texas on Friday, July 24, 2026 — the first test flight since the company's June IPO, which raised $85.7 billion and now trades on the Nasdaq under SPCX. The V3 Super Heavy booster lit all 33 engines at liftoff, and the upper stage deployed 20 Starlink V3 satellites about 18 minutes in — the first V3s ever flown — before making what SpaceX called its softest splashdown yet, coming to rest intact in the Indian Ocean and transmitting telemetry with a largely undamaged heat shield. Not everything was clean: on the booster's landing burn only about 10 of 13 planned engines relit and roughly five appeared to be firing at Gulf splashdown, a harder-than-planned impact. Wall Street's reaction was muted — SPCX closed Friday at $115.07, down 2.68%, roughly 15% below the $135 IPO price and about 47% off its post-listing high near $217, barely above its all-time low of $110.85. Traders were arguably more focused on the August 4 debut earnings report and the lock-up expiration that frees roughly 911.5 million shares (about $116 billion) starting August 6 than on the rocket itself. *Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/24/spacex-launches-massive-starship-rocket-in-first-test-flight-since-ipo.html), [Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/07/25/super-heavy-starship-rocket-chalks-up-mostly-successful-test-flight/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/21/spacex-spcx-earnings-lock-up-expiration.html).*

**2. Google Has the Muscle to Overpower Spending Worries** — Alphabet's Q2 2026 report on July 22 beat on nearly every line — revenue of about $119.8 billion (up roughly 24%) versus a ~$117 billion consensus, Search revenue up 17% to $63.3 billion, and Google Cloud revenue up 82% year over year to $24.8 billion against expectations near 63% growth — yet the stock fell 7% on Thursday, July 23, with GOOG closing at $316.68, down $25.23. The trigger was capex: management lifted 2026 capital-spending guidance to $195–$205 billion from $180–$190 billion, posted $44.9 billion of Q2 capex (up about 101% year over year), and warned 2027 spending would "increase significantly." Against $39.1 billion of operating cash flow, that produced roughly negative $5.9 billion in free cash flow — Alphabet's first negative FCF print since going public more than two decades ago. The bull case rests on demand evidence the selloff arguably ignored: Cloud backlog grew more than $50 billion sequentially to about $514 billion, Cloud operating margin more than tripled to roughly 35.6%, and the Gemini app claims roughly 950 million monthly users. Bank of America's Justin Post reiterated a Buy with a $430 target (about 30% upside from Thursday's close), and the broader Street consensus sat near $421–$428, with targets ranging from $350 to a high of $515. *Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/22/google-earnings-q2-goog-live-updates.html), [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/07/60656208/4-reasons-buy-alphabet-stock-capex-shock-bofa), [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4617114-alphabet-signals-195b-205b-2026-capex-while-expanding-third-party-capacity-as-a-bridge).*

## 📈 Markets & Finance

**1. Wild Markets Week Settles Down** — The week of July 20–24, 2026 was whipsawed by two shocks at once: an AI-capex scare and a Middle East oil spike. Thursday, July 23 was the low point — the Dow fell 506.93 points (0.97%) to 51,711.65, the S&P 500 dropped 1.21% to 7,408.30 and the Nasdaq Composite sank 2.15% to 25,137.69, as Alphabet lost about 7% and Tesla roughly 14% after earnings and the "Magnificent Seven" shed close to $800 billion of market value in a single session, while Brent crude jumped about 7% to settle at $100.69, its first close above $100 since May 26. Friday brought stabilization: the Dow rose 235.60 points (0.46%) to 51,947.25 and the S&P 500 edged up 0.05% to 7,411.98, though the Nasdaq slipped 0.64% to 24,975.82. For the week, the S&P 500 fell about 0.6% and the Nasdaq about 2.1% — back-to-back weekly losses — while the 10-year Treasury yield notched its biggest weekly rise since May 11 at roughly +14 basis points, peaking near 4.70% (an 18-month high) Thursday before easing to about 4.68% Friday, and crude still finished the week up sharply. The VIX ended the stretch around 18.5–18.7, elevated but well short of panic, even as Trump's new Section 301 tariffs of 10%–12.5% on most U.S. imports took effect during the week. *Sources: [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-july-24-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-081854465.html), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/23/stock-market-today-live-updates.html), [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/07/23/stock-market-today-july-23-alphabet-slides-7-after-announcing-2026-capex-of-roughly-usd200-billion/).*

**2. Spending Worries Weigh On Tech Shares** — In the Friday, July 24 session specifically, the AI-capex hangover kept a lid on technology even as the rest of the market recovered: the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.64% to 24,975.82 and the Russell 2000 slipped 0.35%, while the Dow rose 0.46% and the S&P 500 closed essentially flat at 7,411.98. Chips were the epicenter — a gauge of semiconductor stocks fell as much as 4.4% intraday and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) closed down about 3%, with Micron (MU) off roughly 7%, AMD down about 3.3% and Broadcom (AVGO) down about 2.7%. Intel was the day's most striking reversal, falling nearly 8% despite Q2 revenue up 25% year over year to about $16.1 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $0.42 (more than double estimates), as investors fixated on an $11 billion GAAP net loss and a 2026 capex plan raised above $20 billion. The pattern echoed Thursday's Alphabet reaction — beat the numbers, raise the spending, lose the stock — after the Magnificent Seven lost close to $800 billion in value on July 23. Elevated bond yields (the 10-year near 4.68%) and the newly effective 10%–12.5% tariffs compounded the pressure on long-duration growth names. *Sources: [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-july-24-2026-dow-futures-rebound-as-oil-retreats-after-market-sell-off), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/23/stock-market-today-live-updates.html), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-july-24-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-081854465.html).*

**3. U.S. Stocks Stabilize as Oil Retreats on Peace Talk Hopes** — Crude broke a roughly week-long winning streak on Friday, July 24 after Reuters, citing three Pakistani sources, reported that Pakistan was pushing to restart stalled U.S.-Iran peace negotiations with Chinese backing — Beijing reportedly frustrated that Iran's Gulf attacks and the Strait of Hormuz closure were damaging its own supply lines. Brent fell about 4% to trade below $96 a barrel, its biggest one-day drop since late June, retreating from Thursday's $100.69 settle, while U.S. WTI lost roughly 3% to settle at $89.31; both benchmarks still finished the week up sharply, with Brent holding a gain of more than 7%. The relief let equities steady: the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 235.60 points, or 0.46%, to 51,947.25 and the S&P 500 ticked up 0.05% to 7,411.98, having traded higher earlier in the session before chip weakness dragged the Nasdaq down 0.64% to 24,975.82. Treasuries also caught a bid, with the 10-year yield easing about 2.5 basis points to roughly 4.679% after touching an 18-month high near 4.70% the prior day. The stabilization proved fragile — by Monday, July 27 Brent had slid further below $90 as the pause in U.S.-Iran hostilities appeared to hold. *Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/24/oil-price-trump-hormuz-red-sea-iran-war.html), [Yahoo Finance/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/oil-prices-slide-but-head-for-weekly-rise-on-middle-east-escalation-084515480.html), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/23/stock-market-today-live-updates.html).*
