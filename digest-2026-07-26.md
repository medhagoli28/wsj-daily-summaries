## 💻 Tech

**1. Exclusive | Trump Caught Between Apple and Micron in Fight Over Chinese Chips** — Apple CEO Tim Cook and senior executives spent the week of July 20-24, 2026 lobbying President Trump, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent for permission to use DRAM and NAND from China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) and Yangtze Memory Technologies (YMTC) in devices sold outside the United States, arguing the added supply would ease a global memory shortage in which prices have roughly quadrupled over the past year per TechInsights. Micron, the only large US-based memory maker, is lobbying hard against it, with CEO Sanjay Mehrotra warning Lutnick that letting Chinese suppliers into US tech supply chains "could destroy the domestic industry" the way imports hollowed out steel; Micron counters that it can close the gap faster by accelerating its CHIPS Act-backed domestic fabs. Both Chinese firms sit on the Pentagon's 1260H Chinese-military-company list — restored in the newest version after an earlier removal — and YMTC is also on the Commerce Department Entity List, so Apple has sought assurances CXMT would not be added. Apple has accused Micron of price-gouging on gross margins above 80%, while Micron argues today's prices reflect genuine AI-driven scarcity after years of buyers squeezing suppliers; Micron shares fell about 7% on the day the fight became public, though the stock remains up roughly 191% year to date. The dispute forces Trump to choose between two stated priorities — lowering consumer prices and reshoring semiconductor manufacturing — against the backdrop of Apple's $600 billion American Manufacturing Program and its $30 billion Broadcom chip commitment. *Sources: [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/24/report-apple-and-micron-clash-before-trump-over-chinese-memory-chips/), [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/08/apple-begins-testing-controversial-chinese-memory/), [The Mac Observer](https://www.macobserver.com/news/apple-and-micron-fight-before-trump-over-banned-chinese-chips/).*

**2. EU Says TikTok Accounts for Minors Might Breach Bloc’s Digital Rules** — On July 24, 2026 the European Commission sent TikTok preliminary findings that the platform breaches the Digital Services Act by failing to ensure a high level of privacy, safety and security for minors' accounts, escalating a formal proceeding opened against TikTok back in February 2024. The Commission's core objection is that minors can set accounts to "public" by default-permissive settings, letting anyone — including people without a TikTok account — view their content, and that posts by 16- and 17-year-olds can be pushed to any user via the For You feed, creating risks of unwanted contact from predators and cyberbullying. Brussels says TikTok should instead default minors' accounts to visibility only for users the minor has accepted, and that older minors' content should never be reachable by a global audience outside the platform. EU tech sovereignty chief Henna Virkkunen framed it as a design obligation, saying a high level of protection "should not be an opt-in; it should be the default." TikTok can now inspect the case file and reply in writing, with the European Board for Digital Services consulted in parallel; if the findings are confirmed in a non-compliance decision, the DSA allows fines of up to 6% of the provider's total worldwide annual turnover, alongside a separate earlier preliminary finding on TikTok's addictive design and rabbit-hole recommender effects. *Sources: [European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1679), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/tiktok-eu-dsa-child-safety-charge), [Quartz](https://qz.com/tiktok-eu-dsa-children-privacy-charges-072426).*

## 📈 Markets & Finance

**1. Opinion | The Congressional Stock Trading Meme** — On July 22, 2026 the House passed Rep. Bryan Steil's (R-Wis.) Stop Insider Trading Act, H.R. 7008, by 232-198, with roughly 13 Democrats crossing over; the bill bars members, spouses and dependent children from buying individual stocks and requires 7 days' public notice before a sale, but pointedly does not require divestment, so existing positions stay untouched. Reform advocates call that the loophole: the rival bipartisan Restore Trust in Congress Act (H.R. 5106, Reps. Chip Roy and Seth Magaziner, with a Senate companion S. 3649 from Sens. Ashley Moody and Kirsten Gillibrand) would ban ownership outright and force divestment within 180 days, and has drawn well over 120 cosponsors including 35 Republicans, yet was never given a floor vote. Nancy Pelosi, whose family portfolio is the meme-stock symbol of the whole debate, voted no on the Steil bill. The underlying conduct keeps supplying material: Rep. Daniel Meuser filed five partial Nvidia sales between March 31 and July 2, 2026 while sitting on committees overseeing the sector, and Rep. Sara Jacobs disclosed a $500,001-$1,000,000 partial Qualcomm sale on May 6, 2026 while serving on Armed Services and Foreign Affairs; one analysis found 6,170 of 11,016 congressional purchases over 16 months — about 56% — were in sectors those members were about to vote on. The STOCK Act still allows 45 days to disclose and no member has ever been prosecuted under it, and the House-passed bill is widely expected to die in the Senate, where it would need 60 votes and arrives freighted with an attached voter-ID provision. *Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/22/house-congressional-stock-trading.html), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/congress-stock-trading-limits-house-vote/), [NOTUS](https://www.notus.org/money/congressional-stock-ban-bill-passes-house).*

**2. Why Wall Street Firms Are Paying $100,000 a Month for a Fast Track to Trump Posts** — Trump Media & Technology Group unveiled "Truth API" in mid-July 2026, a licensed low-latency data feed pitched to banks, hedge funds and high-frequency trading shops at up to $100,000 a month, or a discounted roughly $60,000 a month on a three-year commitment, for the fastest access to posts from the 10 most influential Truth Social accounts plus an archive going back to 2022. The commercial logic is millisecond arbitrage on presidential market-movers: Trump's April 9, 2025 Truth Social post announcing a 90-day tariff pause sent the major indexes sharply higher within minutes, and a few milliseconds' head start can be worth hundreds of thousands of dollars on a single large trade. TMTG said it had already signed customers ahead of an August 1, 2026 launch but declined to name them. The conflict-of-interest objection is structural rather than hypothetical — the Donald J. Trump Revocable Trust, administered by his children, holds roughly 114.75 million DJT shares, about 41% of shares outstanding — meaning the president's own trust benefits when traders pay for faster access to his statements. Sen. Elizabeth Warren called it "an egregious scheme to profit off the presidency," Sen. Ron Wyden raised similar objections, CREW president Donald Sherman called the arrangement "wildly unethical," and Republican senators including Bill Cassidy, Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski, Thom Tillis and John Curtis publicly criticized it. *Sources: [TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/07/17/truth-social-api-wall-street-trump-media/), [Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/trump-media-pitched-100-000-165416714.html), [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4654429/trump-media-100000-access-truth/).*

## 💰 Personal Finance

**1. Is Passive Investing Sabotaging Fund Managers?** — Indexing has now decisively won the asset war: Investment Company Institute data show index mutual funds and ETFs holding $21.82 trillion versus $18.75 trillion in active vehicles as of May 2026 (53.8% index share overall), and in domestic equity specifically index products control 63.9% of the $23.73 trillion category ($15.17 trillion indexed vs. $8.56 trillion active), up from passive first overtaking active in US domestic equity around August 2018 and across all long-term funds in late 2024. Performance data explain why money keeps leaving: S&P Dow Jones Indices' SPIVA U.S. Year-End 2025 Scorecard found 79% of active large-cap US equity funds trailed the S&P 500 in 2025 — worse than 65% in 2024 and the fourth-worst showing in the scorecard's 25-year history — while roughly 92% underperformed over 20 years and no category saw a majority of active managers beat their benchmark past 15 years. The economics are brutal: active mutual funds bled about $640 billion in 2025, their ninth outflow year of the past decade, against cumulative outflows near $4 trillion, even as active ETFs pulled in roughly $1.2 trillion cumulatively; Morningstar counted about 146-150 active ETF closures in 2025 plus 357 traditional mutual fund shutterings, with only six of the closed active ETFs holding more than $50 million at the start of the year. Index concentration compounds the problem — the S&P 500's top ten names have swelled from roughly 18% of the index in 2010 to about a third of every dollar in a standard S&P 500 fund in 2026, so cap-weighted benchmarks became a bet stock-pickers structurally could not match. Academically the question of whether indexing breaks price discovery remains unsettled: critics such as Michael Burry (arguing since 2019) and Acadian's Owen Lamont warn about distorted valuations and compressed index-inclusion windows, while research documenting a decline in index add/delete price effects and evidence that trading-driven price discovery is still dominated by active managers cuts the other way. *Sources: [Investment Company Institute](https://www.ici.org/research/stats/combined_active_index_0326), [S&P Dow Jones Indices (SPIVA)](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-us/), [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/blog/active-etf-launches-and-liquidations-2025).*

**2. Four Takeaways From Our Report on the IRS Chief Who Spied on Colleagues at JPMorgan** — The subject is Frank Bisignano, who was confirmed by the Senate as Social Security Administration commissioner in May 2025 (nominated by Trump in December 2024, Finance Committee hearing March 2025) after resigning as Fiserv CEO, a post he had held since 2020, and who on October 6, 2025 was additionally named to the newly created, non-Senate-confirmed position of Chief Executive Officer of the IRS while retaining the SSA job. The specific spying allegations could not be independently corroborated outside the originating newspaper — Bloomberg, CNBC and Benzinga all relay that reporting rather than confirming it through their own sourcing, so the underlying claims remain single-sourced. What is verifiable is the reaction: on July 21, 2026 Bisignano told CNBC on camera "None of it's true," called the story "bad journalism," and said he "wouldn't even know how" to access confidential files, while his lawyer stated he "never directed anyone to look through any employee's or executive's communications or engaged in any form of surveillance." As relayed, the report alleged that while co-chief operating officer at JPMorgan Chase more than a decade ago, Bisignano used his authority over the bank's security department to monitor rival executives' email — including Charlie Scharf, then head of consumer banking and now Wells Fargo CEO — and viewed a confidential draft FERC complaint over a questioned derivatives trade. Separately and independently documented: on July 23, 2026, Senate Democrats led by Elizabeth Warren alleged Bisignano may have misled Congress about IRS staffing, part of a broader leadership vacuum in which the commissioner's office went vacant on March 6, 2026 when Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's acting term expired, leaving Bessent saying he is "performing the duties" while denying he is acting commissioner. *Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/21/irs-chief-frank-bisignano-laughs-off-jpmorgan-spying-allegations.html), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/23/frank-bisignano-irs-misled-congress-warren-democrats.html), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/irs-chief-denies-report-he-spied-on-colleagues-while-at-jpmorgan).*
