Meta’s Case for Its AI Spending Keeps Getting Weaker** — Meta reported Q2 2026 results on July 29 that showed revenue up 28% to roughly $60.8 billion but earnings per share of $6.18, missing consensus by about 14% after a $2.4 billion legal charge and severance costs. The Everyday Guide to Supersizing Your Retirement Account** — For 2026 the IRS raised the 401(k)/403(b)/457/TSP elective deferral limit to $24,500 and the IRA limit to $7,500 (IRA catch-up $1,100), with the standard age-50 catch-up at $8,000 for a $32,500 personal cap. SECURE 2.0's "super catch-up" for savers who turn 60, 61, 62 or 63 during the year is $11,250 instead of $8,000, lifting their personal deferral ceiling to $35,750 — though the feature is optional and only works if the employer's plan adopts it. A major change takes effect January 1, 2026: under Treasury/IRS final regulations issued September 16, 2025, anyone 50-plus who earned more than $150,000 in FICA wages in 2025 (box 3 of the W-2, up from the statute's original $145,000) must make all catch-up contributions on a Roth after-tax basis. The overall Section 415(c) annual-additions cap — employee deferrals plus employer match plus after-tax money — rises to $72,000 for 2026 ($80,000 with the age-50 catch-up, $83,250 with the 60-63 catch-up). That is what makes the mega-backdoor Roth work: a plan permitting after-tax contributions plus in-service withdrawals or in-plan Roth conversions can absorb up to $47,500 of after-tax dollars ($72,000 minus $24,500, reduced dollar-for-dollar by any employer match) and convert it to Roth. *Sources: [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500), [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/401k-catch-up-contributions-high-earners), [Quarles](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/secure-2-0-act-retirement-plan-update-roth-catch-up-contributions-in-2026).*

**6. Canada to Cease Compelling U.S. Streamers to Help Finance Domestic Broadcasting** — Under the Online Streaming Act (Bill C-11, passed 2023), the CRTC ruled on June 4, 2024 (Broadcasting Regulatory Policy CRTC 2024-121) that foreign-owned streamers with at least $25 million in annual Canadian revenues must pay a 5% "base contribution" of those revenues, an estimated $200 million a year earmarked for local news, French-language, Indigenous and OLMC content starting in the 2024-25 broadcast year. The base payments were stayed in December 2024, and in May 2026 the CRTC went further with Broadcasting Regulatory Policy CRTC 2026-96, tripling the requirement to 15% of Canadian revenues — a package the regulator valued at over $2 billion, with services above $100 million in revenue required to steer 30% of spending to Canadian broadcaster and independent-producer partnerships. CRTC broadcasting vice-president Scott Shortliffe said of the rules, "We believe that they will be respected by these companies." That framework is now dead: a Department of Justice letter dated July 17, 2026 from Senior General Counsel Michael Morris, filed with the Federal Court of Appeal, states that "the Government's intention is to eliminate the base contribution requirement on streaming services and to provide government funding to replace those contributions," following the government's June 3, 2026 announcement and a June 29, 2026 direction from Justice David Stratas. Ottawa plans to publish the new policy direction in the Canada Gazette Part I for consultation and substitute public money — Michael Geist pegs the replacement envelope at roughly $600 million a year for the audio and audiovisual sector — after the U.S. trade representative repeatedly called the contribution rules an "irritant" in bilateral talks. *Sources: [Michael Geist](https://www.michaelgeist.ca/2026/07/starting-over-court-filing-confirms-the-crtcs-streamer-contribution-decisions-are-dead-with-a-full-online-streaming-act-reset-to-come/), [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11859450/online-streaming-act-canadian-content-crtc-rules/), [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/cancon-online-streaming-act-crtc-9.7289223).*

**7. NYSE Owner to Buy MarketAxess in $6 Billion Deal** — Intercontinental Exchange, the parent of the New York Stock Exchange, agreed on July 30, 2026 to acquire electronic bond-trading platform MarketAxess Holdings for $167 a share in cash, an equity value of about $6 billion and an enterprise value of roughly $5.7 billion, representing a 33% premium to MarketAxess's prior close. MarketAxess shares surged about 28-30%, while ICE shares were roughly flat to slightly higher. ICE Chair and CEO Jeff Sprecher said the combination will "build the fixed-income ecosystem that investors have always deserved — one that is transparent, efficient, fully connected, and accessible to all," pitching a single platform spanning pre-trade pricing and analytics through electronic execution and post-trade compliance; MarketAxess CEO Chris Concannon cast the fit as complementary, pairing MarketAxess's credit-trading network with ICE's retail protocols and data connectivity. ICE targets $100 million in annual run-rate expense synergies within three years of closing and plans to fund the purchase entirely with newly issued debt — bonds, a term loan and commercial paper — returning to a 3.0x gross leverage ratio within 18 to 24 months after close. Both boards have approved; closing is targeted for the first half of 2027, pending MarketAxess shareholder and regulatory approvals, and the deal squares ICE up directly against Tradeweb and Bloomberg in electronic credit trading. *Sources: [Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ice-acquire-marketaxess-6-billion-133421827.html), [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/ice-to-acquire-marketaxess-for-6bn-to-build-new-fixed-income-platform).*
