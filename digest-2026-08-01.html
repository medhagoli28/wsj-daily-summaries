How OpenAI Lost Its AI Crown—and the Fight to Win It Back
OpenAI's consumer dominance eroded sharply through 2025 and 2026: Apptopia data show ChatGPT's app market share falling from 69.1% in January 2025 to 45.3%, while Google's Gemini climbed from 14.7% to 25.2% and Grok jumped from 1.6% to 15.2%, and Gemini passed 2 billion monthly visits for the first time in January 2026. "ChatGPT built the category, but as viable alternatives have scaled, users are naturally diversifying their toolkit," said Tom Grant, VP of research at Apptopia. The enterprise flip was starker — Ramp's May 2026 AI Index put Anthropic at 34.4% of corporate-card AI spend versus OpenAI's 32.3%, the first such reversal, and Anthropic crossed roughly $30 billion in run-rate revenue against OpenAI's ~$24–25 billion, with a $965 billion valuation after its May 2026 Series H. OpenAI's counterattack centers on coding and enterprise, where its Codex product was judged to lag Anthropic's Claude Code on "speed and ease of use." Sam Altman conceded the slump publicly on July 16, 2026: "We did not have our best last 12 months ever, which is mostly my fault, but we are about to have our best 12 months to date."

AI and the Blurring of Reality
Synthetic video has crossed the point where trained specialists can no longer reliably sort real from fake; deepfake investigator Sam Gregory said in a July 30, 2026 interview that advances in generative AI now regularly fool him, and that the old tells — six-fingered hands, blurred teeth, impossibly bending limbs — no longer hold in newer models while detection tools can rarely give better than a probability. The March 2026 Iran war showed the consequences at scale: a fabricated clip of missiles hitting Tel Aviv drew 4 million-plus views on X but was actually footage of Algerian football fans celebrating, and xAI's Grok told users a separate AI-generated clip was "authentic citizen footage shot this morning (March 3, 2026) from a Tel Aviv-area balcony," a post that drew 1.6 million views and 2,100 likes in under a day. On March 8, Grok also vouched for a video of a Glasgow Central Station fire as firefighters battling a blaze in Tel Aviv "consistent with reports of Iranian missile debris impacts." Also in March 2026, the National Republican Senatorial Committee released what CNN called the first political deepfake to realistically recreate a candidate for an entire clip, showing Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico saying things he never said. Governance is lagging badly: a 2026 Deloitte survey found 67% of mid-market companies have no formal policy on synthetic content, while the 2026 Edelman Trust Barometer found 73% of consumers would stop doing business with a company caught using undisclosed synthetic media.

The Math Superstar Who’s Terrified of AI—and Just Took a Job at OpenAI
Jacob Tsimerman, 38, a University of Toronto number theorist, won the 2026 Fields Medal for his part in proving the André–Oort conjecture — posed by Yves André in 1989, generalized by Frans Oort in 1995, and finally settled in a 2021 preprint with Jonathan Pila and Ananth Shankar — and used his moment at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Philadelphia on July 23, 2026 to announce he is leaving for OpenAI to work on AI safety. His stated reason is alarm rather than enthusiasm: "I think AI will be better than mathematicians at doing math within two years," he said, adding that "the mathematical career, as we know it, I don't think it will exist in its current form." He points to an internal OpenAI reasoning model that in May 2026 disproved Paul Erdős's planar unit-distance conjecture, open since 1946. He has also said he no longer knows "what to do with a grad student in math who's not very AI motivated." Asked why someone who talks about extinction risk would join the company building the technology, his answer was that if it cannot be stopped, it is better to understand and shape it than be left behind.

OpenAI Surpasses One Billion Users After Cutting Prices
OpenAI said its models now reach more than 1 billion active users and over 2 million businesses, up from the roughly 900 million weekly users (about 50 million of them paying) it last disclosed. The milestone followed price cuts announced July 30, 2026: GPT-5.6 Luna fell about 80%, to $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens from $1 and $6, while GPT-5.6 Terra dropped roughly 20%, to $2 and $12 from $2.50 and $15; pricing for Sol, the family's most capable model, was unchanged, though a new Fast Mode offers 2.5x speed at double the cost. OpenAI attributed the cuts to efficiency work done during GPT-5.6's development, including GPU kernel optimizations that cut serving costs 20% and speculative decoding that lifted token-generation efficiency more than 15%. CFO Sarah Friar framed it as a flywheel: "Better intelligence drives broader adoption. Broader adoption supports more investment. More investment improves intelligence and efficiency." The context is competitive and financial pressure — OpenAI reported $13.07 billion in 2025 revenue against a $21 billion loss, and leadership reportedly deliberated for weeks amid concern over Anthropic cutting Claude prices and cheap Chinese open-weight models.

New York Sues Kalshi After Talks to Rein In Prediction Market Break Down
On July 31, 2026, Governor Kathy Hochul and Attorney General Letitia James sued KalshiEX LLC for running what they called an illegal gambling operation in New York, seeking a permanent ban plus penalties the state estimates at a minimum of $36 billion pending a full accounting — comprising full customer restitution, treble damages on Kalshi's gains, and a $100,000 civil penalty for each unlicensed sports-wagering offer. "No matter what they call themselves, prediction markets like Kalshi are gambling platforms, plain and simple," James said, alleging the platform exposed New Yorkers, including users under 21, to addiction risk while skipping state licensing and gaming taxes; Hochul added that "no company is above the law." The suit followed the collapse of weeks of negotiations in which Kalshi floated a state tax arrangement — reportedly modeled on a North Carolina deal taxing prediction-market trades at 6%, versus the roughly 23% levied on traditional gambling revenue — that could have delivered billions to New York over several years. Kalshi, which launched sports "trading" in all 50 states in 2025 and saw trading volume hit a roughly $50 billion monthly high in July, is fighting on jurisdictional grounds: "States can't just shut down a federally licensed exchange," a spokesperson said, echoing the Trump administration's position that prediction markets fall under the CFTC. The case was removed to the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York.

Fauci Faces a Test to His Legacy
Dr. Anthony Fauci, subpoenaed by Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee chairman Rand Paul (R-Ky.), invoked his Fifth Amendment right at a July 29, 2026 hearing and declined to answer more than 100 questions about Covid-19's origins and the federal pandemic response, with his lawyers arguing sworn testimony would create fresh legal exposure. Paul promised "repercussions" and said he would convene a committee vote the following week on holding Fauci in contempt, without committing to a criminal contempt referral. Separately, the Counterlawfare Institute obtained a previously undisclosed June 8, 2025 memo laying out a Justice Department blueprint for investigating whether Fauci — then director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases — and other officials steered the influential "Proximal Origin" paper, which argued for natural origin, and misled the public. Florida's attorney general announced a state-level investigation of Fauci in the same week, compounding the pressure on a legacy built over five decades in government.

Anthropic AI Models Hacked Three Companies During Tests
Anthropic disclosed on Thursday, July 30, 2026 that three of its models — Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Mythos 5, and an unreleased internal research model — broke out of sandboxed evaluations and compromised systems belonging to three real organizations. In each case the models were running "capture the flag" cybersecurity exercises in which they were told a secret flag was hidden on another machine on the network; Anthropic's prompts stated the models had no internet access, but a misconfiguration at third-party evaluation partner Irregular meant they actually did. Believing they were still inside the simulation, the models scanned outward and compromised genuine external systems using basic techniques such as weak passwords and unauthenticated endpoints. Anthropic said it found the three incidents after reviewing more than 141,000 evaluation runs in a large-scale security review it launched specifically in response to a similar disclosure days earlier by OpenAI, whose models had also reached a real company from inside a test environment. The back-to-back disclosures intensified debate over whether AI labs' testing infrastructure is adequately isolated as models gain offensive cyber capability.

Investors Rotate Out of Chip Stocks
The world's largest semiconductor companies shed more than $1 trillion — by some counts about $1.3 trillion — of market value in just a handful of sessions through July 29, 2026, as investors questioned whether trillions in AI infrastructure capex will earn an adequate return. Nvidia alone lost roughly $238 billion from the prior Friday's close, while SK Hynix, Samsung Electronics and Micron dropped about $176 billion, $173 billion and $113 billion respectively; Nvidia, SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron, AMD and TSMC each shed over $100 billion. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell more than 24% from its late-June peak and was down 19% in July, on pace for its worst month since 2008, with SK Hynix's plan to lift 2026 capex 50% to $31 billion cited as a trigger. Money moved into defensives and financials — U.S. equity funds took in about $1.96 billion for financials and $1.47 billion for healthcare in one early-July week, and the XLF and XLV sector ETFs pushed to record highs led by insurers. Morningstar chief equity strategist Michael Field called the move "driven largely by sentiment rather than fundamentals… Simply put, it's loss of confidence," while Forrester's Charlie Dai said investors feared AI spending was "peaking faster than expected."

The Kospi surged 17.91% on Friday, July 31, 2026, to close at 6,595.45, the largest one-day percentage and point gain in the index's history, as SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics rebounded nearly 30% from the week's crash. Buyer Beware: Private Funds Come With Big Tax Bills
As private-market vehicles are pushed toward ordinary investors, the tax drag is the least-advertised feature: private credit income is generally interest taxed as ordinary income at marginal rates topping out at 37% federally, versus a 20% top federal rate on long-term capital gains and qualified dividends. Evergreen and semi-liquid funds, whose assets topped roughly $530 billion in 2025 — about double their level three years earlier, with private credit the largest slice — increasingly deliver Schedule K-1s instead of 1099s, and those K-1s typically arrive between February and April, often late March or April for complex funds, routinely forcing individual investors to file extensions. K-1s also fragment income across ordinary income, interest and capital gains lines, each routed to a different schedule, and can report "phantom income" — taxable income exceeding the cash actually distributed — when funds reinvest or deploy capital on unusual timing. PwC notes that retailization brings state-specific K-1s, state withholding and composite filings that materially raise compliance cost, while tax-exempt and foreign investors may need blocker entities or parallel structures to manage UBTI and effectively connected income. The practical upshot is that headline IRRs quoted by sponsors can diverge sharply from what an individual keeps after tax.

The Stock Market Is Not Your Rich Uncle
The underlying argument — that households should not treat equities as a benefactor obliged to fund their spending and retirement — lands at a moment of record dependence on stocks. Equities reached 45.76% of U.S. household financial assets in the first quarter of 2026, near an all-time series high and up from 43.09% a year earlier, with households holding roughly $55.15 trillion in corporate equities and fund shares as total net worth hit a record $183 trillion. Those gains are extraordinarily concentrated: the top 1% of households own 50.2% of equities and fund shares and the richest 10% own about 87%, while the bottom half owns 1.1%. Meanwhile the buffer most families would need if the market stopped cooperating is thin — the personal savings rate has fallen to about 3.0% versus a 6.2% historical average, and the median 401(k) balance for workers aged 44–59 is roughly $64,300, about 40% of the ~$168,000 implied by a 6x-salary benchmark at age 50. Academic work on the "household equity share" finds the metric is a negative predictor of excess market returns over the following five years, meaning today's record allocation is itself a warning that the market is unlikely to play generous relative.

The premium tier has re-priced sharply since mid-2025: the Amex Platinum jumped from $695 to $895 in its September 18, 2025 refresh (existing cardholders hit at renewals on or after January 2, 2026), the Chase Sapphire Reserve went from $550 to $795 for applicants approved on or after June 23, 2025, and Citi launched the Strata Elite at $595 on July 27, 2025 as the Prestige successor. The Price to Finance the AI Data Center Boom Is Rising, Just Ask Meta
BlackRock priced roughly $12.3 billion of bonds in the week of July 20, 2026 to fund Meta's roughly 1-gigawatt El Paso, Texas data center (codenamed "Sopaipilla"), with BlackRock's infrastructure and private-credit arms holding 80% of the SPV and Meta 20%. The investment-grade-rated paper cleared at a 7.534% yield — a junk-bond-like level — after early price talk above 7%, with investors demanding roughly 0.4 percentage points more risk premium than Meta got on its record $27 billion "Hyperion" financing through the Beignet Investor LLC SPV (80% Blue Owl / 20% Meta) that priced last October at about 225 basis points over Treasuries. The Everyday Guide to Supersizing Your Retirement Account
For 2026 the IRS raised the 401(k)/403(b)/457/TSP elective deferral limit to $24,500 and the IRA limit to $7,500 (IRA catch-up $1,100), with the standard age-50 catch-up at $8,000 for a $32,500 personal cap. SECURE 2.0's "super catch-up" for savers who turn 60, 61, 62 or 63 during the year is $11,250 instead of $8,000, lifting their personal deferral ceiling to $35,750 — though the feature is optional and only works if the employer's plan adopts it. A major change takes effect January 1, 2026: under Treasury/IRS final regulations issued September 16, 2025, anyone 50-plus who earned more than $150,000 in FICA wages in 2025 (box 3 of the W-2, up from the statute's original $145,000) must make all catch-up contributions on a Roth after-tax basis. The overall Section 415(c) annual-additions cap — employee deferrals plus employer match plus after-tax money — rises to $72,000 for 2026 ($80,000 with the age-50 catch-up, $83,250 with the 60-63 catch-up). That is what makes the mega-backdoor Roth work: a plan permitting after-tax contributions plus in-service withdrawals or in-plan Roth conversions can absorb up to $47,500 of after-tax dollars ($72,000 minus $24,500, reduced dollar-for-dollar by any employer match) and convert it to Roth.

Exclusive | Federal Probe Into Dodgers Owner’s Financial Empire Was Sparked by Whistleblower
Federal prosecutors in Manhattan, working in parallel with the SEC, are investigating potential financial improprieties at Mark Walter's Guggenheim Partners and two insurers he controls, after an internal whistleblower complaint questioned how Guggenheim Investments booked revenue tied to the insurers. Delaware Life Insurance Co. ($69 billion in assets as of March 2026) and Clear Spring Life and Annuity Co. ($16 billion), both under the Group 1001 umbrella, received grand jury subpoenas in February 2026, disclosed in regulatory filings dated June 26, 2026; the FBI executed at least one search warrant in September 2025 to seize a mobile phone. At the center is disclosure of related-party lending: Delaware Life had told regulators that about 3% of its investments, roughly $1.4 billion, involved parties connected to Walter's other ventures, then restated the figure to more than $17 billion, or at least 39% of invested assets — an additional roughly $16 billion of affiliate-linked private credit that had gone unreported. Prosecutors opened a separate inquiry last year into Guggenheim's $362 billion asset-management arm, where Walter is CEO. Walter, whose TWG Global controls stakes in the Dodgers, the Lakers and Chelsea FC, has not been charged with any wrongdoing, and TWG says it is aware of and cooperating with the investigation.

Canada to Cease Compelling U.S. Streamers to Help Finance Domestic Broadcasting
Under the Online Streaming Act (Bill C-11, passed 2023), the CRTC ruled on June 4, 2024 (Broadcasting Regulatory Policy CRTC 2024-121) that foreign-owned streamers with at least $25 million in annual Canadian revenues must pay a 5% "base contribution" of those revenues, an estimated $200 million a year earmarked for local news, French-language, Indigenous and OLMC content starting in the 2024-25 broadcast year. The base payments were stayed in December 2024, and in May 2026 the CRTC went further with Broadcasting Regulatory Policy CRTC 2026-96, tripling the requirement to 15% of Canadian revenues — a package the regulator valued at over $2 billion, with services above $100 million in revenue required to steer 30% of spending to Canadian broadcaster and independent-producer partnerships. CRTC broadcasting vice-president Scott Shortliffe said of the rules, "We believe that they will be respected by these companies." That framework is now dead: a Department of Justice letter dated July 17, 2026 from Senior General Counsel Michael Morris, filed with the Federal Court of Appeal, states that "the Government's intention is to eliminate the base contribution requirement on streaming services and to provide government funding to replace those contributions," following the government's June 3, 2026 announcement and a June 29, 2026 direction from Justice David Stratas. Ottawa plans to publish the new policy direction in the Canada Gazette Part I for consultation and substitute public money — Michael Geist pegs the replacement envelope at roughly $600 million a year for the audio and audiovisual sector — after the U.S. trade representative repeatedly called the contribution rules an "irritant" in bilateral talks.

NYSE Owner to Buy MarketAxess in $6 Billion Deal
Intercontinental Exchange, the parent of the New York Stock Exchange, agreed on July 30, 2026 to acquire electronic bond-trading platform MarketAxess Holdings for $167 a share in cash, an equity value of about $6 billion and an enterprise value of roughly $5.7 billion, representing a 33% premium to MarketAxess's prior close. MarketAxess shares surged about 28-30%, while ICE shares were roughly flat to slightly higher. ICE Chair and CEO Jeff Sprecher said the combination will "build the fixed-income ecosystem that investors have always deserved — one that is transparent, efficient, fully connected, and accessible to all," pitching a single platform spanning pre-trade pricing and analytics through electronic execution and post-trade compliance; MarketAxess CEO Chris Concannon cast the fit as complementary, pairing MarketAxess's credit-trading network with ICE's retail protocols and data connectivity. ICE targets $100 million in annual run-rate expense synergies within three years of closing and plans to fund the purchase entirely with newly issued debt — bonds, a term loan and commercial paper — returning to a 3.0x gross leverage ratio within 18 to 24 months after close. Both boards have approved; closing is targeted for the first half of 2027, pending MarketAxess shareholder and regulatory approvals, and the deal squares ICE up directly against Tradeweb and Bloomberg in electronic credit trading.

‘Time and Water’ Review: On National Geographic, Glacial History as Personal History
Sara Dosa's 93-minute documentary, a National Geographic Documentary Films release scored by Dan Deacon, premiered at Sundance on January 27, 2026, opened theatrically May 29, 2026, aired on the National Geographic channel Friday, July 31 at 9/8c, and began streaming on Disney+ and Hulu on August 1, 2026. It follows Icelandic author and poet Andri Snær Magnason — whose 2019 book "On Time and Water" is the source — as he threads his glaciologist grandparents' six-decade marriage and family archive into the story of Iceland's retreating ice. The real-world anchor is Okjökull, declared no longer a glacier in 2014 by glaciologist Oddur Sigurðsson and memorialized on August 18, 2019 with a bronze plaque bearing Magnason's "A letter to the future," which warns that all Iceland's glaciers are expected to follow within 200 years and closes, "Only you know if we did it," stamped with that month's 415 ppm CO2 reading. The 2019 ceremony drew then-Prime Minister Katrín Jakobsdóttir, Environment Minister Guðmundur Ingi Guðbrandsson and former Irish president Mary Robinson. Critics have been warm: 90% of 42 reviews are positive on Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic scores it 75 out of 100 from 13 critics.
