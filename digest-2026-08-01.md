**5. Buyer Beware: Private Funds Come With Big Tax Bills** — As private-market vehicles are pushed toward ordinary investors, the tax drag is the least-advertised feature: private credit income is generally interest taxed as ordinary income at marginal rates topping out at 37% federally, versus a 20% top federal rate on long-term capital gains and qualified dividends. Evergreen and semi-liquid funds, whose assets topped roughly $530 billion in 2025 — about double their level three years earlier, with private credit the largest slice — increasingly deliver Schedule K-1s instead of 1099s, and those K-1s typically arrive between February and April, often late March or April for complex funds, routinely forcing individual investors to file extensions. K-1s also fragment income across ordinary income, interest and capital gains lines, each routed to a different schedule, and can report "phantom income" — taxable income exceeding the cash actually distributed — when funds reinvest or deploy capital on unusual timing. PwC notes that retailization brings state-specific K-1s, state withholding and composite filings that materially raise compliance cost, while tax-exempt and foreign investors may need blocker entities or parallel structures to manage UBTI and effectively connected income. The practical upshot is that headline IRRs quoted by sponsors can diverge sharply from what an individual keeps after tax. *Sources: [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/tax/library/retailization-private-market-access.html), [BPM](https://www.bpm.com/insights/private-credit-fund-tax/), [Carta](https://carta.com/blog/k-1-delivery-for-lps/).*
