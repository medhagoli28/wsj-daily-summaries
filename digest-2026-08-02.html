The Race to Build an American Alternative to Cheap AI From China
Chinese open-weight models have taken over the low end of the market: a Hugging Face study dated March 16, 2026 found Chinese open-source models accounted for 41% of downloads, and Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5 currently tops open-model leaderboards at a reported $4.3 billion valuation. Price is the driver — Axios reported on August 1, 2026 that DeepSeek's new coding model charges roughly 28 cents for the same output volume that costs about $25 on Anthropic's Opus 4.8, a ~99% discount, while an hour-long coding session running about $10 on Claude costs under 50 cents on DeepSeek. U.S. buyers have followed: DoorDash built its DoorDash CLI on Moonshot, with cofounder and CTO Andy Fang citing "better quality" at a "cheaper cost"; Cursor used Kimi to help build its Composer 2 agent; and Lindy dropped Anthropic entirely for DeepSeek's V4 models, joining Airbnb and Siemens in testing Chinese providers. The American counterweight is a small cohort of open-weight labs — Arcee AI, Reflection AI and Poolside — with Arcee training Trinity Large plus three smaller open models for about $20 million in under six months on Nvidia Blackwell chips, while Reflection AI, founded by two ex-Google DeepMind researchers, raised $2 billion last year for the same mission. The cost gap they are fighting is severe: Innovation Endeavors estimated Llama 4 cost over $300 million to train and GPT-4 about $100 million, against DeepSeek's claimed $294,000 for R1, and critics such as Horizon3.ai CEO Snehal Antani warn that adopting Chinese models risks "severe data sovereignty violations" — the security argument U.S. labs are selling against price.

A Tech Founder Wanted to Start a New Country. An Actual Country Got in the Way.
The underlying topic is the "network state" movement colliding with existing sovereignty. Praxis, founded in 2019 by Dryden Brown and Charlie Callinan and describing itself as an "internet-native nation," announced up to $525 million in milestone-based financing in October 2024 and claims over 150,000 online citizens plus a 50,000-person waitlist; Brown traveled to Greenland in late 2024 and pitched politicians including Pele Broberg on a privatized charter state jointly owned via tokens by Praxis citizens, the Greenlandic government and individual Greenlanders, before pivoting in April 2025 to "Atlas," a proposed city on Vandenberg Space Force Base in Santa Barbara County, California — a site that as of mid-2025 still lacked the required federal Enhanced Use Lease. The more advanced cautionary tale is Próspera, the semi-autonomous ZEDE on Roatán incorporated in August 2017 by Erick Brimen and backed by Peter Thiel and Marc Andreessen: Honduras's Congress voted unanimously in 2022 to repeal the ZEDE law and the Supreme Court declared the zones unconstitutional in a 3-2 ruling with retroactive effect. Próspera responded with an ICSID arbitration under CAFTA-DR seeking roughly $10.8–11 billion, and on February 26, 2025 the tribunal rejected Honduras's preliminary objections, letting the claim proceed toward the merits with no final ruling expected soon. As of early 2026 Próspera still operates and construction on Roatán continues, and the political climate shifted with the contested election of right-wing president Nasry Asfura, whom Trump publicly backed. The through-line across both projects: raising hundreds of millions and recruiting citizens online is far easier than obtaining the land, legal charter, and host-government consent that statehood actually requires.

The Math Superstar Who’s Terrified of AI—and Just Took a Job at OpenAI
Jacob Tsimerman, 38, a University of Toronto number theorist, won the 2026 Fields Medal for his part in proving the André–Oort conjecture — posed by Yves André in 1989, generalized by Frans Oort in 1995, and finally settled in a 2021 preprint with Jonathan Pila and Ananth Shankar — and used his moment at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Philadelphia on July 23, 2026 to announce he is leaving for OpenAI to work on AI safety. His stated reason is alarm rather than enthusiasm: "I think AI will be better than mathematicians at doing math within two years," he said, adding that "the mathematical career, as we know it, I don't think it will exist in its current form." He points to an internal OpenAI reasoning model that in May 2026 disproved Paul Erdős's planar unit-distance conjecture, open since 1946. He has also said he no longer knows "what to do with a grad student in math who's not very AI motivated." Asked why someone who talks about extinction risk would join the company building the technology, his answer was that if it cannot be stopped, it is better to understand and shape it than be left behind.

How OpenAI Lost Its AI Crown—and the Fight to Win It Back
OpenAI's consumer dominance eroded sharply through 2025 and 2026: Apptopia data show ChatGPT's app market share falling from 69.1% in January 2025 to 45.3%, while Google's Gemini climbed from 14.7% to 25.2% and Grok jumped from 1.6% to 15.2%, and Gemini passed 2 billion monthly visits for the first time in January 2026. "ChatGPT built the category, but as viable alternatives have scaled, users are naturally diversifying their toolkit," said Tom Grant, VP of research at Apptopia. The enterprise flip was starker — Ramp's May 2026 AI Index put Anthropic at 34.4% of corporate-card AI spend versus OpenAI's 32.3%, the first such reversal, and Anthropic crossed roughly $30 billion in run-rate revenue against OpenAI's ~$24–25 billion, with a $965 billion valuation after its May 2026 Series H. OpenAI's counterattack centers on coding and enterprise, where its Codex product was judged to lag Anthropic's Claude Code on "speed and ease of use." Sam Altman conceded the slump publicly on July 16, 2026: "We did not have our best last 12 months ever, which is mostly my fault, but we are about to have our best 12 months to date."

Wall Street Thinks It Knows How Tech Giants Will Make AI Pay
After the July 29–31, 2026 earnings wave, roughly $2 trillion of market value shifted among the megacaps as investors converged on a single test for AI monetization: contracted, disclosed backlog. Microsoft added more than $600 billion in market value on Azure growth of 43%, crossing $100 billion in annual revenue for the first time and a $678 billion commercial backlog up 84% year over year; Amazon and Alphabet each gained more than $400 billion, with AWS reporting a $496 billion backlog and Google Cloud revenue up 82% to $24.8 billion against a $514 billion backlog. Meta, which disclosed no equivalent backlog, fell about 8% despite raising the floor of its capex range, and Morgan Stanley cut it to Equal-Weight citing insufficient visibility into AI revenue relative to the capex ramp. The spending itself keeps escalating — Alphabet raised its 2026 plan by another $15 billion to as much as $205 billion, Amazon guided to about $220 billion, and analysts now peg combined hyperscaler capex at roughly $600–900 billion in 2026 and near $950 billion to more than $1 trillion in 2027. The bear case is the gap: direct AI revenue still amounts to only a small fraction of infrastructure outlays, and capex is consuming close to 90% of hyperscaler operating cash flow, pushing companies toward debt markets to fund the buildout.

OpenAI Surpasses One Billion Users After Cutting Prices
OpenAI said its models now reach more than 1 billion active users and over 2 million businesses, up from the roughly 900 million weekly users (about 50 million of them paying) it last disclosed. The milestone followed price cuts announced July 30, 2026: GPT-5.6 Luna fell about 80%, to $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens from $1 and $6, while GPT-5.6 Terra dropped roughly 20%, to $2 and $12 from $2.50 and $15; pricing for Sol, the family's most capable model, was unchanged, though a new Fast Mode offers 2.5x speed at double the cost. OpenAI attributed the cuts to efficiency work done during GPT-5.6's development, including GPU kernel optimizations that cut serving costs 20% and speculative decoding that lifted token-generation efficiency more than 15%. CFO Sarah Friar framed it as a flywheel: "Better intelligence drives broader adoption. Broader adoption supports more investment. More investment improves intelligence and efficiency." The context is competitive and financial pressure — OpenAI reported $13.07 billion in 2025 revenue against a $21 billion loss, and leadership reportedly deliberated for weeks amid concern over Anthropic cutting Claude prices and cheap Chinese open-weight models.

New York Sues Kalshi After Talks to Rein In Prediction Market Break Down
On July 31, 2026, Governor Kathy Hochul and Attorney General Letitia James sued KalshiEX LLC for running what they called an illegal gambling operation in New York, seeking a permanent ban plus penalties the state estimates at a minimum of $36 billion pending a full accounting — comprising full customer restitution, treble damages on Kalshi's gains, and a $100,000 civil penalty for each unlicensed sports-wagering offer. "No matter what they call themselves, prediction markets like Kalshi are gambling platforms, plain and simple," James said, alleging the platform exposed New Yorkers, including users under 21, to addiction risk while skipping state licensing and gaming taxes; Hochul added that "no company is above the law." The suit followed the collapse of weeks of negotiations in which Kalshi floated a state tax arrangement — reportedly modeled on a North Carolina deal taxing prediction-market trades at 6%, versus the roughly 23% levied on traditional gambling revenue — that could have delivered billions to New York over several years. Kalshi, which launched sports "trading" in all 50 states in 2025 and saw trading volume hit a roughly $50 billion monthly high in July, is fighting on jurisdictional grounds: "States can't just shut down a federally licensed exchange," a spokesperson said, echoing the Trump administration's position that prediction markets fall under the CFTC. The case was removed to the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York.

Exclusive | Situational Awareness Down 67% in July in AI Stock Rout
Situational Awareness, the hedge fund run by 24-year-old former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, lost roughly 67% of its value in July 2026 after a historic momentum reversal hit both sides of its book. The fund peaked at about $45 billion in assets at the start of July — it had been up roughly 439% year to date through June — and was down to roughly $10 billion by July 30. Its core trade was long the AI infrastructure complex (SK Hynix, Micron, Nebius, CoreWeave, Broadcom) against short positions in software companies seen as disrupted by AI, such as Adobe; when chips sold off and software rallied, both legs went the wrong way at once. Reported leverage of roughly four times amplified the damage, triggering margin calls from prime brokers Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America and forcing liquidation of the entire public equity book. The broader AI complex shed roughly $3 trillion in market capitalization during the downturn, and the fund's roughly 12-person team was left with about $10 billion, including private positions such as its stake in Anthropic.

When It’s Time to Have ‘the Talk’ With Your Parents
The underlying problem is that most American families never discuss money across generations: a Fidelity survey of about 1,900 adults age 18 and up found 56% say their parents never discussed money with them, and fewer than two in five adult children have had a substantive conversation about their parents' finances in the past year. The financial stakes are large and rising — CareScout's 2025 Cost of Care data puts the national median at $80,080 a year for an in-home non-medical caregiver (44 hours a week at $35 an hour, up 3% year over year), $74,400 a year for assisted living ($6,200 a month, up 5%), and $129,575 a year for a private nursing-home room ($355 a day, up 1%) — while HHS estimates nearly 3 in 4 Americans over 65 will need some form of long-term care. Roughly 63 million Americans now provide family caregiving, up nearly 50% since 2015, with the average family caregiver spending about $7,200 a year out of pocket (closer to $8,700 for long-distance caregivers), and about half reporting depleted savings, new debt, or lost income. Advisers consistently recommend starting before a crisis rather than in an emergency room, opening with the parent's wishes ("what do you want your care to look like if your health changes?" and "where do you want to live?") rather than with a demand for account balances. The concrete deliverables are a durable financial power of attorney, a healthcare directive/proxy, and a basic estate plan — typically $1,000 to $3,000 in legal fees — plus a shared inventory of accounts, insurance policies and advisers.

Walter, whose TWG Global controls stakes in the Dodgers, the Lakers and Chelsea FC, has not been charged with any wrongdoing, and TWG says it is aware of and cooperating with the investigation.

Banks Want Technophobes and Late Adopters to Use Digital Wallets. It Isn’t Easy.
Digital wallets now clear roughly $18.7 trillion a year globally, up from $1.7 trillion in 2014 and projected past $33 trillion by 2030, and about 65% of U.S. in-person card transactions are contactless — but the growth is lopsided by age. Only 7.9% of baby boomers tap a digital wallet in-store weekly and just 25.7% use one at all, versus 43.2% weekly for Gen Z and 49.4% for millennials, with Gen X at 43.7% overall. PYMNTS' November 2025 survey of 2,108 U.S. adults found 36% of Gen Z used a wallet for their most recent retail purchase, up from 15% in March 2024, while the all-consumer figure was just 15%. Banks' countermeasures are mostly plumbing and nudges: instant digital card issuance so a card is spendable before the plastic arrives, one-touch "Tap to Add" provisioning from inside the mobile app, browser-based web push provisioning for customers who never open the app, and timed prompts such as "Add to your mobile wallet now" during the first 90 days after issuance. The pitch to holdouts leans on the reasons existing users cite — 74% name faster checkout and 69% name security — with 65% of Americans already funding their wallets with bank-issued cards, so issuers risk losing top-of-wallet position if they don't convert the laggards.

The Price to Finance the AI Data Center Boom Is Rising, Just Ask Meta
BlackRock priced roughly $12.3 billion of bonds in the week of July 20, 2026 to fund Meta's roughly 1-gigawatt El Paso, Texas data center (codenamed "Sopaipilla"), with BlackRock's infrastructure and private-credit arms holding 80% of the SPV and Meta 20%. The investment-grade-rated paper cleared at a 7.534% yield — a junk-bond-like level — after early price talk above 7%, with investors demanding roughly 0.4 percentage points more risk premium than Meta got on its record $27 billion "Hyperion" financing through the Beignet Investor LLC SPV (80% Blue Owl / 20% Meta) that priced last October at about 225 basis points over Treasuries. Those Hyperion bonds had already slipped to roughly 96 cents on the dollar in secondary trading, signaling investor fatigue with long-dated AI infrastructure debt that in some cases runs to 2049 against GPUs with a 3-5 year useful life. The El Paso deal nonetheless rallied in early trading Monday, July 27, as the fat yield ultimately pulled buyers in. The repricing matters because AI-related debt is piling up at roughly $100 billion a quarter, with estimates of up to $800 billion of private credit needed by 2028 and $3 trillion of data center investment over five years.

NYSE Owner to Buy MarketAxess in $6 Billion Deal
Intercontinental Exchange, the parent of the New York Stock Exchange, agreed on July 30, 2026 to acquire electronic bond-trading platform MarketAxess Holdings for $167 a share in cash, an equity value of about $6 billion and an enterprise value of roughly $5.7 billion, representing a 33% premium to MarketAxess's prior close. MarketAxess shares surged about 28-30%, while ICE shares were roughly flat to slightly higher. ICE Chair and CEO Jeff Sprecher said the combination will "build the fixed-income ecosystem that investors have always deserved — one that is transparent, efficient, fully connected, and accessible to all," pitching a single platform spanning pre-trade pricing and analytics through electronic execution and post-trade compliance; MarketAxess CEO Chris Concannon cast the fit as complementary, pairing MarketAxess's credit-trading network with ICE's retail protocols and data connectivity. ICE targets $100 million in annual run-rate expense synergies within three years of closing and plans to fund the purchase entirely with newly issued debt — bonds, a term loan and commercial paper — returning to a 3.0x gross leverage ratio within 18 to 24 months after close. Both boards have approved; closing is targeted for the first half of 2027, pending MarketAxess shareholder and regulatory approvals, and the deal squares ICE up directly against Tradeweb and Bloomberg in electronic credit trading.

The ‘Unsettled’ Vibe That’s Creeping Through the Markets
Wall Street closed the week of July 20-24, 2026 with its second straight weekly decline, the S&P 500 finishing Friday at 7,411.98 (+0.05%) while the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.64% to 24,975.82 and the Dow rose 235.60 points to 51,947.25. The unease is not about the headline level — the S&P 500 is still up more than 8% in 2026 and second-quarter earnings are tracking a 26.5% year-over-year gain per LSEG IBES — but about what sits underneath it: Alphabet dropped 7.1% on July 23, its worst day since May 7, 2025, and Tesla sank 15%, its biggest one-day fall since March 10, 2025, both punished for AI capital spending rather than missed profits. Breadth has diverged on 52 trading days so far this year, matching the record set in 2000, and the strain has spread into credit, where hyperscalers have issued $182 billion of bonds in 2026 to fund data centers and Oracle's CDS spread hit a seven-year high near 75 basis points. Tankers Are Being Blown Out of the Water. See How Mideast Strikes Are Piling Up.
Since hostilities began on February 28, 2026, at least 58 commercial vessels have been struck through July 31, killing 17 seafarers and injuring 10, with three ships sunk or captured (MSC Epaminondas, MSC Francesca and Haji Ali, which went down May 13). The most conspicuous recent strike came on July 14-15, when Iranian cruise missiles hit the Adnoc Logistics supertankers Mombasa and Al Bahyah in the southern Strait, killing one sailor and wounding eight; Houthi forces also targeted the Saudi tankers Encelia and Layla in late July. War-risk premiums have exploded from about 0.25% of hull value before the war — roughly $250,000 on a $100 million tanker — to 7.5%-10%, or $3 million to $10 million per voyage, pushing insurance on a single 270,000-tonne VLCC to about $21 million and Gulf-to-China crude freight to $77.96 a tonne against a five-year average of $18.91. Marcus Baker, global head of marine, cargo and logistics at Marsh, said, "War rates have been on a roller coaster mirroring the development of the price of oil," with Brent peaking near $126 a barrel. Traffic has collapsed from a pre-war 120-140 vessels a day (about 20 million barrels of oil daily) to as few as two tankers a day at the worst, and 10-16 in late July, while roughly 6,000 seafarers were reported stranded in the region as of July 8.
