**2. A Tech Founder Wanted to Start a New Country. An Actual Country Got in the Way.** — The underlying topic is the "network state" movement colliding with existing sovereignty. Praxis, founded in 2019 by Dryden Brown and Charlie Callinan and describing itself as an "internet-native nation," announced up to $525 million in milestone-based financing in October 2024 and claims over 150,000 online citizens plus a 50,000-person waitlist; Brown traveled to Greenland in late 2024 and pitched politicians including Pele Broberg on a privatized charter state jointly owned via tokens by Praxis citizens, the Greenlandic government and individual Greenlanders, before pivoting in April 2025 to "Atlas," a proposed city on Vandenberg Space Force Base in Santa Barbara County, California — a site that as of mid-2025 still lacked the required federal Enhanced Use Lease. The more advanced cautionary tale is Próspera, the semi-autonomous ZEDE on Roatán incorporated in August 2017 by Erick Brimen and backed by Peter Thiel and Marc Andreessen: Honduras's Congress voted unanimously in 2022 to repeal the ZEDE law and the Supreme Court declared the zones unconstitutional in a 3-2 ruling with retroactive effect. Próspera responded with an ICSID arbitration under CAFTA-DR seeking roughly $10.8–11 billion, and on February 26, 2025 the tribunal rejected Honduras's preliminary objections, letting the claim proceed toward the merits with no final ruling expected soon. As of early 2026 Próspera still operates and construction on Roatán continues, and the political climate shifted with the contested election of right-wing president Nasry Asfura, whom Trump publicly backed. The through-line across both projects: raising hundreds of millions and recruiting citizens online is far easier than obtaining the land, legal charter, and host-government consent that statehood actually requires. *Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxis_(proposed_city)), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/praxis-another-tech-billionaire-backed-urban-project-gets-525-million-in-funding-2000512690), [UNCTAD Investment Policy Hub](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/investment-dispute-settlement/cases/1292/pr-spera-and-others-v-honduras).*

**1. When It’s Time to Have ‘the Talk’ With Your Parents** — The underlying problem is that most American families never discuss money across generations: a Fidelity survey of about 1,900 adults age 18 and up found 56% say their parents never discussed money with them, and fewer than two in five adult children have had a substantive conversation about their parents' finances in the past year. The financial stakes are large and rising — CareScout's 2025 Cost of Care data puts the national median at $80,080 a year for an in-home non-medical caregiver (44 hours a week at $35 an hour, up 3% year over year), $74,400 a year for assisted living ($6,200 a month, up 5%), and $129,575 a year for a private nursing-home room ($355 a day, up 1%) — while HHS estimates nearly 3 in 4 Americans over 65 will need some form of long-term care. Roughly 63 million Americans now provide family caregiving, up nearly 50% since 2015, with the average family caregiver spending about $7,200 a year out of pocket (closer to $8,700 for long-distance caregivers), and about half reporting depleted savings, new debt, or lost income. Advisers consistently recommend starting before a crisis rather than in an emergency room, opening with the parent's wishes ("what do you want your care to look like if your health changes?" and "where do you want to live?") rather than with a demand for account balances. The concrete deliverables are a durable financial power of attorney, a healthcare directive/proxy, and a basic estate plan — typically $1,000 to $3,000 in legal fees — plus a shared inventory of accounts, insurance policies and advisers. *Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/11/27/56percent-of-americans-say-their-parents-never-discussed-money-with-them.html), [CareScout Cost of Care](https://www.carescout.com/cost-of-care), [Stacker via Alabama Gazette](https://www.alabamagazette.com/story/2026/07/13/seniors/how-do-i-address-the-financial-reality-of-caring-for-my-aging-parents-it-costs-the-average-family-7200-a-year/11873.html).*

NYSE Owner to Buy MarketAxess in $6 Billion Deal** — Intercontinental Exchange, the parent of the New York Stock Exchange, agreed on July 30, 2026 to acquire electronic bond-trading platform MarketAxess Holdings for $167 a share in cash, an equity value of about $6 billion and an enterprise value of roughly $5.7 billion, representing a 33% premium to MarketAxess's prior close. MarketAxess shares surged about 28-30%, while ICE shares were roughly flat to slightly higher. ICE Chair and CEO Jeff Sprecher said the combination will "build the fixed-income ecosystem that investors have always deserved — one that is transparent, efficient, fully connected, and accessible to all," pitching a single platform spanning pre-trade pricing and analytics through electronic execution and post-trade compliance; MarketAxess CEO Chris Concannon cast the fit as complementary, pairing MarketAxess's credit-trading network with ICE's retail protocols and data connectivity. Both boards have approved; closing is targeted for the first half of 2027, pending MarketAxess shareholder and regulatory approvals, and the deal squares ICE up directly against Tradeweb and Bloomberg in electronic credit trading. *Sources: [Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ice-acquire-marketaxess-6-billion-133421827.html), [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/ice-to-acquire-marketaxess-for-6bn-to-build-new-fixed-income-platform).*
