Can Fast Fashion Fit Into Secondhand Clothes?
The underlying business story is resale's shift from niche to structural competitor: ThredUp's 14th Annual Resale Report, released April 2, 2026 with analysis by GlobalData and a survey of 3,268 U.S. consumers conducted in January–February 2026, pegged the global secondhand apparel market at $393 billion, roughly 10% of total apparel spend, and projected the U.S. market alone to hit $78.8 billion by 2030. ThredUp cofounder and CEO James Reinhart said resale "is no longer just growing, it's taking direct market share," noting U.S. secondhand grew nearly 4x faster than the broader retail clothing market in 2025, while GlobalData's Neil Saunders called the sector "more competitive, structurally complex"; Gen Z and millennials are forecast to drive 71% of growth through 2030. Brands have followed the money — branded resale grew about 300% between 2021 and 2025, and branded secondhand storefronts expanded to 147 locations from just 9 in 2020, with Vinted growing net profit 330% between 2023 and 2024 and The North Face Renewed moving 96,000 items in a year against hundreds of millions of new products. The catch for fast fashion is durability: charity and textile-recovery groups report rising volumes of donated garments too poorly constructed to resell, and only 7% of retail executives say they plan to support circular business models, with fewer than a third calling resale a 2026 priority. A 2025 peer-reviewed study in Scientific Reports also found secondhand shoppers frequently replicate fast-fashion consumption patterns — high-volume, low-retention buying — undercutting the sustainability case for simply rerouting cheap clothes through resale.

The Morning Risk Report: Trump Adds to China Forced Labor Blacklist
On July 31, 2026, the Department of Homeland Security announced the addition of 43 China-based companies to the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act Entity List, the largest single expansion in the list's history and roughly a 30% increase that brings the total to 187 entities. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said, "Today we are adding 43 Chinese companies to the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act Entity List, and DHS will ensure their products do not enter our country." The newly listed firms span UFLPA high-priority enforcement sectors including aluminum, apparel, copper, cotton, tomatoes and downstream products, plus pharmaceuticals, and were flagged for sourcing material from Xinjiang or participating in labor-transfer recruitment programs. Beginning August 3, 2026, U.S. Customs and Border Protection applies the UFLPA's rebuttable presumption to goods produced by those entities, meaning shipments are barred unless importers rebut the presumption with clear and convincing evidence. The action is the first update to the Entity List since the close of the Biden administration in January 2025 — a roughly 18-month lapse that human-rights groups had criticized as weakening enforcement.

German Robotics Startup Agile Robots Set to Double Revenue This Year
Munich-based Agile Robots SE, spun out of the German Aerospace Center's Institute of Robotics and Mechatronics in 2018 by Zhaopeng Chen and Peter Meusel, has roughly doubled revenue every year since founding, most recently reaching about €200 million, and now employs more than 3,200 people worldwide. Bloomberg reported on June 2, 2026 that the company was in talks to raise about $800 million, with SoftBank Group in early discussions to contribute more than $300 million — an extension of SoftBank Vision Fund 2's earlier $220 million Series C that first pushed Agile Robots past a $1 billion valuation. Much of the revenue step-up is inorganic: its November 2025 acquisition of thyssenkrupp Automation Engineering added an immediate revenue stream of over €200 million plus automotive expertise and proprietary industrial data for training its foundation models, following an earlier purchase of insolvent rival Franka Emika. The company launched Agile ONE, its first industrial humanoid — with fingertip and force-torque sensors in every joint — in November 2025, showed it at Hannover Messe in April 2026, and began ramping full in-house production at a new Bavarian plant in early 2026. Named customers include Foxconn, BMW and Volkswagen, and the SoftBank interest fits its "Physical AI" push alongside its October 2025 purchase of ABB's robotics unit for $5.375 billion.

AI Images Are Everywhere. Here's What They Do to Our Brains, and What We Can Do.
The research base behind this column is now fairly concrete. An MIT Media Lab study by Pat Pataranutaporn, Chayapatr Archiwaranguprok, Samantha W.T. Chan, Pattie Maes and false-memory researcher Elizabeth Loftus ran 200 participants across four conditions of 50 each and found AI-generated video built from AI-edited images produced 2.05x as many false memories as the control condition, with participants also 1.19x more confident in those false recollections. Detection ability is worse than people assume: a 2026 study of AI versus real photographs found participants correctly identified AI-generated images only 63.7% of the time overall, and just 29% of the time when images came from FLUX.1-dev, with researchers documenting a pronounced gap between perceived and actual ability to tell synthetic from human-made material. EEG work on AI-generated political images shows heightened attentional and emotional processing traceable to late-stage executive network activity via the P300 and late positive potential components, linked to judgments of "humanness" and trustworthiness. Practical countermeasures flowing from this literature include treating provenance labels and truthfulness notifications as necessary but insufficient, slowing down before sharing emotionally charged imagery, and recognizing that repeated exposure — not one-off viewing — is what researchers say most needs further study for cumulative memory distortion.

The Race to Build an American Alternative to Cheap AI From China
Chinese open-weight models have taken over the low end of the market: a Hugging Face study dated March 16, 2026 found Chinese open-source models accounted for 41% of downloads, and Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5 currently tops open-model leaderboards at a reported $4.3 billion valuation. Price is the driver — Axios reported on August 1, 2026 that DeepSeek's new coding model charges roughly 28 cents for the same output volume that costs about $25 on Anthropic's Opus 4.8, a ~99% discount, while an hour-long coding session running about $10 on Claude costs under 50 cents on DeepSeek. U.S. buyers have followed: DoorDash built its DoorDash CLI on Moonshot, with cofounder and CTO Andy Fang citing "better quality" at a "cheaper cost"; Cursor used Kimi to help build its Composer 2 agent; and Lindy dropped Anthropic entirely for DeepSeek's V4 models, joining Airbnb and Siemens in testing Chinese providers. The American counterweight is a small cohort of open-weight labs — Arcee AI, Reflection AI and Poolside — with Arcee training Trinity Large plus three smaller open models for about $20 million in under six months on Nvidia Blackwell chips, while Reflection AI, founded by two ex-Google DeepMind researchers, raised $2 billion last year for the same mission. The cost gap they are fighting is severe: Innovation Endeavors estimated Llama 4 cost over $300 million to train and GPT-4 about $100 million, against DeepSeek's claimed $294,000 for R1, and critics such as Horizon3.ai CEO Snehal Antani warn that adopting Chinese models risks "severe data sovereignty violations" — the security argument U.S. labs are selling against price.

A Tech Founder Wanted to Start a New Country. An Actual Country Got in the Way.
The underlying topic is the "network state" movement colliding with existing sovereignty. Praxis, founded in 2019 by Dryden Brown and Charlie Callinan and describing itself as an "internet-native nation," announced up to $525 million in milestone-based financing in October 2024 and claims over 150,000 online citizens plus a 50,000-person waitlist; Brown traveled to Greenland in late 2024 and pitched politicians including Pele Broberg on a privatized charter state jointly owned via tokens by Praxis citizens, the Greenlandic government and individual Greenlanders, before pivoting in April 2025 to "Atlas," a proposed city on Vandenberg Space Force Base in Santa Barbara County, California — a site that as of mid-2025 still lacked the required federal Enhanced Use Lease. The more advanced cautionary tale is Próspera, the semi-autonomous ZEDE on Roatán incorporated in August 2017 by Erick Brimen and backed by Peter Thiel and Marc Andreessen: Honduras's Congress voted unanimously in 2022 to repeal the ZEDE law and the Supreme Court declared the zones unconstitutional in a 3-2 ruling with retroactive effect. Próspera responded with an ICSID arbitration under CAFTA-DR seeking roughly $10.8–11 billion, and on February 26, 2025 the tribunal rejected Honduras's preliminary objections, letting the claim proceed toward the merits with no final ruling expected soon. As of early 2026 Próspera still operates and construction on Roatán continues, and the political climate shifted with the contested election of right-wing president Nasry Asfura, whom Trump publicly backed. The through-line across both projects: raising hundreds of millions and recruiting citizens online is far easier than obtaining the land, legal charter, and host-government consent that statehood actually requires.

U.S. Stock Futures Rise, Oil Falls as U.S. Shifts Toward Diplomacy with Iran
On Sunday, Aug. 2, 2026, President Trump said on Truth Social that he had called off a planned strike on Iran after Tehran and other Middle Eastern countries requested a pause, claiming the perimeters of a deal covering the "Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT" and an end to Iran's nuclear threat had been agreed. Crude sold off hard on the news: September WTI futures fell roughly 6% to about $79.57 a barrel and October Brent lost 4.88% to about $83.64, a three-week low, after Brent had swung between roughly $72 and $102 during July. U.S. equity futures firmed in the Monday, Aug. 3 pre-market, with Dow futures up about 0.54% near 52,920, S&P 500 futures up about 0.49% near 7,550 and Nasdaq 100 futures up about 0.64% near 28,590. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said talks with Oman over management of the Strait of Hormuz were in their final stages, while other Iranian officials told the Mehr news agency that Trump's account of a pause request was "nothing but a new lie" and that forces remained on high alert. That split messaging kept a geopolitical risk premium in the market even as traders priced in de-escalation.

The Buffett Indicator Keeps Flashing Red. Is It Broken?
The Buffett Indicator — total U.S. stock market capitalization divided by GDP — hit roughly 232.5% as of June 30, 2026, the highest reading in the series' history going back to 1970, and was still around 228–230% in late July and early August 2026. Current Market Valuation's version put the ratio at 219% as of March 31, 2026 ($69.15 trillion in market value against $31.57 trillion of GDP), about 64.8% or 2.1 standard deviations above its historical trend line, yielding a verdict of "Strongly Overvalued." For scale, the long-run median sits near 84%, the dot-com peak reached roughly 172%, and the ratio first crossed 200% in 2021. The main case that it is broken: the numerator increasingly reflects the global earnings power of megacaps like Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Nvidia, while the denominator counts only domestic output, and the model ignores interest rates entirely (the 10-year Treasury was around 4.30%). Practitioners who substitute global GDP or U.S. GNP get meaningfully lower readings, and research consistently finds the ratio predicts 10-year forward returns reasonably well but has essentially no value for timing declines one to two years out.

'Something Was Wrong.' Hasbro's Lessons From a Cyberattack.
Hasbro identified unauthorized access to certain internal systems on March 28, 2026, and responded with a controlled shutdown, taking key systems offline, bringing in outside forensics specialists, invoking its business continuity plan, and delaying its first-quarter earnings report; the company warned it could need "several weeks" before the situation was fully resolved. Order processing, shipping and invoicing were disrupted, though Hasbro kept shipping globally, including Magic: The Gathering and the Secrets of Strixhaven set. When it reported second-quarter results on July 22, 2026, the company quantified the damage at roughly $25 million in lost revenue plus about $11 million in direct incremental expenses, and said order processing, shipping and invoicing had been restored to pre-incident levels. Despite that, Q2 revenue rose 16% year over year, adjusted operating profit reached $282 million (up 14%), adjusted EPS was $1.28, and CEO Chris Cocks noted Magic: The Gathering "eclipsed $500 million in quarterly revenue for the first time," while CFO and COO Gina Goetter raised full-year guidance to 5–7% constant-currency revenue growth. Bank of America upgraded its outlook on the disclosure and lifted its price target to $113.

Exclusive | Situational Awareness Down 67% in July in AI Stock Rout
Situational Awareness, the hedge fund run by 24-year-old former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, lost roughly 67% of its value in July 2026 after a historic momentum reversal hit both sides of its book. The fund peaked at about $45 billion in assets at the start of July — it had been up roughly 439% year to date through June — and was down to roughly $10 billion by July 30. Its core trade was long the AI infrastructure complex (SK Hynix, Micron, Nebius, CoreWeave, Broadcom) against short positions in software companies seen as disrupted by AI, such as Adobe; when chips sold off and software rallied, both legs went the wrong way at once. Reported leverage of roughly four times amplified the damage, triggering margin calls from prime brokers Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America and forcing liquidation of the entire public equity book. The broader AI complex shed roughly $3 trillion in market capitalization during the downturn, and the fund's roughly 12-person team was left with about $10 billion, including private positions such as its stake in Anthropic.

Best High-Yield Savings Accounts for August 2026: Up to 4.50%
As of August 3, 2026, top online savings yields cluster between roughly 4.00% and 4.50% APY, with Go2bank advertising up to 4.50% APY but only on savings-vault balances up to $5,000 and only with an active checking account, making the headline rate a capped promotional offer rather than a broadly available one. Below that, Elevault pays 4.34% APY, Axos ONE up to 4.21%, Newtek Bank's Personal High Yield Savings 4.20% (currently not accepting new applications, which the bank attributes to demand), EverBank via Raisin 4.15% guaranteed for 90 days, and CIT Platinum Savings and Pibank each around 4.10%; NerdWallet's roundup tops out at 4.21% and Bankrate's at 4.15% for uncapped accounts. Rates have stayed elevated because the Federal Reserve voted 9-3 on July 29, 2026 to hold the federal funds target range at 3.50%-3.75% — its fifth consecutive hold of 2026 — with dissents from Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari and Dallas's Lorie Logan, all pushing for higher rates against inflation that has run above the 2% target for more than five years. Markets now price in one to two rate hikes by year-end 2026 rather than the cuts expected earlier in the year, which argues against savings APYs falling near-term. These are variable rates, so anything above 4% today can be repriced the moment the Fed moves.

When It's Time to Have 'the Talk' With Your Parents
The underlying problem is that most American families never discuss money across generations: a Fidelity survey of about 1,900 adults age 18 and up found 56% say their parents never discussed money with them, and fewer than two in five adult children have had a substantive conversation about their parents' finances in the past year. The financial stakes are large and rising — CareScout's 2025 Cost of Care data puts the national median at $80,080 a year for an in-home non-medical caregiver (44 hours a week at $35 an hour, up 3% year over year), $74,400 a year for assisted living ($6,200 a month, up 5%), and $129,575 a year for a private nursing-home room ($355 a day, up 1%) — while HHS estimates nearly 3 in 4 Americans over 65 will need some form of long-term care. Roughly 63 million Americans now provide family caregiving, up nearly 50% since 2015, with the average family caregiver spending about $7,200 a year out of pocket (closer to $8,700 for long-distance caregivers), and about half reporting depleted savings, new debt, or lost income. Advisers consistently recommend starting before a crisis rather than in an emergency room, opening with the parent's wishes ("what do you want your care to look like if your health changes?" and "where do you want to live?") rather than with a demand for account balances. The concrete deliverables are a durable financial power of attorney, a healthcare directive/proxy, and a basic estate plan — typically $1,000 to $3,000 in legal fees — plus a shared inventory of accounts, insurance policies and advisers.

Visa to Buy Fraud Defense Platform BioCatch in $2.4 Billion Deal
Visa announced on August 3, 2026 that it will acquire Tel Aviv-based behavioral-biometrics firm BioCatch for $2.4 billion in cash from private-equity owner Permira and other shareholders. BioCatch's technology profiles typing cadence, touchscreen swipes and device handling to flag scams and account takeovers before a payment clears; it analyzes roughly 19 billion sessions a month, protects 1.8 billion devices and 760 million users, and serves more than 350 financial-institution clients across 21 countries, including over 100 of the world's largest banks. Andrew Torre, President of Value-Added Services at Visa, framed the rationale bluntly: "Account takeovers and scams cost the global economy over $1 trillion annually and AI is enabling these attacks at unprecedented scale." BioCatch CEO Gadi Mazor said real-time insight into customer intent is "increasingly essential" for banks to establish trust inside digital sessions. Visa expects the deal to close by the end of its fiscal second quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals; it is the latest bolt-on to Visa's value-added services unit, one of its fastest-growing divisions.

AI Stocks Stage a Rebound
After a six-session slide driven by fears that hyperscalers would throttle AI capital spending, AI and semiconductor stocks ripped higher on July 30, 2026, with the Nasdaq Composite closing up 2.8% at 25,122.18 — its best day since June — the S&P 500 up 1.7% to 7,437.63 and the Dow up 613.92 points, or 1.2%, to 52,208.06. The catalyst was Microsoft, whose Azure revenue grew 43% in constant currency versus roughly 40% expected; the stock jumped about 16% and added on the order of $450 billion in market value in what was reported as the largest single-day market-cap gain ever for a company. Chips led the tape: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged 8.19%, its biggest one-day gain in about 15 months (since April 9, 2025), with Micron up 15.7%, Lam Research up roughly 17%, SK Hynix ADRs up 15.3%, AMD up 12.7% and Nvidia up about 2%. The rebound carried into Monday, August 3, when the Nasdaq rose another 1.7%, the S&P 500 1.1% and the Dow 1%, helped by Amazon's results and by easing oil prices after a diplomatic turn in the US-Iran conflict. The next test is Nvidia's fiscal second-quarter report on August 26, for which the company has guided to revenue of $91.0 billion, plus or minus 2%.

NYSE Owner to Buy MarketAxess in $6 Billion Deal
Intercontinental Exchange, the parent of the New York Stock Exchange, agreed on July 30, 2026 to acquire electronic bond-trading platform MarketAxess Holdings for $167 a share in cash, an equity value of about $6 billion and an enterprise value of roughly $5.7 billion, representing a 33% premium to MarketAxess's prior close. MarketAxess shares surged about 28-30%, while ICE shares were roughly flat to slightly higher. ICE Chair and CEO Jeff Sprecher said the combination will "build the fixed-income ecosystem that investors have always deserved — one that is transparent, efficient, fully connected, and accessible to all," pitching a single platform spanning pre-trade pricing and analytics through electronic execution and post-trade compliance; MarketAxess CEO Chris Concannon cast the fit as complementary, pairing MarketAxess's credit-trading network with ICE's retail protocols and data connectivity. ICE targets $100 million in annual run-rate expense synergies within three years of closing and plans to fund the purchase entirely with newly issued debt — bonds, a term loan and commercial paper — returning to a 3.0x gross leverage ratio within 18 to 24 months after close. Both boards have approved; closing is targeted for the first half of 2027, pending MarketAxess shareholder and regulatory approvals, and the deal squares ICE up directly against Tradeweb and Bloomberg in electronic credit trading.

Adidas Stock Slumps After World Cup Spend Eats Into Earnings
Adidas shares fell roughly 17-18% on July 30, 2026 — reported as the steepest single-day drop since its November 1995 IPO — even though the company posted record second-quarter net sales of €6.7 billion, up 14% currency-neutral. The problem was margin: operating profit rose only about 5% to €574 million, missing analyst expectations of roughly €623 million, as marketing and point-of-sale spending hit €924 million, or 13.7% of sales versus 12.0% a year earlier — €212 million more than the prior-year quarter — pushing operating margin down to 8.5% from 9.2%. The spending went into FIFA World Cup activation, notably the "Backyard Legends" campaign with Timothée Chalamet, Lionel Messi, Lamine Yamal, Jude Bellingham and Bad Bunny, which Adidas said drew more than 9 billion views and over 400 million engagements. CEO Bjørn Gulden was unrepentant about the tournament push, saying "This World Cup was like a fairy tale for me!" Adidas raised full-year currency-neutral revenue guidance to 9-10% growth from high-single-digit, but left the full-year operating profit target at about €2.3 billion — the combination that told investors the World Cup revenue lift would not drop to the bottom line.
