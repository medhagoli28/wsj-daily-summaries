Can Fast Fashion Fit Into Secondhand Clothes?** — The underlying business story is resale's shift from niche to structural competitor: ThredUp's 14th Annual Resale Report, released April 2, 2026 with analysis by GlobalData and a survey of 3,268 U.S. consumers conducted in January–February 2026, pegged the global secondhand apparel market at $393 billion, roughly 10% of total apparel spend, and projected the U.S. market alone to hit $78.8 billion by 2030. **4. AI Images Are Everywhere. Here's What They Do to Our Brains, and What We Can Do.** — The research base behind this column is now fairly concrete. An MIT Media Lab study by Pat Pataranutaporn, Chayapatr Archiwaranguprok, Samantha W.T. Chan, Pattie Maes and false-memory researcher Elizabeth Loftus ran 200 participants across four conditions of 50 each and found AI-generated video built from AI-edited images produced 2.05x as many false memories as the control condition, with participants also 1.19x more confident in those false recollections. Detection ability is worse than people assume: a 2026 study of AI versus real photographs found participants correctly identified AI-generated images only 63.7% of the time overall, and just 29% of the time when images came from FLUX.1-dev, with researchers documenting a pronounced gap between perceived and actual ability to tell synthetic from human-made material. EEG work on AI-generated political images shows heightened attentional and emotional processing traceable to late-stage executive network activity via the P300 and late positive potential components, linked to judgments of "humanness" and trustworthiness. Practical countermeasures flowing from this literature include treating provenance labels and truthfulness notifications as necessary but insufficient, slowing down before sharing emotionally charged imagery, and recognizing that repeated exposure — not one-off viewing — is what researchers say most needs further study for cumulative memory distortion. *Sources: [arXiv / MIT Media Lab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08895), [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1995-8692/18/6/61), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882125000696).*

**6. A Tech Founder Wanted to Start a New Country. An Actual Country Got in the Way.** — The underlying topic is the "network state" movement colliding with existing sovereignty. Praxis, founded in 2019 by Dryden Brown and Charlie Callinan and describing itself as an "internet-native nation," announced up to $525 million in milestone-based financing in October 2024 and claims over 150,000 online citizens plus a 50,000-person waitlist; Brown traveled to Greenland in late 2024 and pitched politicians including Pele Broberg on a privatized charter state jointly owned via tokens by Praxis citizens, the Greenlandic government and individual Greenlanders, before pivoting in April 2025 to "Atlas," a proposed city on Vandenberg Space Force Base in Santa Barbara County, California — a site that as of mid-2025 still lacked the required federal Enhanced Use Lease. The more advanced cautionary tale is Próspera, the semi-autonomous ZEDE on Roatán incorporated in August 2017 by Erick Brimen and backed by Peter Thiel and Marc Andreessen: Honduras's Congress voted unanimously in 2022 to repeal the ZEDE law and the Supreme Court declared the zones unconstitutional in a 3-2 ruling with retroactive effect. Próspera responded with an ICSID arbitration under CAFTA-DR seeking roughly $10.8–11 billion, and on February 26, 2025 the tribunal rejected Honduras's preliminary objections, letting the claim proceed toward the merits with no final ruling expected soon. As of early 2026 Próspera still operates and construction on Roatán continues, and the political climate shifted with the contested election of right-wing president Nasry Asfura, whom Trump publicly backed. The through-line across both projects: raising hundreds of millions and recruiting citizens online is far easier than obtaining the land, legal charter, and host-government consent that statehood actually requires. *Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxis_(proposed_city)), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/praxis-another-tech-billionaire-backed-urban-project-gets-525-million-in-funding-2000512690), [UNCTAD Investment Policy Hub](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/investment-dispute-settlement/cases/1292/pr-spera-and-others-v-honduras).*

**2. The Buffett Indicator Keeps Flashing Red. Is It Broken?** — The Buffett Indicator — total U.S. stock market capitalization divided by GDP — hit roughly 232.5% as of June 30, 2026, the highest reading in the series' history going back to 1970, and was still around 228–230% in late July and early August 2026. Current Market Valuation's version put the ratio at 219% as of March 31, 2026 ($69.15 trillion in market value against $31.57 trillion of GDP), about 64.8% or 2.1 standard deviations above its historical trend line, yielding a verdict of "Strongly Overvalued." For scale, the long-run median sits near 84%, the dot-com peak reached roughly 172%, and the ratio first crossed 200% in 2021. The main case that it is broken: the numerator increasingly reflects the global earnings power of megacaps like Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Nvidia, while the denominator counts only domestic output, and the model ignores interest rates entirely (the 10-year Treasury was around 4.30%). Practitioners who substitute global GDP or U.S. GNP get meaningfully lower readings, and research consistently finds the ratio predicts 10-year forward returns reasonably well but has essentially no value for timing declines one to two years out. *Sources: [Current Market Valuation](https://www.currentmarketvaluation.com/models/buffett-indicator.php), [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/07/08/buffett-valuation-indicator-june-2026), [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/05/what-the-buffett-market-valuation-indicator-at-rec/).*

**2. When It's Time to Have 'the Talk' With Your Parents** — The underlying problem is that most American families never discuss money across generations: a Fidelity survey of about 1,900 adults age 18 and up found 56% say their parents never discussed money with them, and fewer than two in five adult children have had a substantive conversation about their parents' finances in the past year. The financial stakes are large and rising — CareScout's 2025 Cost of Care data puts the national median at $80,080 a year for an in-home non-medical caregiver (44 hours a week at $35 an hour, up 3% year over year), $74,400 a year for assisted living ($6,200 a month, up 5%), and $129,575 a year for a private nursing-home room ($355 a day, up 1%) — while HHS estimates nearly 3 in 4 Americans over 65 will need some form of long-term care. Roughly 63 million Americans now provide family caregiving, up nearly 50% since 2015, with the average family caregiver spending about $7,200 a year out of pocket (closer to $8,700 for long-distance caregivers), and about half reporting depleted savings, new debt, or lost income. Advisers consistently recommend starting before a crisis rather than in an emergency room, opening with the parent's wishes ("what do you want your care to look like if your health changes?" and "where do you want to live?") rather than with a demand for account balances. The concrete deliverables are a durable financial power of attorney, a healthcare directive/proxy, and a basic estate plan — typically $1,000 to $3,000 in legal fees — plus a shared inventory of accounts, insurance policies and advisers. *Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/11/27/56percent-of-americans-say-their-parents-never-discussed-money-with-them.html), [CareScout Cost of Care](https://www.carescout.com/cost-of-care), [Stacker via Alabama Gazette](https://www.alabamagazette.com/story/2026/07/13/seniors/how-do-i-address-the-financial-reality-of-caring-for-my-aging-parents-it-costs-the-average-family-7200-a-year/11873.html).*

CEO Bjørn Gulden was unrepentant about the tournament push, saying "This World Cup was like a fairy tale for me!" Adidas raised full-year currency-neutral revenue guidance to 9-10% growth from high-single-digit, but left the full-year operating profit target at about €2.3 billion — the combination that told investors the World Cup revenue lift would not drop to the bottom line. *Sources: [SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/financial-results/why-adidas-shares-fell-despite-record-sales-higher-guidance/122476.article), [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/commerce/2026/world-cup-adidas-revenue-stock-drop-1234940532/), [Quartz](https://qz.com/adidas-record-sales-profit-miss-world-cup-new-cfo-073026).*
