## 💻 Tech

**1. Developing Economies Have More to Gain and Less to Lose From AI, Says World Bank** — The World Bank released its *World Development Report 2026: The Promise of Artificial Intelligence* on August 4, 2026, arguing that AI could let poorer countries "do in a decade what might otherwise take a century." The report finds jobs in high-income countries are roughly three times as likely to be automated by generative AI — 14.2% at risk versus 4.5% in low- and middle-income countries — while the productivity upside is nearly comparable, with 16.2% of jobs in developing economies set to see AI-boosted output against 18.7% in rich ones. It also notes AI is diffusing far faster than prior technologies: middle-income countries accounted for half of ChatGPT's global traffic within six months of launch, versus the roughly 40 years electricity took to reach lower-income countries. Co-author Gaurav Nayyar framed it as "a golden opportunity," but warned countries that fail to build complements — reliable electricity, internet access, digital skills, local-language data and institutions — risk falling behind, and the Bank cautions AI could also worsen income inequality, misinformation and political repression. The prescribed framework is "adopt, adapt, advance," favoring low-cost "small AI" delivered over text, voice and basic phones rather than trillion-dollar frontier models, citing examples like the UAE's Falcon model tuned for local languages. *Sources: [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/wdr2026), [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/08/04/world-bank-urges-developing-countries-to-embrace-ai-or-fall-behind/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/world-bank-urges-emerging-nations-to-embrace-ai-amid-weak-growth).*

**2. Exclusive | Security Flaw Placed 30 Years of DNA Evidence at Risk of Hacking** — Thermo Fisher Scientific issued a security bulletin on July 31, 2026 patching CVE-2026-17583, a high-severity flaw (CVSS v4.0 score 8.2) in its Applied Biosystems Human Identification software that let attackers alter the .fsa and .hid data files crime labs generate from physical DNA evidence with essentially no forensic trace. Researchers Nathan Adams of Forensic Bioinformatics, Kevin Dyer and Laura Gaydosh Combs found the flaw and coordinated disclosure through CISA; Adams demonstrated modifying a file from a public dataset in about 45 minutes, and the team used AI coding tools to build the manipulation code, which is part of why a weakness dating to roughly 1995 became practically exploitable only now. Patches shipped for five product lines — 3500/3500xL Data Collection Software 4.0.3, 3730/3730xL 5.0.3, SeqStudio Genetic Analyzer 1.2.6, SeqStudio Flex 1.2.1, and GeneMapper ID-X 1.7.4 — while three end-of-life products get no fix at all. The updates add digital signatures so labs can verify files haven't been altered, and Thermo Fisher recommends encrypted storage, restricted access, chain-of-custody controls and network isolation for systems that can't be patched. As of August 3, 2026 the company said it was aware of no exploitation in the wild, and the CVE had not yet appeared on NVD or in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. *Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/thermo-fisher-patches-flaw-that-could.html), [Forensic Magazine](https://www.forensicmag.com/3594-All-News/627199-Security-Flaw-Left-Popular-DNA-Software-Vulnerable-to-Hacking-for-30-Years/), [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/dna-test-software-vulnerability/).*

**3. Essay | Move Fast and Regulate Later: The Paradox of China's Economic Dynamism** — This is an opinion essay rather than a news event, so the summary covers its subject: China's practice of letting new sectors scale in regulatory gray zones and imposing rules only after national champions emerge. The clearest case study is the roughly 32-month campaign that began in late 2020, when Beijing halted Ant Group's IPO (which would have been the world's largest), forced Didi Global to delist from the U.S., and gutted after-school tutoring — erasing more than $1 trillion in Chinese tech market value before easing from 2023, when Didi resumed new user registration, game licenses restarted, and Jack Ma reappeared publicly. The same sequencing is visible in 2026's "anti-involution" campaign against ruinous price wars: regulators opened a formal antitrust probe into Trip.com and summoned a dozen tech giants including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance's Douyin, Baidu, JD.com and Meituan over price competition ahead of the June shopping festival, and published Price Behaviour Compliance Guidelines for the Automotive Industry targeting below-cost car pricing. The economic backdrop shows why it is a paradox: auto-sector profit margins fell from 7.8% in 2017 to 4.3% in 2024 and 4.4% in 2025, deflation is in its fourth year with consumer prices near zero, and Beijing set its lowest growth target since the 1990s at 4.5–5% — even as Q1 2026 GDP grew 5.0% year over year, passenger-car exports jumped 60.6%, and 2026 fiscal plans allocate roughly 1.3 trillion yuan to science and technology. Analysts note the state now favors targeted enforcement over another blanket crackdown, but the underlying bargain — permissionless growth first, discipline afterward — is what the essay's title flags. *Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/23/china-tech-crackdown-antitrust-tripcom-meituan-food-delivery-anti-involution-deflation-.html), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3227753/timeline-chinas-32-month-big-tech-crackdown-killed-worlds-largest-ipo-and-wiped-out-trillions-value), [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/chinas-q1-2026-gdp/).*
