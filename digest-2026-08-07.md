**3. The Latest Scary-Sounding AI Milestone: A Brand-New Virus** — Researchers at Stanford and the Arc Institute reported in Science (DOI 10.1126/science.aec2657) in early August 2026 the first generative design of complete, viable viral genomes, using the Evo genome language models fine-tuned on roughly 15,000 Microviridae genomes to redesign phiX174, a compact single-stranded DNA bacteriophage of about 5 kilobases and 11 genes. From thousands of computationally generated candidates the team synthesized and tested roughly 300 diverse genomes, and 16 of them replicated in E. coli and lysed the bacteria, with some designs sharing as little as 63% average amino-acid identity with natural proteins. Several of the AI-designed phages showed faster lysis kinetics than wild-type phiX174 or outcompeted it in growth assays, and a cocktail of the generated phages overcame resistance in three phiX174-resistant E. coli strains that the natural phage alone could not defeat — a proof of concept for phage therapy against drug-resistant bacteria. The work first appeared as a bioRxiv preprint on Sept. 17, 2025, and the team excluded human-infecting viruses from the training data as a biosecurity precaution. An accompanying Science perspective by Thomas Inglesby and Moritz S. Hanke argued that biosecurity rules and oversight need to catch up quickly as genome-design models become more capable and more widely available, since the same methods could in principle be pointed at human pathogens. *Sources: [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-sixteen-ai-viruses-route-drug.html), [Science Media Centre](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-generative-design-of-bacteriophages-with-genome-language-models/), [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.12.675911v1.full.pdf).*

A User’s Guide to the Universe of Rogue AI Bots** — The rogue-bot problem is now measurable rather than anecdotal: Cloudflare's investigation, published Aug. 4, 2025, documented Perplexity running undeclared crawlers that spoofed a macOS Chrome user agent (Chrome/124.0.0.0 Safari/537.36) across tens of thousands of domains at 3 million to 6 million requests a day, on top of 20 million to 25 million daily requests from its declared crawler, while rotating IP addresses and source ASNs to evade blocks — prompting Cloudflare to de-list it as a verified bot and ship detection signatures. Cloudflare's own telemetry shows the economics behind the friction: roughly 80% of AI bot crawling is for training, with search and user-action purposes each under 5%, and crawl-to-refer ratios running about 50,000:1 for Anthropic, 887:1 for OpenAI and 118:1 for Perplexity, meaning publishers give up far more content than they get back in traffic. Cloudflare has since moved to blocking training and agent crawlers by default for new domains while leaving search crawlers allowed. Congress responded on July 23, 2026 when Reps. Laurel Lee (R-FL-15), Valerie Foushee (D-NC-04) and Gus Bilirakis (R-FL-09) introduced H.R. 9915, the bipartisan Stealth Bot Prohibition Act, which would require bots to disclose their identity and purpose, bar residential-proxy masking, and let the FTC seek penalties reported at up to roughly $53,000 per violation, with state attorneys general also able to sue. The bill was referred to House Energy and Commerce and drew support from the News/Media Alliance, whose president Danielle Coffey, along with News Corp and Condé Nast executives, framed disguised scraping as an existential drain on publishers. *Sources: [Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/perplexity-is-using-stealth-undeclared-crawlers-to-evade-website-no-crawl-directives/), [Cloudflare Radar](https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-crawler-traffic-by-purpose-and-industry/), [News/Media Alliance](https://www.newsmediaalliance.org/stealth-bot-prohibition-act-introduction/).*

**7. The Dividend Mind Trick** — The column's underlying subject is the "free dividend fallacy" — the behavioral illusion that a payout is bonus money layered on top of the share price, when in fact a stock that pays $1 a share drops by roughly $1 on the ex-date, so collecting a $1,000 dividend and selling $1,000 of stock leave an investor in nearly the same position once taxes and trading costs are counted. The August 2026 backdrop made the illusion unusually expensive: the S&P 500's dividend yield stood at 1.039% as of Aug. 13, 2026, the lowest in the recorded series and far below its historical high near 6.66%, because prices have compounded far faster than payouts and the index is now dominated by megacap technology names that distribute little or nothing. That leaves the income trade-off stark, with the 10-year Treasury yielding roughly 4.65% — about four times what a broad index fund pays — and forcing retirees who built plans around equity income to rethink the strategy. Corporate behavior has reinforced the shift, since US buybacks have exceeded dividends since the late 1990s and JPMorgan estimated a record roughly $1.2 trillion of repurchases, 50% to 100% above the annual dividend payout. The practical cost of compensating by chasing yield is well documented: those investors end up less diversified, concentrated in a handful of high-payout names they often overpaid for, and carrying larger tax bills. *Sources: [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/economic_indicators/150/sp-500-dividend-yield), [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/12/the-sp-500-yield-is-the-lowest-in-history-5-members-of-the-legacy-index-yield-6-or-more/), [Moneywise](https://moneywise.com/investing/investing-basics/sp-500-dividend-yield-record-low-retirees-strategy).*
