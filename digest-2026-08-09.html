Move 37 Is the Moment AI Changes Everything. It’s Suddenly Happening Everywhere.
Move 37 refers to AlphaGo's stone placement in Game 2 against Lee Sedol in Seoul on March 10, 2016, a move human commentators initially read as a mistake but which sat in exactly the right spot roughly 100 moves later to win the game; DeepMind's own models put the odds of a human playing it at about one in ten thousand, and more than 200 million people watched the match. A decade on the phrase has become shorthand for AI producing results its creators did not anticipate, and 2026 supplied a run of them: DeepMind's AlphaEvolve found a way to multiply 4x4 complex-valued matrices with 48 scalar multiplications, breaking Volker Strassen's 1969 record of 49, and across 67 open mathematics problems it improved 23 and matched the best known result on 36. UCLA's Ernest Ryu settled Nesterov's 1983 convergence conjecture in October 2025 after 42 years using ChatGPT across roughly 12 hours over three days; in January 2026 a group including Jordan Ellenberg, Nicolás Libedinsky, David Plaza, José Simental and Geordie Williamson used AI assistance to uncover hypercube structure in Bruhat intervals of permutation groups; and in the February 2026 First Proof challenge models solved more than half of ten research-level problems. The same lineage produced AlphaFold2, which resolved the 50-year protein-folding problem, released structures for all 200 million known proteins, is used by more than 3 million researchers, and won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Skeptics including Akshay Venkatesh and AMS president Ravi Vakil note the models mostly clear a backlog of tractable open problems and raise unresolved questions about attribution, while Toronto's Daniel Litt has argued the shift may prove larger than the arrival of the computer itself.

The Latest Scary-Sounding AI Milestone: A Brand-New Virus
Researchers at Stanford and the Arc Institute reported in Science (DOI 10.1126/science.aec2657) in early August 2026 the first generative design of complete, viable viral genomes, using the Evo genome language models fine-tuned on roughly 15,000 Microviridae genomes to redesign phiX174, a compact single-stranded DNA bacteriophage of about 5 kilobases and 11 genes. From thousands of computationally generated candidates the team synthesized and tested roughly 300 diverse genomes, and 16 of them replicated in E. coli and lysed the bacteria, with some designs sharing as little as 63% average amino-acid identity with natural proteins. Several of the AI-designed phages showed faster lysis kinetics than wild-type phiX174 or outcompeted it in growth assays, and a cocktail of the generated phages overcame resistance in three phiX174-resistant E. coli strains that the natural phage alone could not defeat — a proof of concept for phage therapy against drug-resistant bacteria. The work first appeared as a bioRxiv preprint on Sept. 17, 2025, and the team excluded human-infecting viruses from the training data as a biosecurity precaution. An accompanying Science perspective by Thomas Inglesby and Moritz S. Hanke argued that biosecurity rules and oversight need to catch up quickly as genome-design models become more capable and more widely available, since the same methods could in principle be pointed at human pathogens.

The Pros and Cons of Putting Extra Dollars in a Trump Account
This is an advice column rather than a news event, weighing whether parents should add their own money to the new "Trump Accounts" beyond the free federal seed. Under the Working Families Tax Cuts, every U.S.-citizen child born from Jan. 1, 2025 through Dec. 31, 2028 with a work-authorized Social Security number gets a one-time $1,000 federal pilot contribution that does not count against the annual cap, and accounts began accepting outside money on July 4, 2026. Contributors can put in a combined $5,000 a year per child (indexed for inflation after 2027), of which up to $2,500 can come from an employer; individual contributions are made with after-tax dollars and get no deduction, while government, employer and charitable contributions can be pre-tax. Treasury and IRS guidance (Notice 2025-68) requires the money to sit in low-cost mutual funds or ETFs tracking the S&P 500 or a similar broad U.S. equity index, and bars any withdrawal before Jan. 1 of the year the child turns 18, after which the account is generally treated as a traditional IRA. That last point drives the main trade-off flagged by planners: earnings come out as ordinary income (with a possible early-withdrawal penalty), whereas a 529 pays qualified education costs tax-free and a custodial Roth IRA grows tax-free, so extra dollars often work harder elsewhere once the free $1,000 is claimed.

The Unexpected Joy of Downsizing Slowly in Retirement
This is an advice feature rather than a news event, and the underlying data show most older Americans are not downsizing at all: an Ipsos survey of roughly 4,000 U.S. residents conducted for Redfin in May 2025 found 33.5% of baby-boomer homeowners say they will never sell, another 30% won't sell within the next decade, and 44.6% of the Silent Generation never plan to sell — versus 25% of Gen X and 21% of millennials/Gen Z. Roughly 67% of boomer homeowners have lived in their current home 16 or more years, and 88% are in single-family houses, so a staged, multi-year downsizing is often the practical alternative to a single disruptive move. Reasons for staying are as much financial as sentimental: 55% simply like their home, 30% have it nearly or fully paid off, 31% say they couldn't afford a comparable home in their own neighborhood today, 16% cite home prices, and 8% won't give up a low mortgage rate. The economics cut both ways — the typical U.S. homeowner holds roughly $299,000 in equity (higher for long-tenured older owners), but smaller homes, condos and age-restricted properties frequently cost as much as or more than the house being sold once rising insurance premiums and HOA fees are counted. The demographic pressure is real, with roughly 12,000 Americans turning 65 every day, and about 28% of U.S. three-bedroom-plus homes owned by boomers in one- or two-adult households versus 16% held by millennials with children.

NAHB's framing lumber composite was $558.19 per 1,000 board feet on Aug. 7, 2026 — flat week over week, up 2.1% month over month and down just 2.6% year over year — while lumber futures were off 11.4% annually, and North American softwood consumption actually fell in 2026 as mills in both the U.S. and Canada curtailed output. Trade policy is a big part of the wedge: a 10% Section 232 tariff on softwood timber and lumber has applied globally since Oct. 14, 2025, stacked on antidumping and countervailing duties that put the combined rate on most Canadian softwood at roughly 35% pending a final determination expected in late August 2026, with a preliminary revision cutting the AD/CVD portion to 24.83%. Canada supplies about a quarter of U.S. softwood demand, and industry estimates put the added tariff/duty cost at $10,000 or more on a new single-family home, which uses roughly 15,000 board feet of framing lumber. Meanwhile the building side is sputtering: NAHB/Wells Fargo builder confidence fell to 34 in July 2026, below 40 for a 15th straight month, single-family starts slipped 0.2% in June to a 897,000-to-895,000 annual pace and single-family permits fell 2.4% to 871,000, with June's 19% headline jump in total starts to 1.427 million coming almost entirely from multifamily.

U.K. Waves Through $81 Billion Paramount-Warner Bros. Deal
On Aug. 6, 2026 the U.K. Competition and Markets Authority cleared Paramount Skydance's roughly $81 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery without opening an in-depth Phase 2 probe, concluding the combination would not substantially lessen competition in British film distribution, TV production, children's programming or streaming. The CMA reasoned that the merged company would still face meaningful rivals including Disney, Universal, Sony, Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, Apple, the BBC's iPlayer and ITVX. Separately, Britain's Department for Culture, Media and Sport said it would not intervene on media-plurality grounds after Paramount offered legally binding commitments covering its U.K. footprint — keeping Channel 5's editorial direction fully independent, not bundling its linear channels with on-demand services in the U.K., and keeping kids' channels such as Nickelodeon and Cartoon Network distinct from one another. The dual clearance removed one of the most significant non-U.S. regulatory obstacles to closing what would be one of the largest media deals ever, leaving remaining approvals elsewhere as the gating items.

Flutter CEO to Step Down as U.S. Sports-Betting Business Falters; Profit Outlook Cut
Flutter Entertainment announced on Aug. 5, 2026, alongside second-quarter results, that CEO Peter Jackson will step down Sept. 30, 2026 after nearly nine years, with Dan Taylor — currently president and CEO of Flutter's International division, which produced more than $9 billion of revenue and over $2.2 billion of adjusted EBITDA in 2025 — taking over as group CEO on Oct. 1 and joining the board; Jackson stays on as an adviser through year-end. The results underneath the change were poor: group revenue rose just 3% to $4.326 billion, but Flutter swung to a net loss of $296 million from $37 million of net income a year earlier, and adjusted EBITDA fell to $508 million from $919 million as margin compressed to 11.7% from 21.9%. The U.S. business was the problem — revenue fell 6% to $1.683 billion as FanDuel sportsbook revenue dropped 15% on adverse sporting outcomes, only partly offset by 14% iGaming growth, and U.S. adjusted EBITDA collapsed roughly 70% to $119 million from $400 million; international revenue grew 10% to $2.643 billion but its EBITDA still fell to $476 million from $591 million on higher U.K. gaming taxes and marketing. Flutter cut full-year guidance accordingly, taking group revenue down $395 million to $17.91 billion at the midpoint and adjusted EBITDA down $210 million to $2.655 billion, while lifting U.S. promotional spend to about 6% of handle to defend share. Shares fell about 11.5% on Aug. 5 and were down roughly 50% year to date; the transition follows FanDuel CEO Amy Howe's departure in May.

Fox Will Keep NFL Rights Deal Through 2029
On Fox Corp.'s Aug. 6, 2026 earnings call, CEO Lachlan Murdoch said the company would not amend its NFL media rights agreement early, noting that its existing contractual relationship runs through the completion of the 2029 season and that Fox would engage with the league closer to the 2030 season, the customary timetable. Fox pays roughly $2.25 billion a year for the exclusive NFC Sunday afternoon package under the 11-year agreement announced in March 2021, which began with the 2023 season, runs through 2033, and carries four Super Bowls in 2023, 2025, 2029 and 2033 plus expanded digital rights including NFL content on Tubi. That contract contains a one-time NFL termination option after year seven covering the 2030-2033 seasons — the opt-out at the center of the story — and the league had held preliminary talks with both Fox and CBS parent Paramount Skydance about eliminating the opt-out in exchange for higher rights fees. Murdoch described the NFL relationship as incredibly positive and said he sees a clear path forward through the opt-out, effectively deferring any renegotiation for roughly three more years and blocking the league's attempt to reprice the package early. Fox separately added two national game windows for the coming season and struck a multiyear deal to broadcast NFL games in Mexico.

ConocoPhillips CEO Ryan Lance to Retire, as Finance Chief Takes the Helm
ConocoPhillips announced on Aug. 6, 2026 that Ryan Lance will retire as president and CEO after 14 years in the role and 42 years at the company, moving into a transitional executive chair role on the board. CFO and executive vice president of Strategy and Commercial Andy O'Brien will succeed him as president and CEO and join the board effective Sept. 1, 2026, while Konnie Haynes-Welsh, currently vice president of Finance and Controller, becomes senior vice president and CFO the same day. O'Brien joined Conoco in 1997 and has held progressively senior finance, planning and strategy roles, overseeing Alaska, international operations, LNG, commercial activity, finance and corporate strategy since joining the executive leadership team in 2022; Haynes-Welsh joined in 2012 and worked across corporate strategy, compliance, Lower 48 operations and treasury. Lead independent director Robert Niblock credited Lance with setting the course for the newly independent ConocoPhillips formed in 2012 and building it into a recognized sector leader through major acquisitions and portfolio optimization. Lance was the longest-serving chief executive among the major oil companies, and the succession was disclosed the same day the company posted second-quarter earnings of $3.9 billion, or $3.23 per share, nearly double the year-earlier $2.0 billion.
