Move 37 Is the Moment AI Changes Everything. It’s Suddenly Happening Everywhere.
Move 37 refers to AlphaGo's stone placement in Game 2 against Lee Sedol in Seoul on March 10, 2016, a move human commentators initially read as a mistake but which sat in exactly the right spot roughly 100 moves later to win the game; DeepMind's own models put the odds of a human playing it at about one in ten thousand, and more than 200 million people watched the match. A decade on the phrase has become shorthand for AI producing results its creators did not anticipate, and 2026 supplied a run of them: DeepMind's AlphaEvolve found a way to multiply 4x4 complex-valued matrices with 48 scalar multiplications, breaking Volker Strassen's 1969 record of 49, and across 67 open mathematics problems it improved 23 and matched the best known result on 36. UCLA's Ernest Ryu settled Nesterov's 1983 convergence conjecture in October 2025 after 42 years using ChatGPT across roughly 12 hours over three days; in January 2026 a group including Jordan Ellenberg, Nicolás Libedinsky, David Plaza, José Simental and Geordie Williamson used AI assistance to uncover hypercube structure in Bruhat intervals of permutation groups; and in the February 2026 First Proof challenge models solved more than half of ten research-level problems. The same lineage produced AlphaFold2, which resolved the 50-year protein-folding problem, released structures for all 200 million known proteins, is used by more than 3 million researchers, and won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Skeptics including Akshay Venkatesh and AMS president Ravi Vakil note the models mostly clear a backlog of tractable open problems and raise unresolved questions about attribution, while Toronto's Daniel Litt has argued the shift may prove larger than the arrival of the computer itself.

The Pros and Cons of Putting Extra Dollars in a Trump Account
This is an advice column rather than a news event, weighing whether parents should add their own money to the new "Trump Accounts" beyond the free federal seed. Under the Working Families Tax Cuts, every U.S.-citizen child born from Jan. 1, 2025 through Dec. 31, 2028 with a work-authorized Social Security number gets a one-time $1,000 federal pilot contribution that does not count against the annual cap, and accounts began accepting outside money on July 4, 2026. Contributors can put in a combined $5,000 a year per child (indexed for inflation after 2027), of which up to $2,500 can come from an employer; individual contributions are made with after-tax dollars and get no deduction, while government, employer and charitable contributions can be pre-tax. Treasury and IRS guidance (Notice 2025-68) requires the money to sit in low-cost mutual funds or ETFs tracking the S&P 500 or a similar broad U.S. equity index, and bars any withdrawal before Jan. 1 of the year the child turns 18, after which the account is generally treated as a traditional IRA. That last point drives the main trade-off flagged by planners: earnings come out as ordinary income (with a possible early-withdrawal penalty), whereas a 529 pays qualified education costs tax-free and a custodial Roth IRA grows tax-free, so extra dollars often work harder elsewhere once the free $1,000 is claimed.

Taylor Farms Recalls Products With Jalapeños Implicated in Salmonella Outbreak
On Aug. 9, 2026 Taylor Fresh Foods (Taylor Farms) recalled finished products made with jalapeño peppers — including salsas and guacamole — after Coast Citrus Distributors voluntarily recalled fresh jalapeños over possible Salmonella contamination traced to a grower in Sinaloa, Mexico. The recalled items went to retail distribution centers in 26 states (AL, AR, FL, GA, IL, IN, KS, KY, LA, MA, ME, MI, MN, MO, MS, NE, NH, NY, OH, OK, SC, TN, TX, VT, WI, WV) and carried "Best If Used By" dates through Aug. 16, 2026, reaching stores including Kroger, Walmart, Target, Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, Hannaford and Stop & Shop. The CDC and FDA were investigating a multistate Salmonella outbreak linked to the jalapeños that had sickened 345 people across 27 states with 36 hospitalizations. Taylor Farms said it stopped sourcing from the implicated grower and was filling orders from alternative suppliers, and urged consumers to discard affected product rather than eat it. The episode landed amid an unusually heavy stretch of produce-linked recalls, intensifying scrutiny of imported fresh-cut ingredient supply chains.

Standalone costs bit into profitability: net income attributable to shareholders fell 30.1% to $211 million from $302 million, and adjusted EBITDA declined 8.9% to $624 million on lower distribution revenue, higher interest expense and the cost of operating independently. The company still raised its full-year 2026 outlook to revenue of $6.2-$6.45 billion from $6.15-$6.4 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $1.9-$2.05 billion while holding free cash flow guidance at $1.0-$1.2 billion, pointing to a seventh consecutive month of audience growth, viewership up 14% year over year, more than 120 million monthly viewers across its brands, nearly 3 billion combined YouTube and TikTok views year to date, and the No. 1 news organization ranking on YouTube in June. Shares jumped on the report, rising roughly 6% to as much as 12% intraday to about $40.10, while the board declared a $0.375 quarterly dividend, completed a $100 million accelerated share repurchase and planned another $100 million buyback in the third quarter.

‘Ted Lasso’ Is a Leadership Masterclass
This is a feature/advice column with no underlying hard-news event, pegged to the return of Apple TV's "Ted Lasso," whose 10-episode fourth season premiered Aug. 5, 2026 with weekly Wednesday releases running through the Oct. 7 finale and Ted now coaching a second-division women's club at Richmond. The management ideas the column draws on map onto established research: Harvard Business School's Amy Edmondson defines psychological safety as the belief that one will not be punished or humiliated for speaking up with ideas, questions, concerns or mistakes, and her Fearless Organization survey scores teams on willingness to help, inclusion and diversity, attitude to risk and failure, and open conversation. Google's Project Aristotle research on team effectiveness repeatedly identified psychological safety as the strongest differentiator of high-performing teams, which is the mechanism behind the show's emphasis on curiosity over judgment and a leader modeling vulnerability. Recognition research points the same direction: Gallup's 2022-2024 longitudinal work found employees who felt well recognized were 45% less likely to have left their employer after two years. The takeaway consistently drawn by HR practitioners — the SHRM annual conference has run sessions built explicitly on the show — is that Lasso's approach is a memorable packaging of documented findings, not a substitute for them.

Author Whose $2 Million Book Deal Was Derailed by AI Concerns Says He’s Innocent
Debut novelist Jerry Falade lost a book deal reported at roughly $2 million to $2.4 million with Macmillan's Minotaur imprint after allegations that his manuscript involved generative AI, and in early August 2026 he publicly denied the claims and said he had retained legal counsel. His thriller "Call Me, I'll Hide the Body," slated for 2028 publication, had drawn a 14-way auction among major U.S. publishers before his literary agency withdrew the book and dropped him following a July 29, 2026 meeting, saying that aspects of his account changed and that it could no longer "authenticate how the manuscript fully evolved from origin to completion." Notably the withdrawal was initiated by the agents rather than the publisher, and no public forensic proof of AI authorship was disclosed. Falade says he used AI only for research, has posted 2021-dated texts and emails about the book — predating ChatGPT's November 2022 launch — and has argued the scrutiny was racially inflected, noting that three Black authors who landed major deals in 2026 saw them cancelled or disrupted over AI suspicions. The case has become a flashpoint because publishing still has no accepted method for verifying human authorship, leaving contract clauses and agent judgment as the only enforcement.
