**8. Move 37 Is the Moment AI Changes Everything. It’s Suddenly Happening Everywhere.** — Move 37 refers to AlphaGo's stone placement in Game 2 against Lee Sedol in Seoul on March 10, 2016, a move human commentators initially read as a mistake but which sat in exactly the right spot roughly 100 moves later to win the game; DeepMind's own models put the odds of a human playing it at about one in ten thousand, and more than 200 million people watched the match. A decade on the phrase has become shorthand for AI producing results its creators did not anticipate, and 2026 supplied a run of them: DeepMind's AlphaEvolve found a way to multiply 4x4 complex-valued matrices with 48 scalar multiplications, breaking Volker Strassen's 1969 record of 49, and across 67 open mathematics problems it improved 23 and matched the best known result on 36. UCLA's Ernest Ryu settled Nesterov's 1983 convergence conjecture in October 2025 after 42 years using ChatGPT across roughly 12 hours over three days; in January 2026 a group including Jordan Ellenberg, Nicolás Libedinsky, David Plaza, José Simental and Geordie Williamson used AI assistance to uncover hypercube structure in Bruhat intervals of permutation groups; and in the February 2026 First Proof challenge models solved more than half of ten research-level problems. The same lineage produced AlphaFold2, which resolved the 50-year protein-folding problem, released structures for all 200 million known proteins, is used by more than 3 million researchers, and won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Skeptics including Akshay Venkatesh and AMS president Ravi Vakil note the models mostly clear a backlog of tractable open problems and raise unresolved questions about attribution, while Toronto's Daniel Litt has argued the shift may prove larger than the arrival of the computer itself. *Sources: [Quanta Magazine](https://www.quantamagazine.org/the-ai-revolution-in-math-has-arrived-20260413/), [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/10-years-of-alphago/).*
