Why Big Tech's AI Spending Is $3 Trillion Higher Than It Seems** — An analysis of securities-filing footnotes published in mid-August 2026 found that nine large technology companies — including Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD and SpaceX — carry roughly $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments tied mostly to AI, split between about $1.2 trillion in leases that have not yet commenced and roughly $1.9 trillion in purchase commitments for chips, data-center construction and energy. That figure is about five times the roughly $600 billion in conventional capital expenditures the group reported over the trailing year, and roughly triple what the same companies owe under booked leases and long-term borrowings combined. Alphabet disclosed $811 billion in purchase and contractual commitments as of June 30, 2026; Meta reported $347 billion in leases yet to commence, with total off-balance-sheet obligations near $420 billion — nearly three times its reported debt; Oracle's commitments stand near $273 billion, up more than 30-fold in four years. None of this is improper accounting: under current standards, unstarted leases and purchase commitments are not booked as liabilities until the underlying asset or service goes live. The disclosures have drawn attention from bearish investors including Michael Burry, who argue the obligations are compounding faster than reported capex and understate the AI buildout's true leverage. *Sources: [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/alphabet-meta-microsoft-hiding-3-135343885.html), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/big-tech-3-trillion-off-balance-sheet-ai-commitments/), [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324721/20260817/metas-balance-sheet-hides-420b-off-balance-sheet-ai-debt-ey-flagged-largest-structure.htm).*
