**1. When Your Bumper 401(k) Is a Tax Problem Waiting to Happen** *(advice — underlying topic)* — Under current IRS rules, required minimum distributions (RMDs) from traditional 401(k)s and IRAs begin at age 73 (rising to 75 for those born in 1960 or later), calculated by dividing the prior year-end balance by an IRS life-expectancy factor. A large pre-tax balance can force sizable withdrawals that push retirees into higher tax brackets, increase taxation of Social Security benefits, and trigger Medicare IRMAA surcharges; missing an RMD carries a penalty of up to 25% (10% if corrected within two years) under the SECURE 2.0 Act. Common mitigation strategies include Roth conversions in lower-income early retirement years and Qualified Charitable Distributions, which let those 70½ and older send up to an inflation-indexed $111,000 per person in 2026 directly from an IRA to charity tax-free while still counting toward the RMD. *Sources: [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plan-and-ira-required-minimum-distributions-faqs), [Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/required-minimum-distributions-what-you-should-know).*
