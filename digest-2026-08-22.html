California Draws More Startup Investment Than All Other 49 States Combined
California-based startups have raised roughly $366 billion in venture capital so far in 2026, according to PitchBook data, more than triple the combined total of every other U.S. state and nearly double California's own 2025 record. New York ranks a distant second with about $27 billion. More than 4,000 California-based startups took in funding, and just two companies — OpenAI and Anthropic — accounted for roughly half of the total, underscoring how concentrated the AI boom has become.
TECH
Why a Payments Giant Is Paying $7 Billion for the 'Stripe of AI'
Stripe has finalized a deal to acquire OpenRouter, a New York-based AI "model router" that gives developers a single access point to more than 400 AI models, for more than $7 billion. The price is roughly 5.4 times OpenRouter's $1.3 billion valuation from its Series B round just three months earlier, in May 2026. OpenRouter CEO Alex Atallah has compared his company's role in AI to Stripe's role in payments, the origin of the "Stripe of AI" nickname; the deal gives Stripe a stronger foothold in AI infrastructure as businesses seek cheaper ways to route between models.
TECH
Exclusive | OpenAI's Second-Quarter Sales Show Tepid Growth Compared With Anthropic
OpenAI reported second-quarter revenue of $6.7 billion, up 18% from $5.7 billion in Q1, while its operating loss widened to $12.3 billion from $9.3 billion. Anthropic more than doubled its revenue to $11.6 billion over the same period, surpassing OpenAI in quarterly sales for the first time and posting a small adjusted operating profit of $559 million. Analysts point to decelerating ChatGPT consumer growth and the breakout popularity of Anthropic's Claude Code among enterprise customers as key drivers of the divergence.
TECH
The Brains Who Powered China's Surprising AI Leap
China's AI talent market has tightened sharply, with AI-engineer job applications up nearly 70% in the first week of the 2026 spring recruiting season alone. DeepSeek, which shipped DeepSeek V4 between April and June 2026 alongside rivals MiniMax M3, Kimi K2.7-Code and GLM-5.2, reportedly required a "no poaching" pledge from investors ahead of a $7.4 billion first external fundraising round, reflecting how fiercely Chinese labs are competing to retain researchers. The company recruits heavily from top domestic universities and increasingly from outside traditional computer-science backgrounds to broaden its models' capabilities.
TECH
Chinese Humanoid Robot Leader Soars in Market Debut Despite U.S. Ban
Hangzhou-based Unitree priced its IPO at a $9 billion valuation and shares surged in their debut on Shanghai's STAR Market on August 18, 2026, days after the U.S. banned imports of new foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robot models over national-security concerns. The ban only covers unreleased future models — Unitree's existing robots retain U.S. approval — but the company was separately added to a Pentagon blacklist of Chinese military-linked firms in June 2026. Rivals AgiBot (planning a Hong Kong listing valued up to $6.4 billion) and Leju Robotics and DEEP Robotics (both pursuing mainland IPOs) are lining up to follow.
TECH
OpenAI's Latest Bid to Fight Anthropic: A Promise Not to Keep Customer Data
OpenAI unveiled "Private Safety Processing," a system that scans customer model interactions for safety risks without violating its Zero Data Retention (ZDR) commitments, testing it with Databricks and Microsoft ahead of a wider September 2026 release. The move is aimed squarely at Anthropic, which has acknowledged that its own ZDR implementation for commercial customers using Claude retains prompts and outputs for 30 days to support safety work as of June 9, 2026. OpenAI says its approach lets eligible ZDR customers use frontier models without OpenAI retaining prompts or responses after processing.
TECH
Amazon to Expand Drone Delivery Service to Nearly 500 Locales
Amazon plans to expand Prime Air drone delivery to nearly 500 U.S. cities and towns by the end of 2026, roughly a sixfold jump from the 11 metro areas across seven states where it currently operates. Upcoming launch markets include Chicago, Atlanta, Cleveland, Syracuse and Boise, with drones delivering packages up to 5 pounds — covering more than 60% of Amazon's most commonly ordered items — in as fast as 30 minutes. A top Amazon executive projected the service will complete 1 million deliveries this year, with Prime members getting free delivery on orders over $50.
MARKETS
U.S. Stocks Rise as Bitcoin Nears $80,000
Bitcoin briefly topped $80,000 for the first time since mid-May 2026, capping its biggest weekly rally in more than three years with roughly a 23% weekly gain and about 27% over the prior month. The rebound came as traders assessed the fallout from a spike in Treasury yields and the government's new fiscal-consolidation push, forcing the unwinding of billions of dollars in leveraged short bets. Crypto-linked stocks rallied alongside it — Coinbase and Strategy each gained around 5%, Robinhood jumped over 7%, and miners Riot, Mara and Hut 8 all advanced — with some analysts now eyeing $100,000 by year-end.
MARKETS
How Index Funds Went From Being Mocked to Feared in 50 Years
(analysis) August 31, 2026 marks the 50th anniversary of Vanguard founder Jack Bogle launching the first publicly available index mutual fund tracking the S&P 500 in 1976, an idea initially derided by active managers as "Bogle's folly." Passively managed U.S. fund assets have now overtaken actively managed assets, a crossover that occurred around 2024, fundamentally reshaping how trillions of dollars are invested. Vanguard itself has since faced SEC scrutiny over its scale and influence as index funds' outsized voting power in corporate America has become a subject of regulatory and academic debate.
MARKETS
U.S. Stocks Slide as Bond Yields Rebound; Walmart Disappoints
Walmart shares had their worst day in years, dropping roughly 8.6% after U.S. comparable sales missed analyst expectations and the company cut its adjusted earnings outlook for both the third quarter and full year, citing shoppers pulling back amid rising gas prices. The Dow fell about 704 points (1.32%), the S&P 500 lost 0.87% and the Nasdaq dropped 1% on August 20, 2026, as Treasury yields continued climbing even after the Treasury Department's debt-buyback announcement, and rallying oil prices added to inflation worries.
MARKETS
Here's the Real Problem With Stock Buybacks
(analysis) S&P 500 companies spent over $1.1 trillion on share repurchases in 2025, a record, with the buyback boom continuing into 2026 even as companies with the most aggressive buyback programs have lagged the broader index during the AI-driven rally. Critics, including researchers at Oxfam America, argue buybacks function as financial engineering that flatters per-share earnings without reflecting underlying business fundamentals, disproportionately benefiting wealthy shareholders and executives whose pay is tied to stock performance. The concentration of buyback activity has also raised concerns among analysts about market stability if corporate cash piles shrink or repurchase taxes rise.
MARKETS
The Treasury Market's Coveted Status as a Safe Haven Is Fading
(analysis) Foreign ownership of marketable U.S. Treasuries has fallen from nearly 50% in 2008 to roughly 30% in early 2026 as China, Japan and other major holders reduce exposure amid geopolitical tensions and mounting U.S. debt loads. Since a conflict escalation in February 2026, 10-year yields climbed from about 3.97% to over 4.40% rather than falling as they historically would during stress, and the IMF's spring 2026 Fiscal Monitor warned that rising debt-to-GDP is eroding the "exorbitant privilege" underpinning U.S. borrowing costs. The buyer base is shifting toward domestic institutions and the Federal Reserve, leaving a less diversified, more price-sensitive market.
MARKETS
Bond Yields Dive After Treasury Increases Buybacks
The U.S. Treasury surprised markets on August 19, 2026 by announcing it would more than double its longer-dated bond buybacks to at least $4 billion per operation starting September 9, after the 30-year yield hit a 19-year high near 5.34%. The 10-year yield initially fell more than 5 basis points to 4.647% and the 30-year dropped 9 basis points to 5.196% on the news, though gains proved short-lived as yields largely reversed within 24 hours. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC buybacks "could be more than $4 billion per issue" and may later tap the department's nearly $1 trillion General Account for additional firepower, while billionaire investor Stanley Druckenmiller argued the move undermines Treasury market credibility.
MARKETS
Novonesis Leads Europe's Stoxx 600 on Sales Beat, Guidance Raise
Shares in Danish biosolutions maker Novonesis jumped about 9% on August 20, 2026 after first-half organic sales growth of 8% beat expectations, prompting management to raise full-year organic growth guidance to a 7%-8% range from 5%-7% previously. The company also said adjusted EBITDA margin should land at the higher end of its 37%-38% target and announced its first-ever share buyback, a €1 billion multi-year program set to run through 2029. The results and buyback announcement made Novonesis one of the top-performing stocks on the pan-European benchmark that day.
FINANCE
Gen Z Has Just Discovered the Premium Rewards Credit Card
Gen Z and millennials accounted for 65% of American Express's new consumer accounts globally last quarter and more than three-quarters of its U.S. premium card sign-ups, with the average new Amex Gold cardholder now 29 years old and the average new Platinum holder 33. This adoption comes even as annual fees have jumped sharply: the Chase Sapphire Reserve's fee rose 44.5% to $795 in 2025 (from $550), while the Amex Platinum's climbed to $895 from $695. Issuers are offsetting the higher costs with experience-based perks — Sapphire Reserve's exclusive Sundance Film Festival packages and Amex's VIP access to Formula 1 races and Coachella — alongside expanded statement credits.
FINANCE
A Breakdown of the Financial Advice That's Flying All Over TikTok
(analysis) A 2026 review found 71% of financial advice aimed at Gen Z across TikTok, YouTube and Instagram is inaccurate or incomplete, with TikTok flagged as the most problematic platform; separately, DayTrading.com's Finance TikTok Report Card found 80% of viral finance videos scored a C grade or below, with risk-disclosure failures doubling from 30% to 60% year over year. More than 70% of creators posting financial content lacked a clear, relevant background in financial services, prompting experts to recommend viewers check for disclosed credentials, contact information and conflicts of interest before following investment tips. Budgeting, debt management and emergency-savings content were flagged as generally more reliable than investment-specific advice.
FINANCE
Exclusive | U.S. Workers Are Paying More for Healthcare, and Next Year Will Be Worse
Employer healthcare costs are projected to rise 9.5% in 2027 to over $19,000 per employee, according to Aon, while a separate WTW survey pegs the increase at 11.1% — the steepest jump in 20 years. Two-thirds of large companies with at least 500 employees plan to raise employee premium contributions in 2027, per Mercer, and 48% plan to raise deductibles and copays as well. Workers already saw premiums rise 6.4% in 2026, with average annual worker healthcare spending reaching $5,297 ($3,130 in premiums, $2,167 out of pocket), and group-plan cost growth has now topped 6% for four straight years after hovering near 3% for the prior decade.
FINANCE
Exclusive | Fannie Mae Hit by Turmoil in Senior Ranks as Roughly 12 Executives Are Let Go
Fannie Mae dismissed at least 10 senior executives within a three-hour window on August 19-20, 2026, including chief economist Mark Palim (in the role since September 2024), multifamily COO Chuck Walker, capital-markets SVP Devang Doshi, multifamily CFO Brian Hansen, and low-income housing tax credit VP Dana Brown. The cuts spanned multifamily lending, tax-credit investment, finance, regulatory affairs and communications, and were reportedly attributed in part to work being shifted to artificial intelligence. It's the latest wave of turnover under FHFA Director and Fannie Mae board chairman Bill Pulte, after the company shed roughly 1,200 employees — about 15% of its workforce — during 2025.
FINANCE
Long-Term Yields Fall on Treasury Move While Short-Term Holds Up
Following the Treasury's August 19, 2026 announcement doubling long-dated debt buybacks to at least $4 billion per operation, the 10-year yield fell over 5 basis points to 4.647% and the 30-year "long bond" tumbled 9 basis points to 5.196%, while the 2-year note held closer to steady. The 30-year had touched a 19-year high just before the announcement. CNBC reported the buybacks eased long-term yields somewhat but analysts see only limited, temporary relief given the scale of ongoing Treasury issuance and persistent inflation and fiscal concerns.
FINANCE
Bonds Are Getting Hammered, and Wall Street Says the Rout Won't End Anytime Soon
The 30-year Treasury yield spiked to as high as 5.337% on August 18, 2026, its highest intraday level since 2007, before the government's buyback announcement pulled long-dated yields back from those multi-year highs. Analysts cite persistent inflation worries tied to rising oil prices, heavy federal borrowing, and geopolitical risk as reasons the selloff could continue, with higher yields already pushing up mortgage, auto-loan and credit-card borrowing costs for consumers. CNBC noted the bond-market pressure is "squeezing Main Street" even as markets wait on signals from the Federal Reserve.
FINANCE
'Ghost Job' Ads Are Getting So Bad That Lawmakers Want to Ban Them
At least five state legislatures are advancing bills to crack down on "ghost jobs" — postings for positions that aren't actively being filled — with roughly 19% of online job listings showing no real hiring activity behind them, according to reporting cited by lawmakers. New York's S8877, which passed June 2, 2026, requires employers with 100+ workers to disclose expected fill dates and remove listings within two weeks of being filled; Pennsylvania's HB2321 (the Ghost Job Postings Prevention Act) would require salary ranges, hiring timelines and disclosure of AI use in hiring, with similar bills pending in New Jersey, California and Kentucky. Members of Congress are also exploring federal legislation, while Ontario, Canada has already enacted similar disclosure rules set to take effect in 2026.
