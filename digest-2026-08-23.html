Nvidia Is Spending $6 Billion to Build a Powerful U.S. Alternative to Chinese AI
Nvidia has struck a roughly $6 billion deal with AI startup Poolside, investing about $1 billion directly in the company while paying the rest to license its technology and hire many of its top engineers, as it races to build an open-weight U.S. model that can compete with China's DeepSeek, Kimi K2 and Qwen. The move marks Nvidia's push beyond chipmaking and into the AI model layer itself, using cash from its chip dominance at a moment when Chinese open-weight models are becoming harder for the U.S. industry to ignore.
TECH
The Brains Who Powered China's Surprising AI Leap
(analysis) Research from the Hoover Institution and Stanford HAI on DeepSeek's talent base finds that 53.5% of its researchers are "homegrown," trained entirely at Chinese institutions, while a smaller elite cohort — 80 researchers with U.S. institutional experience — is highly cited (averaging 4,108 citations) and 71% of them have returned to China after working or studying in the U.S. DeepSeek's broader research pool expanded from 223 to 356 named researchers between earlier counts and a June 2026 update, with median citations per researcher rising from 249 to 681, underscoring how much of China's AI edge rests on both domestic training pipelines and reverse brain drain from the U.S.
TECH
The Hidden Debt That Apple Owes to the CIA
(analysis) Multiple foundational iPhone technologies trace back to government-funded research, including Siri, which grew out of a DARPA-backed "Cognitive Assistant that Learns and Organizes" (CALO) project spanning 20 universities before SRI spun out a startup that Apple acquired in 2010. The iPhone's multitouch screen was developed by University of Delaware researchers with grants from the National Science Foundation and the CIA, while GPS came from the military's 1980s NAVSTAR satellite program and lithium-ion batteries trace to Department of Energy research — illustrating how much of Apple's signature hardware and software rests on decades of public research funding rather than in-house invention alone.

Why a Payments Giant Is Paying $7 Billion for the 'Stripe of AI'
Stripe has finalized a deal worth more than $7 billion to acquire OpenRouter, a startup that lets companies route requests across different AI models, in an announcement that came together around August 16-19, 2026. The price is more than five times OpenRouter's roughly $1.3 billion valuation from a funding round just months earlier, reflecting surging enterprise demand to compare and switch between AI models for cost and performance, and gives Stripe a deeper foothold in AI infrastructure beyond its core payments business.
TECH
Justice Department, TikTok Reach $400 Million Settlement Over Children's Privacy Suit
The Justice Department, TikTok and parent ByteDance reached a $400 million settlement on August 21, 2026 resolving a 2024 lawsuit alleging violations of the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) for allegedly letting kids join the app and collecting their data unlawfully. TikTok will pay $300 million immediately and an additional $100 million once a prior consent decree against its Musical.ly predecessor is vacated, making it one of the largest recoveries ever in a COPPA case; the company says it has since strengthened age verification and parental controls.
TECH
California Draws More Startup Investment Than All Other 49 States Combined
California startups pulled in about $366 billion in venture capital in 2026, according to the governor's office, nearly double the state's previous annual record and more than three times the combined total raised by companies in all other 49 states. More than 4,000 California-based startups accounted for the haul, driven largely by the AI buildout and the state's concentration of AI developers, founders and venture firms.
TECH
Amazon to Expand Drone Delivery Service to Nearly 500 Locales
Amazon plans to expand its Prime Air drone delivery service to nearly 500 U.S. cities and towns by the end of 2026, a roughly sixfold increase from its current 11 sites across 10 metro areas in Arizona, Florida, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, Nebraska and Texas. New markets include Chicago, Syracuse, Cleveland, Atlanta and Boise; the company says it has already delivered hundreds of thousands of packages by drone this year with deliveries in as little as 30 minutes, and a top executive has projected 1 million deliveries in 2026 under its FAA Part 135 air-carrier certificate.
MARKETS
Trump Discloses 1,000 Stock Trades in June
A financial disclosure filed August 22, 2026 shows President Trump's investment accounts made 1,051 transactions in June, worth between $78.1 million and $263.1 million, averaging more than 30 trades a day. Notable moves include selling $1 million-to-$5 million stakes in Meta Platforms and Motorola on June 18 while buying at least $1 million each in Berkshire Hathaway, Visa and Mastercard, plus a June 22 sale of $5 million-to-$25 million in a Vanguard ETF; the White House says the accounts are run by independent managers using computer-based models tracking indexes like the Schwab 1000.
MARKETS
How Index Funds Went From Being Mocked to Feared in 50 Years
(analysis) U.S. index-based long-term funds held $21.82 trillion in net assets as of 2025, or 53.8% of all long-term fund assets, surpassing actively managed funds for the first time; the Passive ETF market overall was valued at $16.8 trillion in 2026. Since 2010, roughly 80% of every dollar invested in U.S. markets has flowed through just three providers — BlackRock, Vanguard and State Street — which together manage more than $20 trillion and are collectively the largest shareholder in nearly 90% of S&P 500 companies, fueling concern that passive concentration is inflating valuations (the top 10 S&P 500 stocks now make up over 40% of the index, versus 19% in 1990).
MARKETS
U.S. Stocks Rise as Bitcoin Nears $80,000
Bitcoin climbed above $80,000 this week for the first time since mid-May, extending a roughly 23-25% seven-day advance that would be its biggest weekly gain since March 2023, driven by the Treasury's announcement that it would at least double long-bond buybacks and by more than $2.2 billion of inflows into spot Bitcoin ETFs since August 17. The rally has pushed crypto-linked stocks like MicroStrategy (MSTR), Coinbase (COIN) and Robinhood (HOOD) to their strongest weekly gains since 2024, with Bitcoin's six-month return of roughly 16-22% nearly doubling the S&P 500's approximately 12% gain over the same stretch.
MARKETS
The Treasury Market's Coveted Status as a Safe Haven Is Fading
(analysis) Foreign ownership of marketable U.S. Treasuries has fallen from nearly 50% in 2008 to roughly 30% in early 2026, with foreign central banks and governments now holding about 23% of outstanding U.S. debt, down from a mid-2010s peak above 34%, as China, Japan and others pare exposure amid rising debt-to-GDP and geopolitical tension. The IMF's spring 2026 Fiscal Monitor warns that mounting U.S. debt is eroding the "exorbitant privilege" underpinning American fiscal policy, and investors are increasingly turning instead to the yen, Swiss franc, gold and short-duration bonds for downside protection during stress periods.
MARKETS
Why Scott Bessent Is Playing With the Treasury Market
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is running an "Operation Twist"-style intervention, buying longer-dated debt while selling short-term bills, and has said the government will at least double the maximum size of its buyback operations — from $2 billion to at least $4 billion — starting September 9 through November, targeting the 10-to-20-year and 20-to-30-year segments that have seen a buyers' strike since late June. Sources say Bessent could also tap nearly $1 trillion in the Treasury General Account to fund additional buybacks, part of a broader effort to push down the 10-year yield and keep markets "focused on the fundamentals," though critics warn the approach could stoke inflation and pressure the Federal Reserve.
MARKETS
U.S. Stocks Slide as Bond Yields Rebound; Walmart Disappoints
U.S. stocks fell on August 20, 2026, with the S&P 500 down 0.9% and the Nasdaq 100 off 0.7% for a fifth straight losing session, as rebounding Treasury yields and rising oil prices weighed on sentiment. Walmart shares dropped 8.6% after the retailer's comparable sales came in at 2.6% — missing expectations — and its 2026 guidance disappointed investors amid consumers pulling back on spending, dragging down rivals including Costco, Dollar Tree and Albertsons.
MARKETS
Inside the Collapse of a MAGA Marketplace Backed by Trump Jr.
PublicSquare, the "anti-woke" e-commerce marketplace on whose board Donald Trump Jr. sits, has racked up nearly $160 million in cumulative losses since its 2023 stock-market debut through July 2026, with shares down about 99% and now facing possible NYSE delisting. The company dismantled its marketplace, laid off 41% of its workforce and canceled its streaming show this year, while Trump Jr. continued collecting $42,000 a month in consulting fees (over $500,000 in 2025) even as the business pivots toward becoming a fintech payments company.
FINANCE
Gen Z Has Just Discovered the Premium Rewards Credit Card
Gen Z and millennials made up 65% of American Express's new consumer accounts globally in the second quarter of 2026, and more than three-quarters of its new U.S. accounts, as premium travel cards go from a niche product for frequent flyers to a lifestyle status symbol for young adults. The Amex Platinum now carries an $895 annual fee and the Chase Sapphire Reserve $795, while Capital One's Venture X undercuts both at $395, with younger cardholders increasingly viewing a premium card as a marker of financial "arrival" after a raise or new job.
FINANCE
A Breakdown of the Financial Advice That's Flying All Over TikTok
(analysis) A 2026 accuracy review found that 60% of viral finance videos on TikTok scored a C or D grade for accuracy and none received an A, with 60% failing to include proper risk disclosures and 74% not disclosing the creator's financial credentials. More than a quarter of adults who followed financial advice from TikTok or Instagram later found the information was false or misleading, according to industry surveys, with the worst offenders concentrated in forex trading, crypto speculation and short-term trading strategies, even as some creators provide legitimate budgeting and debt-management guidance.
FINANCE
U.S. Workers Are Paying More for Healthcare, and Next Year Will Be Worse
Employer healthcare costs are projected to jump 9.5% in 2027 according to consulting firm Aon, pushing average per-employee spending above $19,000, while rival consultancy WTW puts the expected increase even higher at 11.1%; a Mercer survey found two-thirds of large employers (500+ workers) plan to raise employees' premium contributions in 2027. Aon estimates workers will pay an average of $5,297 for healthcare in 2026 alone ($3,130 in payroll contributions plus $2,167 out-of-pocket), with about 48% of employers also planning to raise deductibles and copays, driven by rising chronic-disease rates and costly GLP-1 weight-loss drugs.
FINANCE
Bonds Are Getting Hammered, and Wall Street Says the Rout Won't End Anytime Soon
The 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.34% in mid-August 2026, its highest since 2007, while the 10-year climbed to 4.74%, near the highest level of Trump's second term, as a summer-long selloff steepened the yield curve. In Bloomberg's Markets Pulse survey of 392 investors, two-thirds predicted the 10-year yield will top 5% before year-end, with drivers including Middle East-linked energy prices, heavy AI infrastructure spending and swelling federal deficits, all of which are expected to keep pushing up mortgage, auto-loan and credit-card borrowing costs.
FINANCE
'Ghost Job' Ads Are Getting So Bad That Lawmakers Want to Ban Them
Greenhouse data found that 19% of job postings advertised in the second quarter of 2026 showed no meaningful hiring activity, part of a broader pattern experts say affects over a third of all listings. New York passed Senate Bill S8877 in June 2026 requiring employers with 100+ workers to disclose in postings whether and when they intend to actually fill a role, with fines up to $2,500 per violation, while Pennsylvania's proposed Ghost Job Postings Prevention Act would mandate hiring timelines and salary ranges, and Texas's attorney general has opened an investigation into LinkedIn over allegedly profiting from fake listings.
FINANCE
Is Your Therapist Biased? Personal Agendas Are Creeping Into the Therapy Room
(advice — underlying topic) Mental-health commentators have increasingly flagged that therapists' personal political or ideological views can bleed into sessions — through subtle persuasion, dismissive reactions to a client's stated beliefs, or steering couples and family therapy toward outcomes that reflect the clinician's own values rather than the client's autonomy. A 2026 survey of 218 Jewish mental-health professionals found 74% had personally experienced antisemitism in the field and 91% considered it a professional problem, illustrating how identity-based bias can surface in clinical settings; professional bodies like the American Psychological Association continue to publish guidance on keeping self-disclosure and value judgments from crossing ethical lines.
FINANCE
The WSJ Dollar Index Falls 0.74% This Week to 95.33
(analysis — underlying topic) The broader ICE U.S. Dollar Index (DXY), a comparable gauge of the dollar against major currencies, fell to 98.55 on August 22, 2026, its lowest level since May, after the Treasury's announcement that it would at least double its long-bond buyback program and following a divided 9-3 Federal Reserve vote at the July 29 meeting in which three regional presidents dissented in favor of an immediate rate hike. The dollar's slide came even as U.S. business activity kept expanding, with the euro touching a fresh three-month high near $1.1710 against the dollar and the yen holding near 158-159 per dollar on expectations of a possible September Bank of Japan move.
