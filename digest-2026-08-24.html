Nvidia Is Spending $6 Billion to Build a Powerful U.S. Alternative to Chinese AI
Nvidia is putting roughly $6 billion into AI startup Poolside, including about $1 billion in direct equity, technology access, and the transfer of more than 100 Poolside engineers into Nvidia's own Nemotron open-weight model effort. The move is explicitly aimed at countering the rapid rise of Chinese open-weight models such as DeepSeek, Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, and Alibaba's Qwen, which have been downloaded heavily worldwide. It also marks a strategic shift for Nvidia, using cash from its chip dominance to move up the AI stack and compete more directly with model makers like OpenAI and Anthropic rather than just supplying them hardware.
TECH
Data-Center Disenchantment
(analysis) Global data-center capital expenditure is on track to top $1 trillion in 2026, with the five largest hyperscalers alone projected to spend $775–800 billion on AI infrastructure, yet cracks are showing: three of four major hyperscalers lost market value after their most recent earnings calls as investors balked at the scale of commitments. Analysts note the U.S. economy may already be in a technical recession if AI capex is stripped out, since it has become a primary driver of 2025–2026 GDP growth, employment, and supply-chain activity. The Federal Reserve has flagged systemic risk, warning that a sharp AI capex pullback could ripple through data-center REITs, cloud margins, and chip stocks well beyond the tech sector.

Chinese Chip Maker YMTC Seeks $4.9 Billion IPO
Yangtze Memory Technologies Co. (YMTC) filed to raise 33 billion yuan ($4.9 billion) via an IPO on Shanghai's tech-focused Star Market, planning to issue 2.0–2.4 billion new shares (10–12% of post-offering capital) with a listing targeted for 2027. The Wuhan-based NAND flash maker posted first-quarter revenue of 47.04 billion yuan ($7.0 billion) — already surpassing all of 2024 — with quarterly net profit of 33.38 billion yuan, more than double its full-year 2025 profit. YMTC says it aims to overtake Samsung and SK Hynix to become the world's largest NAND manufacturer by the end of 2027, with proceeds earmarked for production-line upgrades and R&D.

Justice Department, TikTok Reach $400 Million Settlement Over Children's Privacy Suit
The Justice Department announced TikTok and parent company ByteDance will pay $400 million to resolve litigation alleging violations of the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) dating back to 2019, with $300 million paid immediately and $100 million more once a prior consent decree against predecessor app Musical.ly is vacated. The lawsuit, filed in 2024, alleged TikTok collected children's data without parental consent and ignored parent requests to delete accounts of users under 13. DOJ called it one of the largest recoveries ever in a COPPA case, while noting TikTok has since added stronger age-verification and parental-control features.

California Draws More Startup Investment Than All Other 49 States Combined
California-based companies have attracted about $366 billion in venture capital since the start of 2026 — nearly double the state's previous annual record and more than three times the total raised by every other U.S. state combined, per PitchBook data, with California capturing roughly 90% of all U.S. VC this year. New York ranks a distant second with about $27 billion. The concentration is extreme: OpenAI's $122 billion round and Anthropic's $95 billion raised across two rounds together account for roughly half of California's total, even as more than 4,000 California startups raised capital in 2026.

The Brains Who Powered China's Surprising AI Leap
(analysis) Chinese AI labs like DeepSeek have drawn heavily on homegrown talent from elite universities such as Tsinghua and Peking University, with research showing 53.5% of DeepSeek's researchers built their entire careers at Chinese institutions. Of the roughly 80 researchers with U.S. institutional experience, about 71% have since returned to China, including engineers who previously worked at Nvidia and Microsoft. DeepSeek founder Liang Wenfeng himself studied exclusively in China, at Zhejiang University, underscoring how domestic technical training — not just returnees from Silicon Valley — has fueled China's AI advances.

Private Equity Is Deploying an Army of AI Wonks to Embed in the Firms They Back
Private equity firms are increasingly placing dedicated AI specialists inside portfolio companies, exemplified by Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman's roughly $1.5 billion partnership with Anthropic to build a roughly 160-person AI expert team, and a competing venture backed by a $4 billion investment led by PE firm TPG partnering with OpenAI. KPMG's 2026 PE survey found data scientists and AI specialists on 51% of PE firms' active recruiting lists, and the "AI operating partner" has become a fast-growing role. FTI Consulting's 2026 Private Equity AI Radar, surveying 200 fund and operating leaders, found 95% report their AI initiatives meeting or exceeding their original business case.

Alibaba to Raise $10.20 Billion for AI Investment With Share Placement
Alibaba priced a HK$80 billion ($10.2 billion) Hong Kong share placement — the largest primary follow-on offering ever by a Hong Kong-listed company and the world's third-largest this year behind only Alphabet and Intel — issuing 710 million new shares at HK$112.70 each (about 3.57% of enlarged share capital). The company said 100% of net proceeds will fund its "full stack" AI push across chips, infrastructure, and model development, with the deal closing August 26 after banks reported orders exceeding the offering size from sovereign wealth funds. Despite the strong demand, Alibaba shares plunged roughly 10% after pricing on dilution concerns.

Trump Discloses 1,000 Stock Trades in June
A financial disclosure published in late August showed President Trump's accounts made more than 1,000 securities trades in June 2026, including sizable purchases of Berkshire Hathaway, Visa, Mastercard, and Cintas. It follows earlier 2026 disclosures showing between $220 million and $750 million in first-quarter transactions across companies including Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, Broadcom, Bank of America, and Goldman Sachs — an average of roughly 50 trades per trading day. The White House says the accounts are managed by professional advisers with no regular contact with the president, but lawmakers have flagged more than a dozen instances where purchases preceded favorable government announcements.

How Index Funds Went From Being Mocked to Feared in 50 Years
(analysis) Vanguard launched the first index fund for individual investors in 1976, once derided as "Bogle's Folly," but passive vehicles now represent roughly half of U.S. equity fund assets — 53.8% of domestic equity assets are passively managed — and the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF became the first ETF to cross $1 trillion in assets. That dominance has flipped the narrative from mockery to alarm: because a handful of mega-cap stocks now drive "broad market" indexes, researchers warn passive funds could be forced into unison selling during downturns, amplifying volatility and posing financial-stability risks that regulators are only beginning to study.

The Wild Week When Scott Bessent Was Schooled by the Bond Market
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expanded the government's long-term debt buyback program, raising the maximum purchase from $2 billion to at least $4 billion, in an effort to tamp down a bond sell-off that had pushed 30-year Treasury yields to their highest since the financial crisis. The intervention barely worked: the 30-year yield rebounded to 5.247% within 24 hours, erasing the buyback's gains, as the Treasury's push for lower long-term yields collided with the Fed's separate inflation fight. Analysts likened the maneuver to a modern "Operation Twist," buying long bonds while issuing more short-term debt, with mixed results for market confidence.

Samsung Plans Biggest-Ever Buyback Amid AI Windfall
Samsung Electronics plans to return up to 110 trillion won ($80 billion) to shareholders in 2026 — five times its previous record of 20.3 trillion won set in 2020 — including 30 trillion won in third-quarter dividends and roughly 15 trillion won in buybacks for employee compensation, giving away about half its free cash flow. The payout follows rival SK Hynix's own record 40 trillion won ($28.7 billion) buyback-and-cancellation program, as both Korean chipmakers cash in on soaring demand for HBM4 memory used in Nvidia's Vera Rubin AI accelerators. The contrasting approaches — Samsung favoring dividends, SK Hynix favoring buybacks — reflect their different ownership structures.

Inside the Collapse of a MAGA Marketplace Backed by Trump Jr.
PublicSquare, the "MAGA alternative to Amazon" that Donald Trump Jr. helped launch at the NYSE in 2023, has racked up nearly $160 million in cumulative losses while its stock has cratered roughly 99% from post-listing highs, prompting the NYSE to warn of possible delisting. The company abandoned its e-commerce marketplace this year in favor of financial technology, cutting 41% of its workforce and canceling a streaming program, after the underlying anti-"woke" business model lost relevance once Trump embraced the same tech companies he once criticized. Despite the collapse, Trump Jr. was paid $42,000 a month in consulting fees since 2024 — more than $500,000 in 2025 alone — along with payments to allies Nick Ayers and Omeed Malik's firm.

U.S. Stocks Slide as Bond Yields Rebound; Walmart Disappoints
The Dow fell nearly 700 points (1.3%), the S&P 500 slid 0.8%, and the Nasdaq dropped 1% as Treasury's buyback-driven yield relief evaporated and oil prices climbed on renewed Iran sanctions threats. Walmart shares lost 8.6% after posting its weakest revenue growth in over six years, with U.S. comparable sales up just 2.6% versus the 3.8% expected, as shoppers pulled back amid rising gas prices. The weakness dragged down the S&P consumer staples sector and spread to Costco, Amazon, and Home Depot, while the 30-year Treasury yield climbed back to 5.238%.

When Your Bumper 401(k) Is a Tax Problem Waiting to Happen
(advice — underlying topic) Large 401(k)/IRA balances create outsized Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) once savers reach age 73, and because the IRS's life-expectancy divisor shrinks with age, RMDs grow as a share of the account even as the balance declines — stacking on top of Social Security and other income and potentially triggering higher Medicare premiums (IRMAA) and taxes on Social Security benefits. Common mitigation strategies include Roth conversions before age 73 to shrink the pretax balance driving future RMDs, and Qualified Charitable Distributions of up to $105,000 per year sent directly from an IRA to charity tax-free (available from IRAs but not directly from 401(k)s, prompting many savers to roll employer plans into IRAs first).

Gen Z Has Just Discovered the Premium Rewards Credit Card
(advice — underlying topic) Gen Z and millennials made up 65% of American Express's new global consumer accounts and more than three-quarters of its new U.S. accounts in the second quarter of 2026, driving demand for premium travel cards like the $895-a-year Amex Platinum, the $795-a-year Chase Sapphire Reserve, and the cheaper $395-a-year Capital One Venture X. Annual fees on these cards have jumped sharply in the past year — Sapphire Reserve's rose by nearly $250 and Amex Platinum's by $200 — as issuers lean into experiential perks like Amex's "Platinum Houses" and Chase's Sapphire Reserve Trips to court younger, high-credit-score customers.

U.S. Workers Are Paying More for Healthcare, and Next Year Will Be Worse
Mercer's survey found employers expect health-benefit costs to rise 6.7% in 2026, pushing average per-employee costs above $18,500 — the steepest projected increase in 15 years — driven largely by a 9.4% jump in prescription drug spending among large employers. Looking ahead, two-thirds of large companies plan to raise employee premium contributions in 2027, and 48% plan to raise deductibles and copays, marking a fourth consecutive year of 6%-plus cost growth; more employers are also tightening or dropping coverage for GLP-1 weight-loss drugs.

Is Your Therapist Biased? Personal Agendas Are Creeping Into the Therapy Room
(advice — underlying topic) Mental-health professionals and ethicists have raised growing concern that therapists' personal political and social views are shaping treatment, with clients increasingly bringing polarized political distress into sessions; one 2026 survey of 218 Jewish mental-health professionals found 74% reported experiencing antisemitism and 91% considered it a problem in the field. Ethics commentators note the clinical risk isn't whether politics comes up in therapy but whether a clinician's own ideology — progressive or conservative — starts overriding a client's autonomy and flattening their experience into a predetermined narrative, and professional counseling bodies have been publishing updated ethical guidance on maintaining neutrality in 2026.

Private-Equity Firms Look for an Exit Three Years After Buying EverBank
A five-firm consortium — Stone Point Capital, Warburg Pincus, Reverence Capital Partners, Sixth Street, and Bayview Asset Management — that bought EverBank from TIAA in 2023 is exploring a sale of the digital bank, which had about $47 billion in assets as of June 2026, with an IPO floated as a fallback if no buyer emerges. The sale process, reportedly underway for months, comes amid reported tensions among the five sponsors over the bank's strategic direction; EverBank operates mostly online but keeps several dozen branches concentrated in Florida and California.
FINANCE
'Ghost Job' Ads Are Getting So Bad That Lawmakers Want to Ban Them
State legislators in Pennsylvania (HB2321), New Jersey (S2136), New York (S8877/S9208), California (AB1251), and Kentucky (HB324) have introduced bills this year to curb "ghost job" postings for roles that don't really exist, with surveys finding roughly 40% of companies admit to posting fake listings and about 19% of online job ads showing no real hiring activity. New York's legislature has already passed a bill requiring employers to disclose hiring timelines or face fines, awaiting Governor Kathy Hochul's signature, while Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton has opened an investigation into LinkedIn over allegedly profiting from fake job ads; members of Congress are also weighing federal legislation.
FINANCE
Treasury Yields Rise Despite Increased Buyback Plans
Despite Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expanding long-bond buybacks to as much as $4 billion to relieve upward pressure on rates, the 30-year Treasury yield rebounded to 5.247% within a day, near its highest level since the financial crisis — a setback with direct consequences for household finances, since Treasury yields feed through to mortgage rates, auto loans, and savings-account yields. The episode highlighted a policy tension between Treasury's push for lower long-term borrowing costs and the Federal Reserve's separate fight against inflation, leaving consumers facing continued high borrowing costs even as short-term savings rates stay elevated.
