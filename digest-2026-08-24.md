**1. When Your Bumper 401(k) Is a Tax Problem Waiting to Happen** *(advice — underlying topic)* — Large 401(k)/IRA balances create outsized Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) once savers reach age 73, and because the IRS's life-expectancy divisor shrinks with age, RMDs grow as a share of the account even as the balance declines — stacking on top of Social Security and other income and potentially triggering higher Medicare premiums (IRMAA) and taxes on Social Security benefits. Common mitigation strategies include Roth conversions before age 73 to shrink the pretax balance driving future RMDs, and Qualified Charitable Distributions of up to $105,000 per year sent directly from an IRA to charity tax-free (available from IRAs but not directly from 401(k)s, prompting many savers to roll employer plans into IRAs first). *Sources: [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-required-minimum-distributions-rmds), [Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/required-minimum-distributions-what-you-should-know).*
