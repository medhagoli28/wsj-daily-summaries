Three Takeaways From Bill Gates's 5,784-Word Warning on AI: 'There Is No Plan'
(advice — underlying topic) In a nearly 6,000-word essay published in late August 2026, Bill Gates warned that "there is no plan to ease the entry into the AI era" and that the transition could be "one of the most turbulent times in human history." Gates argued AI's threat to jobs differs from past technological shifts because it targets cognitive work directly rather than over generations, and could squeeze entry-level hiring while straining education and safety-net systems. He called for new national and international institutions to coordinate AI safeguards, framing AI as either "the greatest equalizer ever invented" or "the worst source of injustice."

(analysis — underlying topic) The underlying story is a standoff between Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh over long-term borrowing costs: the 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.34% in mid-August 2026, its highest since 2007, as investors weighed persistent inflation, ballooning federal debt, and competition from AI-buildout corporate debt for investor demand. Ditching SpaceX Didn't Help You Beat the Market

When Your Bumper 401(k) Is a Tax Problem Waiting to Happen
(advice — underlying topic) Large 401(k) balances can create outsized Required Minimum Distributions once savers hit age 73 (or 75 for those born in 1960 or later), calculated by dividing the prior year-end balance by an IRS-assigned distribution period — and bigger RMDs can push retirees into higher tax brackets, raise Medicare premiums, and increase taxation of Social Security benefits. Common mitigation strategies include Qualified Charitable Distributions of up to $105,000 a year tax-free to charity, Roth conversions before age 73, and QLACs, which in 2026 can shelter up to the lesser of $200,000 or 25% of a retirement account from RMD calculations. Some savers also use the "still-working exception" by rolling IRA funds into a current employer's 401(k) to delay RMDs.

Uncle Sam's $40 Trillion Tab Is Living Rent-Free in Your Mortgage Payment
(analysis) With U.S. national debt surpassing $40 trillion and the CBO projecting a $1.9 trillion fiscal 2026 deficit (5.8% of GDP), a Conference Board report warns rising government borrowing is pushing up the 10-year Treasury yield that mortgage, student loan and small-business rates are priced off of. The report estimates that under a higher-deficit path, a home purchased in 2031 could cost about $53,000 more in total payments over 30 years than under a lower-deficit scenario, while retirees could feel roughly $700 a month in added costs. CBO projects publicly held debt will climb from 101% of GDP in 2026 to 120% by 2036, with net interest costs alone approaching $1 trillion this year.

FDA Approves Alzheimer's Blood Test Developed by Roche and Eli Lilly
The FDA cleared Roche and Eli Lilly's Elecsys pTau217 blood test on August 24, 2026, for identifying Alzheimer's-related brain changes in cognitively declining patients aged 55 and older, offering an alternative to costly, invasive PET scans and spinal taps. The test can run on more than 4,500 Roche lab analyzers already installed across the U.S., and Labcorp and Quest Diagnostics both said they plan to add it to their networks, potentially broadening access quickly. The clearance follows the test's approval in Europe in May 2026.

NHTSA Expands Investigation Into GM Braking System
NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation upgraded its probe of GM's eBoost brake system to a formal engineering analysis on August 24, 2026, covering an estimated 1.16 million vehicles across 14 model lines from the 2023-2026 model years, including the Cadillac Lyriq, Chevrolet Equinox EV and Blazer EV, and GMC Acadia, plus Honda Prologue and Acura ZDX models that share the GM-supplied system. The investigation, opened April 11, 2026 after complaints about lost brake assist in 2023 Lyriqs, has logged 745 unique failure reports, 22 crashes or fires, and six injuries. It is an engineering analysis, not a recall, and GM stock held steady on the news.

The Wild Week When Scott Bessent Was Schooled by the Bond Market
(analysis) Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent tried to cap long-term borrowing costs in mid-August 2026 after the 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.34%, its highest since 2007, by boosting Treasury's long-bond buyback ceiling from $2 billion to at least $4 billion.
