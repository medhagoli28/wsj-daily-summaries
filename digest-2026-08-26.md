When Your Bumper 401(k) Is a Tax Problem Waiting to Happen** *(advice — underlying topic)* — Large 401(k) balances can create outsized Required Minimum Distributions once savers hit age 73 (or 75 for those born in 1960 or later), calculated by dividing the prior year-end balance by an IRS-assigned distribution period — and bigger RMDs can push retirees into higher tax brackets, raise Medicare premiums, and increase taxation of Social Security benefits. Some savers also use the "still-working exception" by rolling IRA funds into a current employer's 401(k) to delay RMDs. *Sources: [Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/required-minimum-distributions-what-you-should-know), [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-required-minimum-distributions-rmds).*
