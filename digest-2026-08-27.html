Nvidia's $279 Billion Supply-Chain Gamble
Nvidia disclosed that its supplier purchase commitments have jumped to $279 billion, more than double the $119 billion reported last quarter, as it races to lock up memory and manufacturing capacity for AI data centers. The total breaks down to roughly $92 billion for the remainder of fiscal 2026, $87 billion in fiscal 2028, $88 billion in fiscal 2029, and smaller sums through 2032. Nvidia has warned a memory-chip bottleneck — with Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron prioritizing higher-margin HBM output — could constrain its growth into early 2028, forcing rivals to pay premiums for scarce chips.
TECH
Kioxia, Sandisk to Invest More Than $31 Billion in Memory Chips
Kioxia and Sandisk announced a joint plan to invest more than $31 billion (about 5 trillion yen) in Japan through 2032, contingent on government support, to expand NAND flash production for AI workloads. The two companies extended their Yokkaichi joint-venture agreement through 2034 and aligned their Kitakami JV to the same timeline, aiming for stable output of advanced 3D flash memory. The move follows Kioxia's separate plan to invest roughly 1 trillion yen ($6.3 billion) in a new production facility in northern Japan, and comes as reported combined capex for the Kioxia-Sandisk alliance has risen about 40% year-over-year.
TECH
Aker Shares Climb After Nscale Gets $45 Billion Contract
Shares of Norwegian investment firm Aker ASA jumped roughly 7% after its portfolio company Nscale, a U.K.-based AI infrastructure provider, signed a $45 billion, six-year deal with Anthropic to supply AI compute capacity from Nscale's Monarch data-center site in West Virginia. The agreement covers about 460 megawatts of power and will use Nvidia's upcoming Vera Rubin chips, expected online in late 2027. Danske Bank said the contract supports a valuation of more than $25 billion for Nscale ahead of its potential September IPO, with some estimates putting the offering's value as high as $33.5 billion; Nscale is also backed by Nvidia, Dell and Nokia.
TECH
Would There Be an AI Revolution If There Were No Nvidia?
(analysis) This analysis piece probes Nvidia's outsized role in the AI buildout, a question underscored by the company's August 26 fiscal Q2 earnings, where data-center revenue rose more than 50% year-over-year toward roughly $85.7 billion against consensus total revenue near $92 billion. CEO Jensen Huang said Blackwell Ultra production "is ramping at full speed" with "extraordinary" demand, while analysts project total industry AI capex will exceed $1 trillion this year, up about 111% year-over-year, driven by roughly 98% growth in hyperscaler spending from Amazon, Alphabet and Meta. That concentration of demand — and Nvidia's simultaneous $279 billion in supply commitments — illustrates why rivals and customers alike see the company as effectively the AI buildout's chokepoint.
TECH
Meta Reaches $18 Billion Settlement Over Child-Safety Claims
Meta agreed to pay about $18 billion to settle a multistate lawsuit, joined by 48 states, alleging it knowingly designed addictive Facebook and Instagram features that harmed young users' mental health. Roughly 70% of the fund will be paid to states in installments over a decade, with the remaining 30% contingent on YouTube and TikTok agreeing to similar payments and platform changes; just over $17 billion resolves the core case brought by 29 states in 2023. Meta also agreed to platform changes including a default two-hour daily time limit for users under 18, following earlier losses this year including a nearly $1 billion New Mexico judgment.
MARKETS
U.S. Stocks Mixed After Bessent Outlines Plan to Squeeze Iran
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled a sanctions push dubbed "Operation Economic Outcast" aimed at cutting off Iran's oil revenue by targeting countries and companies that keep trading with Tehran, though he stopped short of imposing major new penalties immediately. China, India, Turkey, Iraq and the UAE were flagged as most exposed, given China's role as Iran's largest oil buyer amid an existing blockade that has sharply curtailed Iranian exports. U.S. stocks traded mixed as investors weighed the geopolitical announcement against upcoming Nvidia earnings and the Jackson Hole symposium.
MARKETS
The Fed Chairman Is Heading to Jackson Hole Without a Clear Stance on Inflation
Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole address as chair on Friday, August 28, with inflation still running around 3.4%, well above the Fed's 2% target, even as trade tensions and geopolitical strain weigh on growth. Warsh's "Monetary Barbell" approach pairs a non-mechanical stance on short-term rates with accelerated balance-sheet runoff from the Fed's $6.6 trillion holdings, and investors are watching for signs he might abandon average-inflation targeting for a stricter 2% goal. The symposium's official theme this year is "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy," running August 27-29 in Wyoming.
MARKETS
Kevin Warsh Has to Pick a Side in the Bond-Market Battle
Long-dated Treasury yields have climbed to levels last seen before the financial crisis, with the 30-year around 5.16%-5.3% and the 10-year near 4.65%, as bond traders test new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's resolve on inflation. Warsh has removed forward guidance from FOMC statements and welcomed the yield rise as market-driven tightening, saying in July "we haven't done much in 42 days — the markets have done quite a bit." That stance has put him at odds with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, whose bond-buyback interventions aim to push long-term rates lower to ease mortgage and borrowing costs, a tension CNN and the Motley Fool both frame as a looming clash of strategies ahead of Jackson Hole.
MARKETS
Nasdaq Futures Buoyed by Nvidia Earnings, Software as Oil Falls
Nasdaq futures rose as investors digested Nvidia's fiscal Q2 report, released after the bell on August 26, with the stock and broader software names in focus following the results and Jensen Huang's commentary on Blackwell demand and China exposure. Oil prices fell in early trading on renewed optimism after Iran and Oman resumed talks over a joint shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz. The combination of an AI-earnings bounce and easing Middle East tensions gave futures a lift heading into the Jackson Hole symposium later in the week.
MARKETS
Jameson Maker Pernod Ricard Warns of Sales Hit as Americans Ease Off on Drinking
Pernod Ricard reported a worse-than-expected 3.9% organic sales decline for fiscal 2026, its third straight annual drop, with U.S. sales down 14% and China down 19% as weak demand hit prestige brands like Martell cognac. CEO Alexandre Ricard said the U.S. market "is not in growth" and will likely stay soft through the company's outlook period to 2029, prompting Pernod to guide toward only the low end of its long-term sales targets. Shares fell roughly 5% on the announcement as tourism disruption tied to Middle East conflict compounded the U.S. and China weakness.
MARKETS
Hormuz Risk Premium Eases as Oman, Iran Discuss Shipping Corridor
Iran and Oman have agreed on a temporary maritime route through the Strait of Hormuz, including a roughly 7-mile-wide joint navigation corridor and a plan to clear mines from the waterway, following a crisis that began after U.S.-Israel strikes on Iran on February 28 effectively shut the strait to shipping. Traffic has cratered to as few as eight vessels transiting on some days, compared with roughly 130 per day before the conflict, and an oil tanker was recently disabled by a projectile near Oman's coast, underscoring lingering risk. The prospect of a negotiated corridor eased the oil-market risk premium and contributed to falling crude prices this week.
MARKETS
Burlington Stores Plans to Use Tariff Refunds to Lower Prices
Burlington Stores reported second-quarter net income of $184 million and revenue up 11% to $3.00 billion, with comparable-store sales up 2%, and said it will reinvest $55 million in tariff refunds into lower prices rather than book them as profit — roughly 40% in Q3 and 60% in Q4. Adjusted EPS excluding tariff refunds and bankruptcy-lease costs came to $2.37, up 38% from $1.72 a year earlier, and the retailer raised its full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $11.77-$11.97 from $11.45-$11.80. CEO Michael O'Sullivan framed the move as protecting Burlington's off-price value positioning as tariff-refund windfalls flow through the sector.
FINANCE
Gold Trades Flat With Limited Downside on Debasement Trade
(analysis) Gold has surged roughly 14% in August, climbing from below $4,000 in late July to near $4,650-$4,656 an ounce, its highest level since mid-May, as the "debasement trade" returns amid concerns over U.S. government spending and Treasury debt. The rally was reignited by the Treasury's unusual step of expanding debt buybacks under Secretary Scott Bessent, which investors read as a signal of fiscal stress, pushing money into gold and bitcoin as hedges against dollar weakening. Analysts note gold has kept rising even as long-dated yields held roughly flat, suggesting the metal is tracking fiscal-sustainability fears more than the path of interest rates.
FINANCE
Wheat Soars to Highest Level in Three Years as Black Sea Crisis Deepens
Wheat futures have climbed roughly 35% since the start of 2026 and about 20% in the past two months, reaching multi-year highs, as Russian missile strikes on Odesa's ports and Ukrainian drone attacks on Sea of Azov shipping have cut Black Sea grain shipments by more than 40% versus a year ago. The disruption compounds a global supply shortfall: the USDA projects the top seven wheat exporters' combined 2026/27 production will fall 11% and exports will drop 7% from last year, worsened by regional drought. Rising wheat costs threaten to feed through to consumer food prices worldwide as the conflict-driven supply squeeze persists.
FINANCE
U.S., European Bond Yields Rise, Dollar Steady Ahead of Jackson Hole Meet
The 10-year Treasury yield stood near 4.645% and the 30-year near 5.16% as global bond yields rose ahead of the Jackson Hole symposium, which opened August 27 and runs through August 29 under the theme "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." The dollar held steady to slightly firmer as traders awaited Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's keynote address on Friday, with recent data showing July inflation ran hotter than economists expected, reinforcing bets that rates stay restrictive into year-end. For savers and borrowers, the higher-for-longer yield backdrop keeps mortgage rates and savings-account yields elevated relative to earlier in the year.
FINANCE
Philippine Central Bank Raises Rates for Third Straight Meeting
The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas raised its benchmark reverse repurchase rate by 25 basis points to 5%, its highest level in over a year, marking a third consecutive hike after moves in April and June. Overnight deposit and lending facility rates rose in tandem to 4.5% and 5.5% respectively, even as headline inflation eased to 6.2% in July from 6.4% in June and core inflation slowed to 4.2%. Policymakers cited continued upside risks including volatile global oil prices, an ongoing El Niño episode threatening agricultural output, and possible wage adjustments as reasons to keep tightening despite the peso-support rationale.
