Exclusive | Nvidia Pauses Revenue-Sharing Deals With AI Cloud Companies
Nvidia has paused deals under a financing program launched in July 2026 that offered credit support to AI cloud companies in exchange for a cut of their cloud revenue, stepping back from the initiative last week (week of August 24, 2026) after Reuters reported the news on August 27, 2026. Under the proposed arrangement, Nvidia would have taken 50% of any revenue cloud providers earned from its chips beyond a certain threshold, and the company reportedly told some customers they could only rent the chips to approved clients and preferred capacity spread across multiple smaller AI firms rather than concentrated with one large customer. Some Nvidia employees flagged that the deal structure could invite antitrust scrutiny given the degree of control Nvidia sought over how customers ran their businesses. A company spokesperson said the broader "new business model" introduced in July remains in place and "continues to evolve due to high demand," without confirming the pause.

Chinese Chip Maker CXMT Cashes In on AI-Fueled Memory Crunch
China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) reported first-half 2026 revenue of 150.31 billion yuan (about $22.4 billion), up 873.64% year-on-year, with net profit of 77.61 billion yuan reversing a 2.33 billion yuan loss a year earlier — its first earnings since a blockbuster Shanghai IPO in July 2026 made it China's most valuable listed company. The surge was driven by soaring AI-industry demand and tight global DRAM supply, with newer DDR5 products now making up 46.3% of core revenue, up from 31.9% in 2025. CXMT has also landed major supply deals, including a five-year agreement worth more than $7 billion with ByteDance and a $3 billion-plus deal with Tencent, and now accounts for roughly 7% of global DRAM revenue.

Nvidia’s $279 Billion Supply-Chain Gamble
Nvidia disclosed in its fiscal Q2 2027 earnings report (period ended July 26, 2026, released August 26, 2026) that its long-term purchase commitments jumped to $279 billion, more than doubling from $119 billion the prior quarter. The company said the increase is "primarily related to the procurement of memory," reflecting deals for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) as well as existing wafer/packaging commitments at TSMC, as it locks up supply amid demand it expects to outstrip capacity through fiscal 2028. Roughly $267 billion of the total comes due by the end of fiscal 2029, with about $92 billion owed in the remainder of the current fiscal year alone.

Exclusive | Thinking Machines Lab Co-Founder Barret Zoph Joins Google
Barret Zoph, co-founder and CTO of Thinking Machines Lab, is joining Google DeepMind as Vice President of Research, focusing on reinforcement learning and post-training for Gemini. Zoph left OpenAI in 2022 to eventually co-found Thinking Machines with Mira Murati in 2025, briefly returned to OpenAI in January 2026 alongside fellow co-founder Luke Metz, then departed OpenAI in June 2026 after about five months. His new Google role marks a return to the company where he began his career in the 2016 Google Brain Residency program and worked on AutoML/neural architecture search before leaving in 2022.

Exclusive | Google Moves AI-Responsibility Team Out of DeepMind Lab in Latest Shake-Up
Google is moving its roughly 90-person AI-responsibility team out of Google DeepMind and into the company's global affairs organization, which oversees lobbying and public policy, as part of a broader reorganization set to take effect in early September 2026. The unit — which studies chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear risks from Google's AI models, how people interact with AI, and the psychological effects of chatbots — is led by VP Helen King, who has told staff their computing resources, headcount, and ability to publish research externally will stay unchanged. The shift has unsettled some researchers, who worry about losing independence and direct access to Gemini's development teams. It follows other recent changes at DeepMind, including Demis Hassabis moving from CEO to a chairman role as Google works to convert DeepMind from a semi-autonomous unit (its status since the 2014 acquisition) into a division more tightly integrated with the rest of the company.

Nvidia Reports Blowout Quarter, Says Demand for AI Chips Is Getting Even Hotter
Nvidia reported record revenue of $96.2 billion for its fiscal second quarter (ended July 26, 2026), up 18% from the prior quarter and 106% year-over-year, with Data Center revenue alone hitting $89 billion. Net income came in at $59.69 billion ($2.46 per diluted share GAAP; $2.22 non-GAAP, beating the ~$2.09-2.10 consensus estimate), and gross margins held at 75%. For the current quarter, Nvidia guided to roughly $108 billion in revenue — about an 89% year-over-year increase — citing continued surging demand for its AI chips; shares jumped nearly 9% on the news, adding about $440 billion in market value.

The World’s Craziest Stock Market Has Turned Into a Fright Ride
The WSJ headline refers to South Korea's Kospi index. After surging as the world's best-performing major market in 2025-26 on an AI/semiconductor boom, the Kospi crashed roughly 40-44% over six weeks between late June and July 2026, tumbling from a June 22 closing peak of 9,114.55 to the 5,600 range — its worst rout since the Great Recession. Single-stock leveraged ETFs tied to Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, launched in May 2026, amplified the swings: the SK Hynix leveraged ETF fell more than 80% from its June 23 peak and the Samsung version fell nearly 75%. Roughly 92% of the estimated $9.2 billion invested in these products came from retail investors, who collectively lost an estimated $38.7 billion, with about 1.2 million South Koreans (3.4% of the adult population) hit with margin calls, prompting a public apology from a government minister.

Opinion | Let the Bond Market Speak
Brief: On August 19, 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department announced it would roughly double its long-dated bond buyback operations — from about $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation — after the 30-year Treasury yield touched a 19-year high. Yields initially dipped but had round-tripped back above their starting levels within a day, and CNBC reported the buyback-driven rally in longer-dated Treasurys quickly fizzled. The move, championed by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, has drawn criticism (notably from investor Stanley Druckenmiller, Bessent's former boss at Soros Fund Management) that intervening in bond prices undermines market credibility rather than addressing underlying fiscal fundamentals. The episode has also fed broader market unease about Fed independence ahead of Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks, with the 10-year yield near 4.65-4.68% and the 30-year near 5.20% as of August 27, 2026.

U.K. Defense Stocks Fall as Government Likely to Drop Spending Targets
UK defense contractors slid on August 28, 2026 after reports emerged that Chancellor John Healey will shelve the target of raising defense spending to 3% of GDP by 2030 in his first budget, due October 28, instead deferring the decision to next year's Treasury spending review. On the news, BAE Systems fell 1.8%, Babcock International lost 1.3%, and QinetiQ declined 0.8% in London trading. The October budget is instead expected to focus on funding an existing defense equipment funding gap of nearly £5.0 billion (about £1.2 billion a year) left by the prior government, with the UK still nominally committed to NATO's 3.5%-of-GDP-by-2035 goal but without a firm interim path to 3%. The move drew political criticism, notably because Healey himself resigned as defense secretary in June over what he called insufficient funding ambition before later becoming Chancellor.

Investors Brace for Less-Than-Perfect Nvidia Results, Pull Stocks Lower
Ahead of Nvidia's fiscal Q2 earnings, released after the close on Wednesday, August 26, 2026, US stocks slipped as investors grew cautious, with tech shares leading indexes lower amid rising Treasury yields and the approaching Jackson Hole Fed symposium. Nvidia shares themselves had pulled back from an 11-week high near $228 earlier in August to around $217.56 by August 19, as analysts weighed forecasts of roughly $91.9 billion in revenue (about 97% year-over-year growth) against concerns over the company's massive AI infrastructure and customer-financing commitments. Investors were also unsettled by estimates that Nvidia's broader AI-related credit exposure could approach $200 billion by 2028. Nvidia ultimately beat expectations, reporting $96.22 billion in revenue and $2.22 EPS, with shares closing near $227.98 the following day.

Exclusive | Nuveen Raises $1 Billion to Finance Improvements for Commercial Buildings
Nuveen Green Capital, an affiliate of Nuveen, has raised more than $1 billion at the first close of its fourth C-PACE (Commercial Property Assessed Clean Energy) lending fund, surpassing its predecessor fund's $785 million close by over 25%. C-PACE is a state-administered public-private financing program that gives commercial building owners and developers long-term private capital for energy efficiency, water efficiency, and climate-resilience upgrades. The new close brings total commitments across Nuveen's C-PACE fund series to more than $3 billion since the program launched in 2023, with multifamily properties expected to make up roughly 35-45% of the new fund's deployment. CEO and CIO Alexandra Cooley attributed the growth largely to repeat commitments from existing insurer investors seeking recurring C-PACE allocation.

Bath & Body Works Is Still Cautious Despite Signs of Turnaround
Bath & Body Works reported second-quarter fiscal 2026 net sales of about $1.5 billion, down 2.3% year-over-year, with adjusted earnings per diluted share of $0.62 — well above the $0.24 analyst consensus and beating the company's own guidance. Despite the beat, management struck a cautious tone for the rest of the year, forecasting third-quarter sales to decline 2.5%–5% with adjusted EPS of just $0.07–$0.12, citing continued softness in body care, tariff pressure, and higher energy costs. The company did raise its full-year fiscal 2026 outlook, now guiding to adjusted EPS of $2.60–$2.80 on a narrowed sales decline of 2.5%–4% versus fiscal 2025's $7.291 billion in revenue, framing the results as early progress in a multi-year turnaround rather than a quick fix.

Fed’s Preferred Inflation Gauge Remains Above Target Range
The Commerce Department reported on August 26, 2026 that the PCE price index — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge — rose 0.2% in July and was up 3.7% year-over-year, matching June's pace and coming in slightly above economists' expectations of 3.6%. Core PCE (excluding food and energy) rose 0.2% monthly and 3.3% annually, in line with forecasts. Both measures remain well above the Fed's 2% target, a level the gauge has now exceeded for more than five straight years, complicating the central bank's path on rate policy. Personal income rose 0.4% and spending rose 0.2% in the same report, both slightly stronger than expected.

Exclusive | Anthropic Expected to Tell Investors It Sees Over $30 Trillion in Potential Revenue
Anthropic is expected to tell prospective investors it sees a total addressable market of more than $30 trillion for AI, a figure that would top the $28.5 trillion TAM SpaceX cited in its May 2026 IPO filing, according to a Wall Street Journal report picked up by Reuters. The company reportedly calculates the number by looking at "the full scope of work that could be completed with AI models" rather than a narrower software market definition. The pitch comes as Anthropic is projecting roughly $190-200 billion in actual 2028 revenue and prepares investors ahead of a planned IPO, with the TAM figure meant to justify its valuation and heavy infrastructure spending amid rivalry with OpenAI and Google. Anthropic did not immediately respond to a request for comment on the figures.
FINANCE
Burlington Stores Plans to Use Tariff Refunds to Lower Prices
Burlington Stores said it will pass along roughly $55 million in tariff-related refunds to customers through lower prices during the back half of fiscal 2026, rather than keeping the money as extra profit. The plan, disclosed alongside second-quarter results in which Burlington reported net income of $184 million ($2.88 per diluted share), calls for reinvesting about 40% of the refunds in the third quarter and the remaining 60% in the fourth quarter. CEO Michael O'Sullivan said passing the savings to shoppers "feels like the right thing to do," framing the move as part of Burlington's value-focused strategy amid ongoing tariff pressure on retailers.
