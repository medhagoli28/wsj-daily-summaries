Apple to Give New CEO $3 Million Salary
Apple set new CEO John Ternus's pay package at $3 million in base salary plus $55 million in target annual equity, along with a one-time prorated $2.5 million RSU grant for fiscal 2026, bringing his total compensation target to about $58 million, according to a proxy filing disclosed Tuesday, September 1, 2026. Ternus formally succeeded Tim Cook as CEO, with the transition effective around September 26, 2026. Cook, moving into the newly created role of executive chairman, will see his own pay cut to a $2 million salary plus a $45 million equity award for fiscal 2027. The disclosure came via Apple's SEC filing (Form DEF 14A) marking the first day of Ternus's tenure as chief executive.

Apple Updates Maps to Show Lake America to U.S. Users
Apple updated its Maps app for U.S. users on September 1-2, 2026, renaming Lake Ontario as "Lake America," following an executive order from President Trump issued about a week earlier. The change applies only to the U.S. version of Apple Maps, while the Canadian version still shows "Lake Ontario" and international versions display both names. Apple's move came days after Alphabet made the same change to Google Maps for U.S. users on August 29-30, 2026. U.S. Interior Secretary Doug Burgum said Trump personally contacted Apple to request the change, part of a pattern that also included Apple's earlier renaming of the Gulf of Mexico to "Gulf of America" and Denali to "Mount McKinley."

Broadcom’s AI Success Story Gets Complicated
This aligns with Broadcom's earnings report due today, September 2, 2026, which likely prompted the WSJ piece. I now have solid, concrete facts from CNBC and TheStreet to write the brief. Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue more than doubled to $10.8 billion in its fiscal second quarter (reported June 2026), and CEO Hock Tan has guided for AI chip revenue to exceed $100 billion by fiscal 2027, with fiscal Q3 guidance calling for roughly $16 billion in AI revenue at over 200% year-over-year growth ahead of the company's earnings report after market close on September 2, 2026. The growth is driven by custom AI chips built for just a handful of customers — Google, Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic — with Meta's agreement running through 2029 and Alphabet's through 2031, raising investor concern about revenue concentration in a small number of hyperscaler relationships. Broadcom shares have swung sharply on this dynamic, including a slide after June's report when strong results still fell short of elevated market expectations amid broader worries that AI infrastructure spending has outpaced demand.

Exclusive | Can Evan Spiegel Sell the World on $2,195 Smart Glasses?
Snap unveiled its next-generation augmented-reality glasses, called Specs, on June 16, 2026, at the AWE conference in Long Beach, California, pricing them at $2,195 with a $200 refundable deposit for preorders. CEO Evan Spiegel positioned the device as "a new type of computer, a see-through computer," notable for lacking a tethered computing puck and using a proprietary liquid-crystal-on-silicon display with a 51-degree field of view and 16 million colors. The glasses weigh 132 grams (47mm frame) or 136 grams (51mm frame), run on two Qualcomm Snapdragon processors, and offer roughly four hours of battery life under mixed use. Specs are set to ship this fall in the U.S., U.K., and France, making them pricier than any other smart glasses currently on the market.

Bond Yields Around the World Soar in Challenge to Government Borrowing
Yields on long-dated government debt surged to near two-decade highs in early September 2026, with the 30-year U.S. Treasury yield reaching roughly 5.29% and the 10-year hitting about 4.81%, its highest since November 2023. The selloff is global: 30-year UK gilt yields climbed to their highest since 1998, 30-year Japanese government bond yields neared record highs at around 4.19%, and a Bloomberg gauge of G7 government debt yields hit its highest average since September 2000. Investors are retreating from long-dated sovereign bonds on mounting fiscal deficits, stubborn inflation, an oil-price spike tied to Middle East tensions, and competition from a boom in AI-related corporate borrowing, with governments and companies expected to borrow a record $29 trillion from bond markets in 2026.

U.S. Stocks Fall as Oil Surges After New Middle East Strikes
U.S. stocks fell on September 1, 2026, with the S&P 500 down about 0.7% to 7,633, the Dow Jones Industrial Average off roughly 0.79%-0.9% (around 456 points) to near 52,767, and the Nasdaq Composite dropping about 1% to around 26,100, as the U.S. and Iran exchanged strikes for the first time in about a month, with American forces hitting an island in the Strait of Hormuz and Iran responding with attacks on the United Arab Emirates and Jordan. The escalation revived fears of supply disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz and stoked inflation concerns that pushed bond yields higher, adding pressure on equities already grappling with tech-sector weakness.

London Stock Exchange Plans Tokenized Trading
The London Stock Exchange has partnered with Payward (Kraken's parent company) to tokenize the 100 largest London-listed companies as "xStocks," each backed 1:1 by the underlying share, with availability expected in the coming weeks through Kraken and other platforms in over 110 countries, though not initially for UK-based investors. Subject to regulatory approval, LSE plans to list and trade these tokenized shares directly on a new venue called LSE 24 starting in 2027, with testing set to begin by the end of 2026. LSE 24 is designed as a near-continuous, Monday-to-Friday trading venue running from 17:00 to 07:50 London time with a 30-minute end-of-day processing pause, aimed at attracting international retail investors and competing with crypto markets that never close. The partnership also includes plans to explore native equity tokens issued directly through LSE infrastructure, carrying the same shareholder rights as traditional shares while remaining fungible with them.

U.S. Stocks Fall as Iran War Inflation Heats Up
U.S. stock markets fell on June 13, 2025, after Israel launched military strikes on Iran, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropping close to 500 points (about 1.1%) in early trading as investors grew worried about a renewed spike in inflation. Oil prices spiked sharply on the news, with U.S. benchmark crude jumping $4.97, or 7.3%, to $72.91 per barrel — the biggest one-day jump in crude prices since Russia's invasion of Ukraine more than three years earlier. Analysts warned that sustained higher oil prices could reverse the recent decline in gasoline costs that had been helping keep U.S. inflation in check, and JPMorgan noted that an all-out war could push oil toward $120 a barrel and U.S. CPI toward 5%. Global markets also slid in sympathy, with Japan's Nikkei down 0.9%, South Korea's Kospi down 0.9%, and Germany's DAX falling 1.4%.

A Guide to Owning Bonds When They Are Selling Off
The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield hit 4.80%–4.81% on September 2, 2026, its highest level since November 2023, while the 30-year Treasury yield climbed above 5.3%, its highest since 2007, as investors weigh persistent inflation, a roughly $2 trillion federal deficit, and over $40 trillion in outstanding government debt; bond prices move inversely to yields, so the selloff has hit existing bondholders' portfolio values even as new buyers can lock in higher income. In response to the volatility, the U.S. Treasury said it would at least double the size of its long-term debt buybacks, from $2 billion to at least $4 billion, with purchases running through September to November to help support prices. Financial advisers are steering investors navigating the selloff toward ultra-short bond ETFs (which drew $12.8 billion in inflows in July) and intermediate-term bonds in the four- to ten-year maturity range, rather than concentrating in long-duration debt.

Natural Gas Futures Edge Down

October Nymex natural gas futures (contract NGV26) fell 1.06%, closing down 3.1 cents to settle lower on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, as US weather forecasts turned cooler, with below-normal temperatures expected in the Northeast, reducing expected air-conditioning demand from power generators. Oil Prices Rise as Renewed U.S.-Iran Fighting Deepens Hormuz Supply Risks
Oil prices surged above $95 a barrel on September 2, 2026, extending a 4.6% rally as renewed fighting between the U.S. and Iran deepened fears over the Strait of Hormuz, with Brent crude up 0.87% to $95.47 and WTI trading 0.55% higher at $90.72; the strait, which carries roughly 20% of the world's oil shipments, saw vessel crossings drop sharply from 23 the prior Wednesday to just 10 by Monday. The escalation followed U.S. strikes on Iranian targets near Hormuz, which Washington said were retaliation for Iranian sea mines and missile attacks on a U.S. base in Jordan, with Iran vowing reprisals and Kuwait, Jordan, and Bahrain reporting missile alerts and interceptions.

Judge Presses Mamdani Administration on Pied-à-Terre Tax Rollout
Staten Island Supreme Court Judge Wayne Ozzi pressed New York City's attorneys on August 31, 2026 over the rollout of Mayor Zohran Mamdani's pied-à-terre tax, questioning why a "supplemental roll" of more than 900,000 properties was published when only a small subset was actually meant to be flagged for the surcharge. The tax targets non-primary residences valued at $5 million or more (and condos/co-ops worth $1 million-plus), and is projected to raise roughly $500 million for the city budget; three homeowners sued after being mistakenly included on the published list, which exposed names and addresses. Ozzi had issued a temporary restraining order on August 10, 2026 halting the rollout, finding the city's notices failed to explain why recipients were flagged and shifted an unfair burden onto property owners to prove they shouldn't be taxed, but an appellate judge allowed implementation to continue while the city appeals. At the August 31 hearing, Ozzi weighed whether homeowners were actually harmed by the erroneous notices as the legal fight over the rollout's legality continues.
