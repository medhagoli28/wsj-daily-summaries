Thoma Bravo-Owned Proofpoint in Advanced Talks to Buy Varonis
Thoma Bravo-backed Proofpoint is in advanced talks to acquire Israeli cybersecurity firm Varonis Systems in a deal that would value the company at roughly $5.4 billion, Bloomberg reported on September 2, 2026, citing people familiar with the matter. Varonis, which specializes in data security, had a market capitalization of about $5 billion before the report and saw its shares jump more than 10-13% on the news. People familiar with the matter said a transaction could be announced in the coming weeks, though no final decision has been made and the talks could still fall apart or another bidder could emerge. Representatives for Thoma Bravo, Proofpoint and Varonis did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Microsoft to Change Reporting Structure to Reflect Effects of AI
Microsoft announced on September 2, 2026 that it will overhaul its financial reporting structure starting in fiscal year 2027, collapsing its three current segments into two: "Agents and Infra" and "Devices and Consumer." The Agents and Infra segment will bundle Microsoft 365, GitHub, Azure, and other productivity and server licensing businesses, while Devices and Consumer will cover search, advertising, Xbox, and Windows OEM/devices; Microsoft projects the former to generate $75.15-75.75 billion and the latter $14.7-15.2 billion in an early reporting period. As part of the change, Microsoft will begin disclosing Azure's quarterly revenue for the first time, giving investors clearer visibility into how it stacks up against AWS and Google Cloud. CEO Satya Nadella said the shift reflects how AI "is changing the way we build and operate, blurring the boundaries between our products and reshaping our business model."

U.S. Government Backs OpenAI in Copyright Fight With Publishers
The Trump administration's Justice Department filed a statement of interest on Wednesday, September 2, 2026, backing OpenAI's fair-use defense in the copyright lawsuit The New York Times filed against OpenAI and Microsoft in 2023 over the use of millions of Times articles to train AI models. The DOJ argued that "the creative possibilities and public benefits" of training large language models on copyrighted content "far outweigh any competitive harm," and said it has a "strong interest" in fostering a "robust and competitive" U.S. AI industry. The filing is non-binding but marks the first time the federal government has weighed in on the wave of AI-training copyright suits brought by authors, publishers, and news outlets. A Times spokesperson, Graham James, criticized the move, saying the DOJ was siding with "trillion-dollar AI companies" over American creators.

Tech CEOs, Trump Administration Ask G-20 to Adopt Pro-AI Policies
Tech executives from OpenAI, Nvidia, and Anthropic joined the Trump administration at a G20 Innovation Ministerial summit hosted in North Carolina and led by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, pressing world leaders to adopt a light-touch approach to AI regulation. On Wednesday, September 2, 2026, all G20 member nations endorsed the US-proposed framework, which calls on policymakers to "reserve new regulation for novel considerations" rather than treating each AI advance as a first-of-its-kind problem. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman told attendees that adopting AI was "non-negotiable," comparing it to electricity, while Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang urged governments to regulate based on demonstrated harms rather than theoretical risks; Lutnick separately advocated for letting AI companies train models on copyrighted material. The push reflects the administration's broader strategy of avoiding new AI regulatory institutions to keep the US ahead of China in the global AI race.

Uber, Wayve Bring Robotaxis to London
Uber and British AI startup Wayve launched supervised autonomous ride-hailing in London on September 2, 2026, marking Wayve's first commercial public robotaxi service anywhere in the world and London's first robotaxi offering. Riders booking UberX, Uber Comfort, or Uber Electric can be matched at no extra cost with one of fewer than 20 Wayve-equipped Ford Mustang Mach-E vehicles, each carrying a licensed safety operator on board since fully driverless operation isn't yet authorized. The companies had secured the necessary licenses from UK regulators back in early August 2026, and London becomes the second European city, after Zagreb, where Uber offers Wayve-powered rides. Wider driverless rollout still depends on further approvals from Transport for London.

See the New Building Techniques Turbocharging the Data-Center Boom
Data centers that once took roughly two years to build are now being delivered in a matter of months as builders shift to prefabricated, factory-assembled construction, with companies like Montreal-based Hypertec Construction reporting that a "prefab-first" approach—building power skids, cooling assemblies and electrical modules off-site while site work proceeds in parallel—can cut timelines by roughly half. Industry-wide, modular and prefabricated delivery methods are producing 30-50% schedule reductions compared with conventional stick-built projects, according to construction-industry trade coverage. In Asia, Alibaba Cloud has said it can now deliver a large data center in about 100 days, down from nearly a year previously. The push for speed comes as Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta alone are expected to spend roughly $725 billion on AI infrastructure in 2026, part of an industry-wide capex tab approaching $1 trillion, with executives citing competitive pressure to add capacity faster than rivals.

OpenAI Hits Back at Apple Lawsuit Claiming It Stole Trade Secrets
Apple sued OpenAI on July 10, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, accusing the company and two former Apple employees, Chang Liu and Tang Tan, of stealing trade secrets tied to hardware design, manufacturing, and supply-chain operations. OpenAI moved to dismiss the case in early August, arguing Apple "failed to demonstrate it owns a protectable trade secret," failed to allege plausible misappropriation, and failed to show any actual injury, calling the suit "a mess of Apple's own making" intended to slow a rival and deter employee departures. In a filing OpenAI also disclosed it has hired roughly 400 former Apple employees for its hardware projects. Apple pushed back on August 19, reaffirming what it called "pervasive trade secret misappropriation," and on August 31 submitted new forensic evidence alleging Liu used stolen trade secrets at OpenAI and even had an AI agent run them.
