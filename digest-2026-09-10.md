- **Watch the AI Boom’s Weakest Link** — Business development companies (BDCs) had lent roughly $115 billion to software firms as of early 2026, representing about a fifth of their total lending and more than 80% of their fastest-growing technology portfolios, according to a Bank for International Settlements bulletin. Between October 2025 and February 2026, software company stocks fell nearly 30% amid fears that AI tools could disrupt traditional software businesses, dragging BDC share prices down about 10% on average and rattling the private-credit market that many retail investors access through non-traded BDC funds. In the first quarter of 2026 alone, investors requested close to $14 billion in redemptions from the non-traded BDC sector, though sponsors honored only about half of those requests, underscoring liquidity strains as the sector's exposure to AI-vulnerable software debt came under scrutiny. _Sources: <https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-128-ai-disruption-private-credit-exposure-software-firms-bdcs>, <https://www.forbes.com/sites/greatspeculations/2026/05/15/is-the-ai-triggered-meltdown-in-private-credit-overblown/>_
- **The Next Big Opportunity in Data Centers: Insuring Them** — Global insurers are set to collect roughly $10 billion in new premiums from data centers in 2026 alone—about double the size of the global aviation insurance market—with cumulative premiums tied to the sector projected to reach $134 billion between 2026 and 2030, according to S&P Global estimates cited by Insurance Journal and Bloomberg. Swiss Re separately pegs the current annual premium pool at about $10.6 billion, forecasting it will more than double to $24.2 billion by 2030 as AI-driven data center construction accelerates and facilities carry far higher insured values due to the soaring cost of GPU hardware. Insurers such as Aon have responded by expanding capacity: in April 2026 Aon boosted its Data Center Lifecycle Insurance Program to $3.5 billion, adding $1 billion in new capacity and extending coverage to existing operating facilities, not just construction projects. Despite the growth, brokers and reinsurers warn the market remains one of the hardest to place, with billions of dollars in construction risk still uninsured because demand has outrun available underwriting capacity. _Sources: <https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2026/04/14/865754.htm>, <https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-13/data-centers-offer-a-potential-10-billion-windfall-for-insurers>, <https://www.enr.com/articles/62899-report-billions-of-dollars-in-data-center-construction-risk-is-uninsured>_
