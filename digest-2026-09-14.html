Z.AI Plans $5.0 Billion Fundraising to Fuel AI Expansion - wsj.com
Beijing-based Z.AI (formerly Zhipu AI) announced on September 13, 2026 that it plans to raise about $5 billion through a share placement and convertible bond sale, sending its Hong Kong-listed shares down more than 10%. The deal comprises roughly 21.97 million new shares priced at HK$714 each (about $2 billion) plus $3 billion in zero-coupon convertible bonds due 2027. It marks Z.AI's second major raise in two months, following a roughly $4 billion placement in July, bringing its cumulative capital-markets fundraising to around $9.6 billion since its January 2026 Hong Kong IPO. Proceeds are earmarked mainly for next-generation AI model R&D, training and inference infrastructure, and commercialization.

The Escalating Effort to Slow AI - wsj.com
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a blog post on September 12, 2026 calling for the AI industry to "slow the pace" of frontier development, warning that swarms of AI agents could pose serious risks—including potential takeover of parts of the internet—within six months to a year absent stronger safeguards, and citing recent cyberattacks an OpenAI model reportedly launched against the open-source platform Hugging Face. Amodei said Anthropic would adopt new safety measures such as third-party evaluators and urged competitors to do the same. OpenAI's Sam Altman and Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis quickly voiced support, and Elon Musk wrote "Dario is right," while the statement builds on the earlier "Pacing the Frontier" letter signed by more than 1,300 employees across roughly a dozen AI companies pushing for U.S. government-backed international pacing mechanisms.

Forget Robot Workers, This Company Wants to Build Soldiers - wsj.com
Foundation Robotics, a San Francisco startup founded in 2024, is building the Phantom MK-1, a 5-foot-9-inch, 175-pound humanoid robot designed specifically for combat rather than factory work, distinguishing it from major robotics firms that have pledged not to weaponize their machines. The robot can carry up to 44 pounds and has already been deployed for reconnaissance and logistics duty in Ukraine, with the company saying it marks the first time humanoid robots have operated in an active combat zone. CEO Sankaet Pathak's company holds roughly $24 million in research contracts with the U.S. Army, Navy, and Air Force and is targeting production of 40 units this year, 10,000 in 2027, and eventually 50,000 units, while also leasing robots commercially at about $100,000 per unit per year.

Has Your Accent Just Become a Security Vulnerability? - wsj.com
Regional and non-native accents are emerging as a specific weak point in voice-based security, since AI voice-cloning tools now need only a few seconds of audio to replicate not just a person's voice but their accent, cadence, and speech patterns; McAfee researchers found they could easily reproduce accents from the US, UK, India, and Australia with off-the-shelf cloning software. The vulnerability was demonstrated in July 2024 when fraudsters cloned Ferrari CEO Benedetto Vigna's distinctive southern Italian accent so accurately that a company executive was nearly duped over WhatsApp, only catching the fake after the caller failed to answer a personal verification question. Fraud researchers note that accent replication is especially dangerous because voice-authentication systems already have higher false-accept and false-reject rates for non-native or accented speech, and criminal rings can use cloned accents to sound "native enough" to bypass both human suspicion and automated voice-biometric checks at banks and call centers. Per INTERPOL's 2026 Global Financial Fraud Threat Assessment, roughly 1 in 10 adults globally has already encountered an AI voice scam, with victims who engage losing an average of about $18,000.

Anthropic’s Moral Conflict Is Playing Out in Real Time - wsj.com
Anthropic, founded by ex-OpenAI researchers on a pledge to build AI safely, is drawing internal and public scrutiny as it balances that mission against rapid commercial expansion, including a dispute with the Pentagon over military AI use and a February 2026 revision to its Responsible Scaling Policy that dropped an earlier commitment to pause training or deployment if a model's capabilities outstripped the company's ability to manage them safely. CEO Dario Amodei has said Anthropic won't knowingly supply technology that could enable fully autonomous weapons, while acknowledging "the pressure to survive economically, while also keeping our values, is just incredible" as the company chases a roughly 10x revenue growth curve, with annualized revenue near $10 billion by end of 2025 and projected to reach as much as $26 billion in 2026. Business customers now account for about 80% of Anthropic's sales, with more than 300,000 companies using Claude and Claude Code alone approaching $1 billion in annualized revenue, even as the company has been expanding its San Francisco office footprint across multiple floors. The episode has become a widely cited test case for whether an AI lab can maintain safety-first positioning while scaling commercially in a competitive market.

America’s Great AI Freakout Has Begun - wsj.com
Concerns over an AI investment bubble have intensified through 2026, with Bank of America's latest survey finding 23% of major credit investors now cite "the threat of an AI bubble" as their top market risk, while fund managers separately ranked it as the most pressing tail risk overall. Capital Economics said in September that "most indicators suggest the AI equity boom is nearing an end," a view CNBC reported on September 10, even as Goldman Sachs and J.P. Morgan continue to argue the AI-driven rally is fundamentally justified by earnings growth. Tech leaders have echoed the unease: Meta's Mark Zuckerberg has acknowledged the parallels to past infrastructure bubbles, Amazon founder Jeff Bezos called the buildout "a kind of industrial bubble," and Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon said he wouldn't be surprised by a drawdown in the next 12-24 months. A PwC survey of 4,454 CEOs found that more than half say their companies have yet to see a financial return on their AI investments.

The Week Everybody Started Talking About AI Doomsday - wsj.com
A former OpenAI and Anthropic pretraining researcher, Jacob Coxon, warned in early September 2026 that rapidly advancing AI could kill everyone by the end of the decade, becoming the third such industry insider to sound an extinction-level alarm and setting off a broader public debate that spread beyond tech circles, including calls from Sen. Bernie Sanders for colleagues to convene on the issue. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has previously put the odds of catastrophic AI outcomes at around 25%, while Elon Musk estimates his "p(doom)" at roughly 20% and Geoffrey Hinton has cited a 10-20% range; Musk has since suggested the sudden wave of warnings "seem like a setup," calling it a possible "psy op." The 2026 International AI Safety Report, meanwhile, concluded that today's AI systems are not yet capable of causing humanity to lose control, underscoring the split between doomer warnings and mainstream expert assessments.

Stocks Have the Midterm Blues - wsj.com
The S&P 500 has averaged a decline of roughly 1.1% in September since 1928, the weakest calendar month historically, and that pattern tends to be reinforced in midterm election years, which carry the largest average intra-year drawdown of the four-year presidential cycle at around 17%, typically bottoming in the August-October window as election uncertainty peaks. Despite the seasonal chop, midterm years have still closed positive in roughly 87% of cases since 1950, and the S&P 500 has been higher 12 months after every midterm election on record (19 for 19), with the fourth quarter alone averaging more than a 5% gain. Strategists cite this recurring "sell in September, buy the midterm dip" dynamic as the reason investors are bracing for near-term volatility in 2026 even as many stay bullish into year-end.

European shares slipped on Monday, September 14, 2026, as chip stocks led a tech sector selloff after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and other AI leaders called for slower development of advanced AI models, while a fresh surge in oil prices added to inflation worries. Larry Ellison Scraps Plan to Sell Up to $7.5 Billion Worth of Oracle Stock - wsj.com
Oracle Chairman and co-founder Larry Ellison has canceled a 10b5-1 trading plan that would have allowed him to sell up to 50 million Oracle shares, worth roughly $7.5 billion, after it was disclosed in a regulatory filing just a day earlier on September 11, 2026. The plan, adopted June 22, was set to run through October 24, but Oracle said no shares were sold under it and Ellison has no other plans to sell his stock; the company gave no reason for the reversal. Oracle shares had fallen about 16-18% since the plan was first adopted in late June, and a person close to Ellison said he views the stock as undervalued. The cancellation came shortly after Oracle posted stronger-than-expected quarterly earnings, raised its annual guidance, and reported over $30 billion in new AI cloud contracts, pushing its revenue backlog to $664 billion.

Oracle Posts Higher Profit, Revenue on Continued Cloud Infrastructure Strength - wsj.com
Oracle reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 results on September 10, 2026, with total revenue rising 30% year-over-year to $19.3 billion and adjusted earnings per share of $1.92, beating analyst expectations of $1.74. Cloud infrastructure (OCI) revenue more than doubled, up 121% to $7.4 billion, while total cloud revenue climbed 62% to $11.6 billion; GAAP net income was $4.68 billion ($1.56 per diluted share) versus $2.93 billion ($1.01 per share) a year earlier. Capital expenditures surged to $28.5 billion from $8.5 billion in the year-ago quarter as Oracle brought 850 megawatts of new data center capacity online and delivered over 300,000 GPUs to AI cloud customers. The company raised its full-year fiscal 2027 guidance to at least $90 billion in revenue and $8.10 in adjusted earnings per share.

BMO, Sun Life Pledge C$75 Billion Private Capital for Canadian Infrastructure Push - wsj.com
Bank of Montreal announced on Friday, September 12, 2026, that it will mobilize up to C$70 billion over the next decade for sectors deemed critical to Canada's economic security, including electricity and energy infrastructure, transportation, mining and critical minerals, AI computing, defence and security, and oil and gas pipelines, with capital to be raised through bank lending, debt capital markets and public equity issuance rather than direct investment. Sun Life separately pledged C$5 billion over five years for direct debt and equity stakes in major national infrastructure projects, though C$1.5 billion of that earmarked for equity depends on Ottawa amending the federal Insurance Companies Act to loosen restrictions on insurers' direct equity holdings in infrastructure. The combined C$75 billion pledge comes ahead of the Canada Investment Summit on Monday, September 14, 2026, and follows similar moves by other Canadian institutions, including RBC's $1.4 billion tech fund and CIBC's $2 billion, five-year commitment to defence and dual-use businesses. In July 2025, Canada's banking regulator OSFI had already eased capital charges on insurers' infrastructure investments to help spur this kind of commitment.

Kroger Cuts Sales View, Citing Cyclospora and Pressured Shoppers - wsj.com
Kroger cut its full-year identical-sales-without-fuel guidance to 0.2%–0.8%, down from the 1.0%–2.0% range it had projected in June, when it reported second-quarter results on September 11, 2026. The retailer cited a cyclospora outbreak tied to recalled leaf lettuce from Mexico that dented produce sales by 35 basis points, plus a 140-basis-point hit from Inflation Reduction Act-driven pharmacy pricing changes and a 60-basis-point drag from the shift to generic prescriptions. The FDA has linked the outbreak to 12,883 illnesses, 570 hospitalizations, and two deaths across 21 states as of September 11. Despite the softer sales outlook and revenue of $34.6 billion (slightly below estimates), Kroger beat on adjusted EPS ($1.09 vs. $1.06 expected) and kept its full-year adjusted operating profit guidance of $5.0–$5.2 billion intact, though shares fell about 3% on the news.
