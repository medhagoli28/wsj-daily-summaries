- **Has Your Accent Just Become a Security Vulnerability? - wsj.com** — Regional and non-native accents are emerging as a specific weak point in voice-based security, since AI voice-cloning tools now need only a few seconds of audio to replicate not just a person's voice but their accent, cadence, and speech patterns; McAfee researchers found they could easily reproduce accents from the US, UK, India, and Australia with off-the-shelf cloning software. The vulnerability was demonstrated in July 2024 when fraudsters cloned Ferrari CEO Benedetto Vigna's distinctive southern Italian accent so accurately that a company executive was nearly duped over WhatsApp, only catching the fake after the caller failed to answer a personal verification question. Fraud researchers note that accent replication is especially dangerous because voice-authentication systems already have higher false-accept and false-reject rates for non-native or accented speech, and criminal rings can use cloned accents to sound "native enough" to bypass both human suspicion and automated voice-biometric checks at banks and call centers. Per INTERPOL's 2026 Global Financial Fraud Threat Assessment, roughly 1 in 10 adults globally has already encountered an AI voice scam, with victims who engage losing an average of about $18,000. _Sources: <https://www.mcafee.com/blogs/privacy-identity-protection/artificial-imposters-cybercriminals-turn-to-ai-voice-cloning-for-a-new-breed-of-scam/>, <https://www.motor1.com/news/728264/ferrari-ceo-deepfake-voice-phone/>, <https://www.unboxfuture.com/2026/05/the-rise-of-ai-voice-cloning-scams-in.html>_

- **Stocks Have the Midterm Blues - wsj.com** — The S&P 500 has averaged a decline of roughly 1.1% in September since 1928, the weakest calendar month historically, and that pattern tends to be reinforced in midterm election years, which carry the largest average intra-year drawdown of the four-year presidential cycle at around 17%, typically bottoming in the August-October window as election uncertainty peaks. Despite the seasonal chop, midterm years have still closed positive in roughly 87% of cases since 1950, and the S&P 500 has been higher 12 months after every midterm election on record (19 for 19), with the fourth quarter alone averaging more than a 5% gain. Strategists cite this recurring "sell in September, buy the midterm dip" dynamic as the reason investors are bracing for near-term volatility in 2026 even as many stay bullish into year-end. _Sources: <https://schwabnetwork.com/articles/september-seasonality-and-midterm-elections-put-volatility-back-in-focus>, <https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/the-surprising-truth-about-midterms-and-stocks>, <https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/technical-analysis/sp-500-seasonality-and-presidential-cycles-what-historical-patterns-suggest-for-2026/>_
