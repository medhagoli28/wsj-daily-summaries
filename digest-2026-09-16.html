How a Chinese Hacking Firm Tapped AI to Supercharge Cyber-Spying
I have solid, corroborated details from Bloomberg's reporting (via CyCraft's findings) to write the brief now. Cybersecurity researchers at Taiwan-based CyCraft found that a small Chinese hacking-tool vendor, roughly 10 employees, used ChatGPT to help build a software module to decrypt a Signal messaging database it had stolen from an employee at a Western think tank, according to screenshots reviewed by Bloomberg News. The discovery stemmed from a publicly exposed shared drive containing thousands of Chinese-language screenshots, some as recent as this past February, documenting the firm's development and sale of hacking tools priced between 300,000 and 500,000 yuan (roughly $44,600 to $74,400) to at least four separate hacking groups. Investigators found that activity tied to one of those customer groups overlapped with tactics previously attributed to Mustang Panda, a hacking operation the U.S. Justice Department has linked to the Chinese government. OpenAI said it is committed to identifying and disrupting attempts to abuse its models for such purposes.

Mistral Aims to Grow AI Consumer Base Through Mozilla Partnership
Mistral AI and Mozilla announced a partnership under which Mistral's open-weight models will power Firefox's new "Smart Window" AI browsing assistant, currently in beta and rolling out to users in France and North America first, with the UK and Germany to follow later in 2026. Smart Window helps users navigate complex searches, recall previously visited content, and pull context from open browser tabs, and Mistral is fine-tuning its models on regional languages and dialects to support localized responses. Under the deal, conversations are not stored on Mozilla's servers by default and Mistral has agreed to zero data retention, aligning the partnership with Firefox's privacy-focused positioning as both companies emphasize open-source AI development.

Essay | For the Love of God, Please Put on Headphones
A Wall Street Journal essay by Jamie Waters, published September 15, 2026, argues that public speakerphone use has become rampant, with people blasting work calls, TikToks, and livestreams in cafes, on buses, and on planes; a 2025 survey from NumberBarn found 37% of Americans admit to using speakerphone in public, rising to nearly half of Gen Z, with Miami ranked the worst U.S. city for phone etiquette and New York second. MIT's Sherry Turkle says smartphones trap people in "asocial digital bubbles," while Brown University epidemiologist Erica Walker notes that "personal listening devices are supposed to be personal," making the public broadcasts especially grating. In response, the UK pub chain Wetherspoons has banned speakerphone use in its venues, and United Airlines says it reserves the right to remove or ban repeat offenders.

Oil Futures Rise as More Oil Routes Are Obstructed
Oil futures climbed further this week as renewed attacks choked off key global shipping routes, with Brent crude pushing above $100 a barrel — its highest level in roughly six weeks — after Iranian forces said they targeted vessels near the Strait of Hormuz and Iran-backed Houthi rebels seized a strategic port on Yemen's western coast on September 10, 2026. The Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil and gas supply, has been effectively shut to most shipping since March amid drone, missile, mine and small-boat attacks. The disruption has also hit consumers directly: the U.S. national average diesel price hit a record $5.94 a gallon this week, according to AAA data. Separately, Ukrainian drone strikes have continued to hammer Russian oil infrastructure, including the Black Sea export terminal at Novorossiysk, compounding the supply pressure driving futures higher.

Hedge Funds Are the Wild Card in the Turbulent Bond Market
Hedge funds held roughly $2 trillion in US Treasuries at the start of 2026, more than double their holdings five years earlier and a record share of the market, according to Federal Reserve and Office of Financial Research data; a Fed note from June 2026 found large funds' gross Treasury exposure (cash and derivatives combined) reached about $4 trillion by September 2025, split between roughly $2.4 trillion long and $1.6 trillion short. Much of this growth is driven by the Treasury cash-futures "basis trade," which hit about $830 billion in outstanding positions in September 2025, nearly double its prior peak from early 2020, funded largely through more than $6.6 trillion in repo and prime-brokerage borrowing. Regulators including the Fed, OFR, and Dallas Fed have flagged the concentrated, highly leveraged positioning as a financial-stability concern, warning that a spike in repo rates or a sudden jump in futures margin requirements could force rapid unwinds and amplify volatility across the $31 trillion Treasury market.

Exclusive | How BlackRock Plans to Make the Corporate 401(k) Look More Like a Pension
BlackRock has been pushing to make workplace 401(k) plans function more like traditional pensions by embedding guaranteed lifetime income directly into target-date funds: its LifePath Paycheck product, launched in April 2024, automatically shifts about 10% of a participant's balance into an income-generating allocation starting at age 55, rising to roughly 30% by age 65, with an annuity-purchase window open from age 59½ to 71 through insurers Equitable and Brighthouse Financial. As of January 2025, six employer retirement plans holding a combined $16 billion in assets had adopted the product, making it available to more than 200,000 eligible U.S. workers, at a cost comparable to BlackRock's standard LifePath Index target-date fund (about a 0.14% expense ratio) with no added commissions or surrender fees. BlackRock is also moving to add private equity and private credit into target-date funds in the first half of 2026, targeting allocations of roughly 5% to 20% of a fund, which the firm estimates could boost annual returns by about 50 basis points. Together, the moves aim to give 401(k) savers pension-like features — diversified private-market exposure and a built-in path to guaranteed retirement income — that defined-contribution plans have historically lacked.

Crypto Exchange That Was Hub for Illicit Iran Cash Closes Suddenly
The U.S. Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control sanctioned Dubai-based crypto exchange Shelbit General Trading LLC on August 7, 2026, saying it helped Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps move money and evade sanctions, with IRGC-linked wallets sending more than $1 million in crypto to Shelbit and over $2 million flowing back from Shelbit to Iranian wallets. Treasury said the unlicensed exchange had processed roughly $6.3 billion in transactions between May 2024 and March 2026, despite prior enforcement action by Dubai's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority in January 2025. Shelbit's website had already gone dark and was inoperative for months before the sanctions, leaving customers unable to complete transactions, and it was designated alongside another Iran-linked exchange, Aban Tether, on the same day. The action extended a broader 2026 U.S. crackdown that in June had already sanctioned Iran's largest exchange, Nobitex, and three other Iranian platforms.

Mark Walter’s Financial Empire Was Fueled by Retirement Savers
Federal prosecutors in Manhattan and the SEC are investigating Delaware Life Insurance Co. and Clear Spring Life and Annuity Co., two life insurers controlled by Dodgers and Lakers owner Mark Walter through his Group 1001 holding company, over whether they properly disclosed that ordinary customers' annuity and life-insurance premiums were funneled into private-credit loans backing other parts of Walter's business empire. Delaware Life and Clear Spring received grand jury subpoenas in February 2026, and June 2026 regulatory filings revealed the companies had understated their related-party investments, revising the figure from roughly $1 billion to about $16 billion, or as much as 40% of invested assets according to some reports. The probe runs alongside an earlier inquiry, opened in 2025, into Walter's $362 billion Guggenheim Partners asset-management arm, where he serves as CEO; one insurer has since agreed to cut $6.5 billion in investments amid the fallout. The case follows a whistleblower complaint and centers on whether Walter's retirement-product businesses used everyday savers' money to prop up his broader sports and finance holdings, including the Dodgers.

DOJ Says Binance Customers Used Crypto Exchange to Funnel Iran Oil Money
The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York filed a civil forfeiture complaint on Monday, September 14, 2026, seeking to seize roughly $61 million in cryptocurrency that prosecutors say represents proceeds from black-market sales of sanctioned Iranian oil; the complaint alleges Iran laundered more than $1.5 billion in illicit oil money through crypto overall. Prosecutors named two Chinese entities, Blessed Trust and Hexa Whale, which allegedly used trading accounts on Binance to funnel the funds to Iran's government and its proxies, including the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Binance itself was not charged, and the exchange remains under a three-year compliance monitorship stemming from its 2023 guilty plea to U.S. anti-money-laundering and sanctions violations, which carried $4.3 billion in penalties. The case follows an August 2026 Reuters investigation that found an unlicensed Dubai exchange tied to Iranian sanctions evasion had sent Binance at least $676 million.
