- **How to Press Your Case When Your Airline Seat Gets Downgraded** — Under U.S. Department of Transportation rules, airlines involuntarily downgrading a passenger must refund the fare difference between the class purchased and the class received, and if the downgrade causes a delay of two hours or more, the passenger is entitled to 400% of the one-way fare, up to $1,550. A DOT complaint filed in August 2026 highlighted the gap between rule and practice: a traveler who paid $10,644 for a business-class ticket on American Airlines was downgraded to coach and received only a partial refund, after reports surfaced that American caps downgrade refunds at 40% of the fare difference rather than reimbursing it in full. Consumer advocates recommend passengers get a written reason for the downgrade, photograph their new seat and boarding pass, and escalate unresolved claims to the DOT if the airline doesn't pay out within about seven days. Airlines must issue any refund in cash or by bank transfer, not vouchers, unless the passenger agrees to accept credit instead. _Sources: <https://aviationa2z.com/index.php/2026/08/28/pay-10644-get-coach-american-airlines-downgrade-refund-sparks-dot-complaint/>, <https://www.airtraveler.club/news/american-airlines-downgrade-refund-40-percent-cap/>, <https://www.cnn.com/travel/airline-seat-downgrade-heres-what-to-do>_
