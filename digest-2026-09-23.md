- **Americans Are About to Lose Access to Cheap Gray-Market Prescriptions From Canada** — Starting October 22, 2026, U.S. Customs and Border Protection will begin requiring formal customs entry for mailed drug shipments that fall under FDA jurisdiction, a procedural change expected to effectively shut down the gray-market pipeline that lets an estimated two million-plus Americans buy prescription drugs from Canadian and other foreign mail-order pharmacies. The arrangement has persisted for years because customs largely declined to enforce the technical illegality of these imports, letting patients pay a fraction of U.S. list prices — one patient cited in reporting pays about $10 a week for a drug that costs roughly $1,100 a week without insurance in the U.S. A study by the Campaign for Personal Prescription Information found average savings of about 65% across ten commonly prescribed drugs bought this way. GLP-1 weight-loss and diabetes drugs are among the medications expected to be hit hardest by the new screening rule. _Sources: <https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/pharmacy/millions-of-americans-could-lose-access-to-cheap-canadian-prescriptions-next-month-3-notes/>, <https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/sep/27/millions-of-americans-get-discount-prescriptions-f/>, <https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/prescription-panic-will-canadian-drug-imports-stop-on-october-22>_

- **LinkedIn Tightens Verification Rules to Fight Fake Recruiters** — LinkedIn began rolling out new anti-fraud tools on September 23, 2026, aimed at curbing fake profiles, invented work histories, and impersonated recruiters and companies. Companies can now remove accounts that falsely claim employment from their official page and from search results tied to that page, and LinkedIn is piloting a requirement that members verify employment—such as via a company email address—before linking their profile to a business page.
