Stocks Rise to Cap Week of Increasing Yields, Volatile Oil Prices
U.S. stocks closed out their first winning week in three on Friday, September 25, 2026, as easing oil prices took pressure off Treasury yields; the S&P 500 rose 0.5% to pull within 0.7% of its record high, the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained about 300 points (0.9%), and the Nasdaq Composite added 0.5%. The rally followed a stretch in which Brent crude had dropped below $98 a barrel and the 10-year Treasury yield had eased back from levels near its highest since 2007. The reprieve proved short-lived, however, as oil prices turned higher again the following Monday, September 28, pushing crude to about $93.16 a barrel intraday and pulling stocks lower as yields climbed once more.

The Rise of the Almost-Premium Credit Card
As top-tier travel cards like the Chase Sapphire Reserve and American Express Platinum have pushed annual fees to $795–$895, card issuers have rolled out a new "almost-premium" tier priced between roughly $325 and $395 a year, including the Amex Gold ($325), a Citi/American Airlines co-brand card ($350), and the Capital One Venture X and Alaska Atmos Rewards Summit card (both $395). These cards offer a slice of premium perks—such as airport lounge access or dining credits—at under half the cost of the top-tier products, effectively occupying the price point premium cards held about a decade ago. Analysts covering the trend note that the fee savings often don't fully offset the reduced benefits, meaning many cardholders would come out ahead with a no-annual-fee card instead.

Exclusive | Blackstone’s Top Private-Equity Executive Is Preparing to Leave Firm
Joseph Baratta, Blackstone's global head of private equity, is leaving the firm by the end of 2026 after 28 years, according to an announcement made September 25, 2026. Baratta had overseen the unit, which manages roughly $450 billion in assets, and Blackstone does not plan to fill his role, instead distributing his responsibilities among the leaders of its various PE verticals. His exit coincides with the departure of real estate chief Nadeem Meghji, with the two units together having managed nearly 60% of Blackstone's $1.3 trillion in total assets; growth-equity head Jon Korngold left the firm earlier this year as well. CEO Steve Schwarzman and President Jon Gray framed the moves as a shift toward a broader management team rather than a roster dominated by a few star dealmakers.

Cracks in Oracle’s AI Data-Center Build-Out Appear in Massive New Mexico Project
Oracle sent a "force majeure" notice on September 24, 2026 to the developer of Project Jupiter, a 1,400-acre, 2.45-gigawatt AI data center campus under construction in Doña Ana County, New Mexico, that is part of the Stargate buildout with OpenAI; the notice would let Oracle delay rent-like payments if the facility misses its 2028 target date. The delays stem from a stalled natural gas pipeline meant to fuel Bloom Energy fuel cells on-site and the New Mexico Environment Department's failure to appoint a new independent hearing officer for the project's air-quality permit. Oracle's stock fell more than 3% following the news, even as the company maintained that "Project Jupiter remains on our planned schedule" and it is "fully committed to New Mexico."
