## Personal Finance

- **Yes, It’s OK to Ask Your Wedding Guests for Money to Help Buy a Home** — Nearly 40% of engaged couples registering through Zola in 2026 have added a home down-payment fund to their wedding registry in place of traditional gifts, according to the platform's 2026 First Look Report, and 91% of couples now say asking guests for cash is "totally acceptable." A LendingTree survey found 48% of newlywed homeowners who made a down payment in the past two years asked wedding guests for cash toward the purchase instead of registry items, while 35% said their wedding delayed their home purchase and 36% said they put down less than planned because of wedding costs. The trend reflects persistent affordability pressure, with 41% of couples who set up a cash fund saying they intend to use it toward buying a home or moving. _Sources: <https://www.housingwire.com/articles/newlyweds-asking-for-down-payment-help-over-wedding-gifts-lendingtree-survey/>, <https://www.theknot.com/content/house-fund-wedding-registry>_
- **The Surprise Social Security Benefit That Goes to Parents Who Have Kids Late** — Parents who have children after age 62 can claim Social Security's "child's benefit," which pays a dependent child up to 50% of the retired or disabled parent's monthly benefit (or up to 75% if the parent has died), on top of the parent's own retirement check, as long as the child is unmarried and under 18 (or under 19 and still in high school, or disabled before age 22). The perk is little used — only about 1% of Social Security beneficiaries, roughly 714,000 people, are children of retired workers — largely because few 62-plus retirees have minor children. A family benefit cap of about 150% to 180% of the parent's full retirement amount applies when multiple dependents claim at once; in one illustrative case cited by 247wallst.com, a 67-year-old father with a 12-year-old could trigger about $1,400 a month in child benefits. _Sources: <https://money.usnews.com/money/retirement/articles/dont-miss-this-social-security-benefit-for-older-parents>, <https://finance.yahoo.com/news/older-parent-dont-miss-little-163000752.html>, <https://247wallst.com/personal-finance/2026/05/15/how-a-67-year-old-father-with-a-12-year-old-can-trigger-1400-a-month-in-childrens-social-security-benefits/>_
- **Why Are So Many Adults Cutting Off Their Parents?** — I have enough concrete, well-sourced data points from non-WSJ outlets to write the brief. Roughly a quarter to a third of American adults now report being estranged from a close family member, with a 2025 YouGov poll of about 4,400 U.S. adults finding 38% say they no longer have a relationship with at least one immediate family member, up from 27% in a 2020 survey, and a 2024 Harris Poll putting the figure at 35%; specific to parents, about 10% of respondents in the earlier survey said they had cut off a parent or child, with estrangement from fathers (26%) far more common than from mothers (6%). Psychologists and researchers point to several drivers behind the rise, including "value dissimilarity" between parents and adult children, histories of emotional abuse, rising individualism and divorce rates, and the amplifying effect of social media, where content framing no-contact decisions as protecting one's mental health has proliferated on platforms like Instagram and TikTok. Reconciliation is common, however: studies cited by researchers found 81% of estranged mothers and 69% of estranged fathers eventually reconnected with their adult children. _Sources: <https://knowablemagazine.org/content/article/mind/2026/causes-of-family-estrangement>, <https://thehill.com/blogs/blog-briefing-room/4104138-one-quarter-of-adult-children-estranged-from-a-parent/>, <https://www.newsweek.com/parenting-family-mom-dad-children-estrangement-cut-off-1739384>_
