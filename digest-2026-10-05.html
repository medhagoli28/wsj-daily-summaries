Exclusive | Robotics Startup RobCo Hits $1 Billion Valuation
Munich-based RobCo, a German industrial-robotics startup, has reached a valuation above $1 billion (€892.4 million) after a $40 million transaction combining new investment with an employee secondary share sale, announced around October 5, 2026. The valuation has roughly doubled in nine months since RobCo's January 2026 Series C round, which raised €89.2 million (about $100 million) co-led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and Lingotto. Existing backers Sequoia, Lightspeed, Greenfield, Kindred, Lingotto, and Promus Ventures joined new investors Cherry Ventures and European Tech Collective in the latest deal. Founded in 2020 by CEO Roman Hölzl, RobCo builds an "Autonomous Manufacturing Platform" pairing modular robot hardware with physical-AI software for tasks like machine tending, palletizing, and welding, and has deployed over 1,000 robots to industrial customers including BMW.

Deutsche Telekom Bets on AI to Cut Costs, Boost Revenue
Deutsche Telekom announced on October 5, 2026 that it expects AI and automation to generate around €2.5 billion in indirect cost savings by 2030, building on roughly €1.1 billion in gross savings targeted outside the U.S. by 2027 versus a 2023 baseline. The company also projects AI-related revenue outside the U.S. to grow from about €250 million in 2026 to around €800 million by 2030. Operationally, its "Frag Magenta" AI chatbot handled roughly 2.6 million customer service interactions in the first half of 2026, and Deutsche Telekom plans to reinvest part of the additional 2027 savings into expanding Germany's fiber-optic network.
MARKETS
Bank Stocks Are Haunted by the Ghosts of 2023
Regional bank stocks tumbled on October 17, 2025, after Zions Bancorporation and Western Alliance Bancorp disclosed bad-loan exposure tied to alleged fraud, with Zions taking a roughly $50 million charge-off on two commercial loans originated by its California Bank & Trust unit and Western Alliance disclosing litigation against borrower Cantor Group V over falsified title documents and diverted funds. Zions shares fell more than 8% and closed down about 13%, Western Alliance closed down roughly 11%, and the S&P Regional Banks Select Industry Index sank about 6.3% in a single session, its worst drop since April. The sell-off, which also hit Jefferies, drew immediate comparisons to the March 2023 collapse of Silicon Valley Bank and Signature Bank, reviving investor worries about hidden credit risk in banks' loan books, including exposure to the opaque private-credit market. Both banks characterized the losses as isolated incidents rather than signs of systemic deterioration.
MARKETS
Equities Won’t Be Dragged by Spike In Bond Yields
The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to roughly 5.3% in late September and early October 2026, its highest level since 2002, driven by strong economic growth expectations, rising energy costs, heavy corporate debt issuance to fund AI expansion, and a Fed rate hike last month that broke a three-year pause. Despite this surge — yields are up about 80 basis points over the past three months — the S&P 500 has held up, sitting roughly 1% below its all-time high and up slightly month-to-date as of October 5. Analysts including JPMorgan attribute the resilience to strong corporate fundamentals, noting interest expense as a share of corporate profits remains near 50-year lows even as borrowing costs climb; JPMorgan added that the earlier equity selloff tied to the yield spike "will not persist."
MARKETS
U.S. Treasuries Face Renewed Term-Premium Pressure
The 30-year U.S. Treasury yield climbed to roughly 5.5%, its highest level since 2004, during a bond-market selloff in late September 2026, with the 10-year yield also pushing above 5.2%. The move reflects a rising "term premium" — the extra compensation investors demand for holding longer-dated debt — which the San Francisco Fed's model now pegs at around 1.4 percentage points on the 10-year note, up sharply from near zero earlier in the year. Traders have been pricing in over a 72% chance of another Federal Reserve rate hike at its October meeting, following a 12-0 FOMC vote to raise rates 25 basis points, as persistent inflation concerns and heavy Treasury issuance weigh on demand for long-duration bonds.
MARKETS
‘A Fabulous Debt’ Review: In the Name of Good Credit
"A Fabulous Debt: The Epic Story of How Bonds Built the Modern World," by Financial Times global finance correspondent Robin Wigglesworth, was published September 29, 2026, and traces roughly 1,000 years of bond-market history from medieval Venice to today; it recounts how Venice's failed 12th-century war against Constantinople turned an emergency loan into permanent, tradable public debt — creating the world's first bond market — and follows that innovation through financing everything from the Crusades to Netflix and AI data centers. The book argues bonds have grown bigger than the stock market and now rival the global banking system in influence, while linking overleveraged bond issuance to both the 2008 financial crisis and the roughly $40 trillion U.S. national debt today. Reviewers, including The Times and Publishers Weekly, have praised it for its narrative pace and vivid historical anecdotes.
