- **Spending on AI Is Becoming Almost Impossible for Businesses to Budget** — Only 11% of nearly 400 surveyed businesses said they could accurately forecast their AI spending, according to reporting published October 5, 2026, with 62% of surveyed companies spending more than $10 million annually on new AI tools; researchers from Stanford, Carnegie Mellon, UC Berkeley, and Microsoft Research tested AI models on more than 6,800 tasks and found that in roughly 32% of scenarios, cheaper models ended up costing more than premium ones once token usage was factored in. Unlike traditional software licenses, AI tools are typically billed per token consumed, and the same task can require wildly different numbers of tokens depending on how a model approaches it, making costs swing unpredictably even when usage patterns look similar. A PYMNTS Intelligence report cited in the same coverage found that of 60 U.S. companies examined, only about 20% of 437 AI deployments had been embedded into regular workflows, underscoring how early-stage and volatile enterprise AI cost structures remain. _Sources: <https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/businesses-finding-it-harder-to-predict-ai-spending/>_
- **Tech Companies Roll Out Cuddly Mascots to Ease AI Anxiety** — Big tech firms are rolling out cartoonish mascots to make AI assistants feel less intimidating as adoption accelerates: Meta's "Muse" assistant is personified by a rosy-cheeked, egg-shaped character nicknamed Jolly, Anthropic represents Claude with a pixelated orange crab called Clawd, Microsoft gave Copilot an animated blob character named Mico, and OpenAI introduced bow-tie-and-glasses-wearing characters for its "dots" AI agents. Critics, including psychologists cited in coverage, warn the cute personas risk making unreliable or opaque AI systems seem more convincing and trustworthy than they actually are. _Sources: <https://fortune.com/2026/10/02/meta-openai-ai-agent-mascots-jolly-dots-trust/>, <https://www.aol.com/articles/openai-meta-why-ai-companies-152514000.html>_

- **Community Banks Swing Back at Trump Regulators Over Crypto Charters** — The Independent Community Bankers of America sued the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency on October 2, 2026, in D.C. federal court, naming the OCC and Comptroller Jonathan Gould as defendants, arguing the agency exceeded its authority by granting national trust bank charters to crypto firms that neither take deposits nor primarily act as fiduciaries. ICBA says the OCC has approved 21 trust banks under the Trump administration, at least 13 of them crypto companies, including Circle, Ripple, BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets, Paxos, Coinbase, Crypto.com, and the Trump-family-linked World Liberty Trust Company. These charters let crypto firms gain federal-bank credibility and faster payment settlement while avoiding FDIC insurance, capital requirements, and Community Reinvestment Act obligations. ICBA CEO Rebeca Romero Rainey said the approvals let companies "conduct substantial non-fiduciary activities" beyond what Congress authorized. _Sources: <https://decrypt.co/380017/banking-group-sues-block-crypto-side-door-banking>, <https://cointelegraph.com/news/community-banks-sue-occ-over-trust-bank-charters-of-crypto-firms>, <https://news.bloomberglaw.com/crypto/community-banks-sue-occ-over-expanding-trust-charters-to-crypto>_
