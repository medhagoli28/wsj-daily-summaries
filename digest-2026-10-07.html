AI Solved One Math Problem and Everyone Freaked Out. It Just Cracked Hundreds More.
I have enough concrete detail to write the brief now. Since October 2025, AI tools—most notably OpenAI's GPT-5 and successor models—have helped move roughly 100 of the late mathematician Paul Erdős's open problems into the "solved" column, according to a public tracker maintained by Fields Medalist Terence Tao; most of these resolutions amount to AI surfacing forgotten or obscure solutions already buried in the literature rather than genuine new proofs. The saga began when Columbia researchers Mehtaab Sawhney and Mark Sellke used ChatGPT to dig up solutions or partial solutions to roughly 20 Erdős problems, and escalated in mid-2026 when OpenAI said an internal model (and later an unreleased model called Astra) helped crack more significant, decades-old problems, including disproving the Erdős unit distance conjecture and resolving a problem open for 60 years in about 80 minutes. Tao has cautioned that most of these are "lowest-hanging fruit" solvable with existing techniques rather than profound mathematical breakthroughs, and that an earlier October 2025 claim that GPT-5 "solved" ten Erdős problems was walked back after it turned out the model had merely found pre-existing solutions, not made original discoveries.

A Data-Center Crunch is Coming
US data center capacity is set to tighten sharply as AI demand outpaces the grid's ability to keep up: industry trackers project power demand nearly doubling to roughly 18 GW in 2026 and climbing toward 35 GW by 2027, while only about 5 GW of the 12-16 GW of new capacity planned for 2026 is actually under active construction, leaving the rest delayed or canceled. The binding constraint isn't chips but electrical equipment — transformers now take three to five years to deliver and switchgear is sold out through 2028 — pushing some analysts to estimate a supply gap as large as 57 GW by 2029. Hyperscalers including Microsoft, Amazon, and Google have already disclosed project delays tied to power and grid-interconnection bottlenecks rather than GPU availability. The crunch is reshaping investment, with billions flowing into transformer and turbine manufacturers and utilities as the industry races to add megawatts fast enough to match AI compute growth.

Becton Dickinson to Invest $19 Billion in U.S. in Exchange for Tariff Relief
Becton Dickinson, the Franklin Lakes, N.J.-based medical device maker, agreed to invest $19 billion in U.S. manufacturing over several years in exchange for relief from future tariffs, becoming the first major U.S. medical device company to strike such a deal with the Trump administration. The company will put roughly $3 billion toward expanding domestic manufacturing, including over $1 billion in Nebraska, aiming to raise U.S. production of essential medical consumables by about 5 billion units annually and bringing the domestically supplied share to roughly 80%; it also plans to manufacture all U.S.-used needles domestically with American-made steel. Becton Dickinson said it is not yet quantifying the deal's financial impact since the tariff relief terms depend on future policy changes and the company meeting agreed milestones. The announcement came October 6, 2026.

AI Agents Are Offering to Run Your Life. Should You Let Them?
I now have solid independent, non-WSJ reporting with concrete numbers to write the brief. AI startups and major tech firms are racing to launch "agentic" personal assistants that handle real-world tasks like booking travel, managing email, and completing purchases rather than just answering questions. At OpenAI's DevDay on September 29, 2026, the company unveiled "dots," an always-on agent accessible via chat, Slack, or Microsoft Teams that is bundled free with its Pro and Business Premium plans (also offered standalone alongside a new $500-per-month ChatGPT tier); it requires explicit user permission for sensitive actions like password changes or software installs and is not yet available in the EU, UK, or Switzerland due to data-privacy rules. Dots arrived roughly three weeks after Meta's rival agent "Muse," which operates inside WhatsApp to send emails, book travel, and fill out forms, and alongside the invite-only "Instinct" assistant, which runs via text message with no dedicated app. OpenAI reported 1.2 billion weekly ChatGPT users and 2.5 million business customers at the time of the announcement.

New York City Council held a marathon hearing on October 5, 2026, questioning executives from OpenAI, Meta, Anthropic, and Google, alongside former AI-lab employees, as part of its review of roughly 10 proposed bills to regulate AI development and deployment in the city. Surprise, Your Child Has a ‘Trump Account.’ Here’s What to Know.
Trump Accounts, a federal savings program created by the "One Big Beautiful Bill" signed into law on July 4, 2025, launched for enrollment on July 4, 2026, giving every U.S. citizen child born between January 1, 2025, and December 31, 2028, a $1,000 seed deposit from the Treasury, invested in low-cost index funds and growing tax-deferred until age 18. As of early October 2026, the IRS reports about 4 million children have been signed up, with roughly 1 million families having claimed the $1,000 contribution, either online at trumpaccounts.gov or via IRS Form 4547 filed with a 2025 tax return. Treasury officials have also floated auto-enrolling eligible children by default, a change that could add as many as 60 million accounts. Parents or guardians can contribute up to $5,000 per year on top of the government seed money.

Hackers Use Chinese AI Tool to Hit South Korean Banks, Exposing New Risk
South Korea's National Police Agency is investigating cyberattacks on at least seven financial firms that used an open-source Chinese AI hacking tool called Artex, which taps external AI models (including Anthropic's Claude Opus, OpenAI's GPT and China's DeepSeek) to find vulnerabilities and plan intrusions; the breaches, which came to light around September 30, 2026, exposed personal data for roughly 68,000 people. Shinhan Bank confirmed about 25,000 affected customers, KB Kookmin Bank saw credit card data for 119,000 clients compromised, and Hana Bank reported a limited breach via its sales-support system; stolen data included annual income figures and personal-loan limits. Investigators have traced the intrusions to more than two dozen IP addresses across roughly a dozen countries, including the US, Japan and Germany, and no suspects have yet been identified; officials called it the first known case of AI agents being used to hack the financial sector.

Bond Yields Are Surging Around the World—but Not in China
Benchmark government bond yields have surged to multi-decade highs across most major economies in 2026: the U.S. 10-year Treasury briefly touched 5.04% on September 15, its highest since 2007, Japan's 10-year yield hit its highest since 1996, UK 30-year gilt yields reached their highest since 1998, and France's and Germany's 10-year yields hit their highest since 2008 and 2009 respectively. China's 10-year yield, by contrast, sits near record lows around 1.69%, as the country remains mired in a multi-year deflationary stretch tied to its 2021 property-market crash. The global rise is largely attributed to investor concern over expanding government debt and spending, compounded by bets that major central banks will keep interest rates higher for longer.
