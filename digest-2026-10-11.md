- **Courts Grapple With Problem of People Betting on the Outcome of Jury Cases** — Prediction markets like Polymarket and Kalshi have begun hosting betting contracts on the outcomes of high-profile criminal trials, drawing concern from judges, defense attorneys, and legal scholars over jury integrity and courtroom fairness. Polymarket's market on the murder case of Massachusetts mother Lindsay Clancy drew more than $600,000 in trading volume, including bets placed even after a mistrial, while a market on Tyler Robinson, the Utah man charged with killing activist Charlie Kirk, has attracted over $300,000 in wagers. In the Lockerbie bombing trial, the judge took the unusual step of instructing jurors not to "visit, consult, or participate" in any betting or prediction market tied to the case, naming Kalshi and Polymarket specifically. - **When Companies Stop Driving a Hard Bargain, This Fed Official Starts Worrying** — Beth Hammack, president of the Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, said this week that when businesses stop negotiating hard on prices, it signals they still hold enough pricing power to keep inflation elevated — a warning sign for policy. Speaking Monday, October 12, 2026, Hammack cited visits with local business leaders, including industrial-hardware maker Jergens in Cleveland, where she was told that price has slipped to third in importance for customers, behind quality and speed, suggesting sellers retain leverage to keep raising prices. She said inflation has broadened beyond energy costs and tariffs into how companies generally set prices, and argued the Fed should move toward more restrictive policy relatively quickly, favoring smaller, more frequent rate increases over waiting for a bigger one later. Hammack added she hopes to cool demand and inflation without severely damaging the job market, but stressed that current policy isn't yet tight enough to return inflation to the Fed's 2% target. _Sources: <https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/feds-hammack-hope-to-cool-demand-and-inflation-without-job-market-pain-4945244>, <https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-12/fed-s-hammack-says-smaller-frequent-rate-adjustments-preferable>, <https://finance.biggo.com/news/2741e1a8-9bba-4636-84de-9ff4517e404e>_
- **Why Economists Think Higher Bond Yields Are Here to Stay** — The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5% on September 14, 2026 — a threshold not durably breached since 2007 — before touching a roughly 19-to-24-year high near 5.26%-5.37% by late September, driven by sticky inflation, heavy federal bond issuance, an AI-investment boom, and a ballooning national debt. The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget estimates that a 10-year yield stuck near 5% (about 80 basis points above the CBO's prior baseline) would push annual federal interest costs to roughly $2.7 trillion over the coming decade, more than projected spending on Social Security or Medicare. PIMCO's chief investment officer Dan Ivascyn told the Financial Times in early October 2026 that the 10-year yield could still climb toward 6% — a level last seen around 2000 — citing fuel costs, inflation, and the growing public debt load, while a Bank of America fund-manager survey released October 13, 2026 found 37% of investors would shift from stocks into bonds if yields reached that 6% mark. Economists broadly describe the move as structural rather than cyclical, meaning they expect borrowing costs to stay elevated for years rather than reverting to the low-rate norms of the 2010s. _Sources: <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/bofa-survey-10-year-treasury-yield-bonds.html>, <https://fortune.com/2026/09/19/treasury-yields-cbo-forecasts-us-debt-interest-costs-deficits/>, <https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/14/investing/bond-yields-market-turmoil>_
