## Tech

- **Meta and Apple, Longtime Foes, Team Up to Put Open-Weight AI on Macs** — Meta and Apple announced a collaboration on October 15, 2026, to run Meta's new open-weight AI model, Muse Spark Open, on Apple's Mac hardware, marking a notable thaw between two companies that have frequently clashed over privacy and app-store policy. Muse Spark Open is an 816-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model built for coding and agentic, long-horizon reasoning tasks, released under a license permitting limited commercial use. Apple updated its MLX machine-learning framework to optimize the model for the Mac Studio with M5 Ultra chip, and customers will be able to link two to four Mac Studios together to run the model locally for coding, video analysis, and other agentic workloads. Running the model on-device rather than in the cloud is pitched by both companies as improving both data security and cost compared with cloud-based AI services. _Sources: <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/apple-meta-partner-ai.html>, <https://www.axios.com/2026/10/15/apple-and-meta-bury-the-hatchet-for-ai>, <https://www.cultofmac.com/news/meta-apple-muse-spark-open-weight-ai-mac>_
- **The Engineer Accused of Bugging OpenAI’s Top-Secret Conference Room** — Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national who had worked as an OpenAI engineer since July 2025, was arrested on October 9, 2026, and charged with using a device to intercept oral communications after a recording device was found plugged into a USB port on a TV in an OpenAI executive conference room, concealed in toilet paper; the device was discovered September 30 and determined to have been installed around September 28. Surveillance footage reportedly showed Alfozan near the room on multiple dates despite not being authorized to enter it, and prosecutors say he used an AI chatbot in August and September to ask about OpenAI's building addresses, meeting-room locations, capacities, and calendars, while a chatbot linked to his spouse allegedly queried how to build, install, and remotely access a recording device. An unattended bag containing his wallet, ID, and a second recording device was also found at OpenAI's offices. OpenAI terminated his employment the day of his arrest; he is also reported to be a partner at the Saudi venture firm Raed Ventures. _Sources: <https://sfstandard.com/2026/10/13/openai-engineer-arrested-alleged-bugging-executive-conference-room/>, <https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/14/openai-engineer-charged-after-boardroom-bug-found-wrapped-in-toilet-paper/5303000>, <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/openai-alfozan-recording-raed-ventures.html>_

- **The Tiny Robot Designed to Solve a Massive Underground Crisis** — A collapsed sewer pipe outside Washington, D.C. spilled more than 240 million gallons of raw sewage into the Potomac River in January 2026, likely the largest sewage spill in U.S. history, out of an estimated 75,000 such spills the EPA says occur nationwide each year. In response, the EU-funded PIPEON project, backed by roughly $9 million, is developing miniature robots designed to crawl through confined, hazardous sewer pipes to inspect for cracks, blockages, and structural wear without requiring excavation. The robots aim to let utilities detect and fix leaks and damage in aging underground water and sewage infrastructure before failures like the Potomac spill occur. Similar mini-robot efforts are also being tested to find and repair water pipe leaks without digging up roads. _Sources: <https://www.foxnews.com/tech/mini-robots-detect-fix-water-pipe-leaks-without-digging>, <https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/new-tiny-robots-promise-to-fix-underground-water-pipe-leakage-without-excavation>_

## Markets & Finance

- **How to Invest for the Middle Class Blues** — The underlying economic story is that September 2026 CPI rose 3.7% year-over-year, an acceleration from the prior month driven largely by rising gasoline and diesel prices, and inflation is now outpacing wage gains for the first time in roughly three years; chief economist Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union called it "a true financial squeeze," noting that living standards for middle-, working-class, and working-poor households are currently declining. The squeeze also has a longer arc: real average weekly earnings for the private middle-class wage cohort now sit around $1,083, still below the roughly $1,140 peak that cohort reached in the early 1970s. Regionally, states including New York, California, and Maryland have seen the weakest middle-class wage growth relative to inflation. The accompanying boost to Social Security benefits (tied to the same September inflation data) is set to take effect for 2027 payments. _Sources: <https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/insight/inflation-now-outpacing-wages-squeezing-middle-class-budgets/gm-GM4EAFD1AB>, <https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/middle-class-americans-living-standards-in-decline-at-this-point-as-inflation-outpaces-wage-gains-143939724.html>, <https://www.hppr.org/npr-news/2026-10-14/inflation-sizzled-in-september-and-social-security-benefits-will-get-a-boost>_

- **For Stocks, the Future Ain’t What It Used to Be** — Now I have enough concrete, non-WSJ-sourced facts to write the brief. A Wall Street Journal investing-newsletter column by Jason Zweig, published October 13, 2026 and titled "For Stocks, the Future Ain't What It Used to Be," draws on Roger Ibbotson's new CFA Institute monograph "Exponential Wealth: Centuries of Stock and Bond Returns" to caution investors against assuming past performance will repeat; the book, marking the 50th anniversary of the original SBBI return series, finds that one dollar invested in U.S. large-cap stocks a century ago grew to $14,751, a 10.1% annualized return, or roughly 800 times its value after accounting for 2.9% average annual inflation. The monograph introduces a new "Ibbotson Equity and Bond Indices" data series as successor to the discontinued SBBI data and extends the analysis to pre-1926 U.S. history and centuries of global market data. The column's thrust, per commentary referencing it, is that such historical averages are difficult for individual investors to actually capture and should not be extrapolated directly into forward return expectations. _Sources: <https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/foundation/2026/exponential-wealth-centuries-stocks-bond-returns>, <https://getscuttlebutt.substack.com/p/roger-ibbotson-a-dollar-in-us-stocks>, <https://ritholtz.com/2026/10/10-wednesday-am-reads-394/>_

- **Inflation Runs Hot, Stocks Go Cold** — The U.S. Labor Department reported on October 14, 2026, that the September Consumer Price Index rose 3.7% year-over-year, up from 3.4% in August and above the 3.6% economists expected, with core CPI (excluding food and energy) at 2.6%. Stocks tumbled in response as traders priced in a greater chance the Federal Reserve would need to keep raising rates: the S&P 500 fell 1.11% to 7,718.94 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.88% to 26,692.87, each logging their worst day since July, while the Russell 2000 slid 2.2%.
