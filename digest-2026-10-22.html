Meta Is Getting Creative to Fund Its Massive AI Investments
Meta sold $25 billion in investment-grade bonds in a jumbo offering in late April/early May 2026, its second such mega-deal in six months after a record $30 billion sale in 2025, as it raised its 2026 AI capital-expenditure guidance to $125 billion–$145 billion. Beyond public debt, Meta has turned to off-balance-sheet financing through special-purpose vehicles tied to individual data centers, including roughly $30 billion raised via a structure linked to Blue Owl Capital, plus joint ventures like the Hyperion data center project in Louisiana (about $27 billion in building costs) and long-term leases. As of June 30, 2026, Meta's total bonds outstanding stood at $84.0 billion, and analysts estimate Big Tech firms collectively will issue around $540 billion in AI-related debt this year, prompting some concern about circular financing arrangements in the sector. Meta's stock fell roughly 9.5% after the spending plan was announced.

Exclusive | Senators Express ‘Grave Concerns,’ Launch Inquiry into Google’s AI Push in Schools
U.S. Senators Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) and Josh Hawley (R-MO) sent a letter to Google and Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai on October 21, 2026, demanding answers about the company's aggressive marketing of AI tools, including Gemini, to schoolchildren, citing "grave concern" that the push is harming students' academic performance and wellbeing. The letter asks why AI features were set as the default on school-issued devices, what Google knows about potential harms to children, and what promotional tactics it used to expand AI adoption in classrooms, and gives Google until November 2, 2026 to respond. The senators' inquiry follows reporting that cited more than 60 interviews with teachers, parents, students, and clinicians describing declining test scores and students' reduced ability to complete work without AI assistance.

Exclusive | AI Just Found a Bug That Puts up to 70% of the World’s Websites at Risk
The PHP Foundation issued emergency patches on October 21, 2026 for two critical vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, which allow unauthenticated remote code execution and were rated 9.2 out of 10 on the CVSS severity scale; because PHP powers an estimated 70% of the world's websites, the flaws were described as among the most severe ever found. The bugs were uncovered by security researchers using advanced AI models in a project involving OpenAI, and a co-founder of Google's Wiz cybersecurity team called it one of the most severe AI-assisted vulnerability discoveries to date. In response, government agencies in France, Denmark, Finland, and the Netherlands took public-facing websites offline as a precaution, Cloudflare's CTO reported early exploitation attempts emerging, and the U.S. National Security Agency urged organizations to patch immediately or isolate internet-facing applications if they could not update right away.

Why AI’s Deepest Mind Believes the World Is About to Change Radically
Demis Hassabis, Google DeepMind's co-founder and CEO, told The Wall Street Journal Magazine in October 2026 that he now expects artificial general intelligence within two to three years, down sharply from the five-to-ten-year estimate he gave at Davos in January and the "2030, plus or minus a year" timeline he cited at Google I/O in May. He defines AGI as a system matching the full cognitive range of the human brain, and frames the shift as a "species-level transition" with little room for error, warning of risks from a competitive "race to the bottom" among AI labs even as he predicts the technology could eventually enable "radical abundance" and help cure disease. His accelerating timeline reflects recent breakthroughs he's seen inside DeepMind's models rather than a single new product launch.

Healthcare Stocks Are Rallying, and Flashing a Warning for the Rest of the Market
Healthcare stocks led the S&P 500 to a record close on October 20, 2026, with the sector hitting an all-time high as Eli Lilly jumped 4.7% to a record; the move came as investors rotated out of AI and semiconductor names after the Nasdaq fell 4.5% and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF dropped 10.5% the prior week amid concerns over AI spending and stretched valuations. Analysts note the rotation reflects a broader shift toward "defensive" sectors with steadier earnings and lower valuations, which some read as a signal of growing investor caution about the durability of the AI-driven rally elsewhere in the market. Oil prices also retreated the same day, adding to the risk-on tone even as the healthcare-led gains were framed as a hedge against tech weakness rather than pure optimism.

U.S. Stocks Rise as SpaceX Spurs AI Relief Rally
The S&P 500 rose about 0.4% to close at roughly 7,690 and the Nasdaq Composite gained about 1.1% to around 26,431 on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, as a strong earnings report from SpaceX eased investor worries about an AI-spending-driven selloff. SpaceX, which also owns xAI and X after a February 2026 acquisition, posted its first quarterly profit since going public in June 2026, with net income of $4.40 billion versus a $1.05 billion loss a year earlier and revenue up 161% to $14.6 billion, driven largely by renting out computing power to AI firms. Elon Musk also said he would back a potential merger between SpaceX and Tesla. The relief rally was reinforced by Super Micro Computer, which issued preliminary first-quarter results well above its own guidance (revenue of $18.5–$19.5 billion versus a prior $14.5–$15.5 billion forecast), lifting chip and AI-infrastructure names such as Dell, Hewlett Packard Enterprise, CoreWeave and Nebius.

Exclusive | How Much Can You Safely Spend in Retirement? A 401(k) Giant Offers Guidance
Capital Group, which manages roughly $2.9 trillion in assets and is a major 401(k) recordkeeper, published new retirement withdrawal guidance on October 21, 2026, offering retirees two drawdown strategies instead of the traditional fixed 4% rule. For predictable income, it recommends a fixed initial withdrawal rate of 4% to 4.5%, which would generate $40,000 to $45,000 in year one from a $1 million portfolio, with the dollar amount adjusted annually for inflation. For retirees who can tolerate fluctuating income, Capital Group suggests a variable approach starting at 5% to 5.5% of the portfolio, with payments moving up or down each year based on account performance. The firm says the higher variable rate is sustainable because annual withdrawals adjust with market swings rather than staying fixed.

Four Financial Lessons From the Hedge-Fund Divorce Captivating Wall Street
John and Laura Overdeck, married 24 years and co-founders of quant hedge fund Two Sigma, are locked in a New Jersey divorce trial that could become one of the largest settlements in U.S. history. John has offered $723 million based on a 2016 postnuptial agreement, valuing his Two Sigma stake at roughly $4.9 billion, while Laura is seeking $6.2 billion, or 35% of the firm's value, and is trying to have the postnup thrown out as signed under pressure. The case, overseen by Judge Bruce Buechler, has also featured sanctions against Laura for alleged discovery abuses, including accessing John's private emails and deleting phone evidence, and a protective order shielding Two Sigma's proprietary trading strategies from public disclosure. The couple married in 2002 without a prenuptial agreement, and Two Sigma manages roughly $80 billion in assets.

Exclusive | Larry Ellison Went on Borrowing Spree to Bankroll His Son’s Warner Bros. Bid
Larry Ellison personally guaranteed $40.4 billion of the equity financing behind Paramount Skydance's roughly $110 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery, pledging shares of Oracle, Tesla, SpaceX and Paramount as collateral to secure billions in loans from banks including Citigroup, HSBC, JPMorgan, UBS and BNP Paribas rather than selling his stock outright. In September 2026, Oracle disclosed that Ellison had scrapped an earlier plan to sell about $7.5 billion of Oracle stock, opting instead to borrow against his holdings to avoid triggering capital gains taxes. The Paramount Skydance-Warner Bros. Discovery deal closed on October 6, 2026, with his son David Ellison leading the combined company; the borrowing spree has since drawn scrutiny, including a lawsuit from 12 states seeking to block aspects of the deal and a roughly 34% drop in Oracle's stock this month.
FINANCE
Oil Futures Settle Lower in Volatile Trade
Brent crude settled near $109 a barrel on October 20, 2026, after a choppy session in which prices fell as much as 3.3% intraday before paring losses, as traders weighed conflicting signals on US-Iran negotiations following a Bloomberg report disputing claims the two sides were close to a deal. WTI crude for November delivery fell $1.87, or 1.8%, to settle at $101.79 per barrel the same session. Crude has risen roughly 80% so far this year amid escalating Middle East hostilities, including Iran's stepped-up attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, with traders also watching for any White House moves to de-escalate the conflict ahead of the November 3 midterm election.
