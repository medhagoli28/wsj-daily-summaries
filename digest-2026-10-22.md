## Tech

- **Meta Is Getting Creative to Fund Its Massive AI Investments** — Meta sold $25 billion in investment-grade bonds in a jumbo offering in late April/early May 2026, its second such mega-deal in six months after a record $30 billion sale in 2025, as it raised its 2026 AI capital-expenditure guidance to $125 billion–$145 billion. Beyond public debt, Meta has turned to off-balance-sheet financing through special-purpose vehicles tied to individual data centers, including roughly $30 billion raised via a structure linked to Blue Owl Capital, plus joint ventures like the Hyperion data center project in Louisiana (about $27 billion in building costs) and long-term leases. As of June 30, 2026, Meta's total bonds outstanding stood at $84.0 billion, and analysts estimate Big Tech firms collectively will issue around $540 billion in AI-related debt this year, prompting some concern about circular financing arrangements in the sector. - **Exclusive | Senators Express ‘Grave Concerns,’ Launch Inquiry into Google’s AI Push in Schools** — U.S. Senators Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) and Josh Hawley (R-MO) sent a letter to Google and Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai on October 21, 2026, demanding answers about the company's aggressive marketing of AI tools, including Gemini, to schoolchildren, citing "grave concern" that the push is harming students' academic performance and wellbeing. The letter asks why AI features were set as the default on school-issued devices, what Google knows about potential harms to children, and what promotional tactics it used to expand AI adoption in classrooms, and gives Google until November 2, 2026 to respond. The senators' inquiry follows reporting that cited more than 60 interviews with teachers, parents, students, and clinicians describing declining test scores and students' reduced ability to complete work without AI assistance. _Sources: <https://www.blumenthal.senate.gov/newsroom/press/release/blumenthal-and-hawley-demand-answers-from-google-about-aggressive-ai-push-in-classrooms>, <https://www.hawley.senate.gov/hawley-blumenthal-demand-answers-from-google-about-rampant-a-i-push-in-classrooms/>_
- **Exclusive | AI Just Found a Bug That Puts up to 70% of the World’s Websites at Risk** — The PHP Foundation issued emergency patches on October 21, 2026 for two critical vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, which allow unauthenticated remote code execution and were rated 9.2 out of 10 on the CVSS severity scale; because PHP powers an estimated 70% of the world's websites, the flaws were described as among the most severe ever found. The bugs were uncovered by security researchers using advanced AI models in a project involving OpenAI, and a co-founder of Google's Wiz cybersecurity team called it one of the most severe AI-assisted vulnerability discoveries to date. In response, government agencies in France, Denmark, Finland, and the Netherlands took public-facing websites offline as a precaution, Cloudflare's CTO reported early exploitation attempts emerging, and the U.S. National Security Agency urged organizations to patch immediately or isolate internet-facing applications if they could not update right away. _Sources: <https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/21/websites-scramble-fix-software-flaw-that-could-put-much-internet-risk/>, <https://www.semafor.com/article/10/21/2026/ai-helps-find-vulnerability-affecting-up-to-70-of-websites>_
- **Why AI’s Deepest Mind Believes the World Is About to Change Radically** — Demis Hassabis, Google DeepMind's co-founder and CEO, told The Wall Street Journal Magazine in October 2026 that he now expects artificial general intelligence within two to three years, down sharply from the five-to-ten-year estimate he gave at Davos in January and the "2030, plus or minus a year" timeline he cited at Google I/O in May. He defines AGI as a system matching the full cognitive range of the human brain, and frames the shift as a "species-level transition" with little room for error, warning of risks from a competitive "race to the bottom" among AI labs even as he predicts the technology could eventually enable "radical abundance" and help cure disease. His accelerating timeline reflects recent breakthroughs he's seen inside DeepMind's models rather than a single new product launch. _Sources: <https://stanforddaily.com/2026/05/29/deepmind-ceo-warns-about-ai/>, <https://www.axios.com/2026/05/26/deepmind-ceo-demis-hassabis>, <https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3646186509667977>_

- **Exclusive | How Much Can You Safely Spend in Retirement? A 401(k) Giant Offers Guidance** — Capital Group, which manages roughly $2.9 trillion in assets and is a major 401(k) recordkeeper, published new retirement withdrawal guidance on October 21, 2026, offering retirees two drawdown strategies instead of the traditional fixed 4% rule. For predictable income, it recommends a fixed initial withdrawal rate of 4% to 4.5%, which would generate $40,000 to $45,000 in year one from a $1 million portfolio, with the dollar amount adjusted annually for inflation. For retirees who can tolerate fluctuating income, Capital Group suggests a variable approach starting at 5% to 5.5% of the portfolio, with payments moving up or down each year based on account performance. The firm says the higher variable rate is sustainable because annual withdrawals adjust with market swings rather than staying fixed. _Sources: <https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/capital-group-retirement-withdrawal-rates-401k-1827092>_
