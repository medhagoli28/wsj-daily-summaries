AI Hacks Are Coming Thick and Fast and Overwhelming the Internet
Researchers at Wiz uncovered two critical PHP vulnerabilities, collectively named "php2shell" (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619), that could enable remote code execution on servers running PHP, which powers an estimated 70% of websites worldwide; the PHP Foundation issued an emergency patch after the flaws were disclosed in August 2026. Frontier AI models, including OpenAI's GPT-5.6 "Sol," played a key role in surfacing the exploit chain, which had previously been too complex for human researchers to practically demonstrate despite the underlying bugs existing for decades. Separately, starting September 25, 2026, a hacker unleashed a swarm of AI agents that autonomously attempted to breach more than 1,200 websites over several weeks without further human input, illustrating how AI-driven "agent swarms" are accelerating both the discovery and exploitation side of cyberattacks. Experts say the combined effect is a surge in AI-assisted and increasingly autonomous attacks that is straining defenders' ability to patch and respond in time.

The Signal Chats That Cost OpenAI Researchers Their Jobs
OpenAI fired three safety researchers — Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni, and Jasmine Wang — on October 1, 2026, after its security team found they had used disappearing Signal messages to communicate with outside AI-safety researcher Ryan Greenblatt. The investigation began after a September report revealed details about OpenAI's newest model, code-named Astra, which the company treated as a leak of confidential model-design information; security staff then called the researchers into meetings and asked them to unlock their phones to review the chats. OpenAI described the dismissals as stemming from a "significant breach of trust," while the three researchers say they were never told specifically what information they allegedly leaked and deny sharing confidential material, suggesting the firings are instead linked to their advocacy for more transparent AI safety practices.

What to Know About the AI-Triggered Cybersecurity Crisis
The second batch of results matches the recent, dated story: a critical PHP vulnerability set, dubbed PHP2Shell, was disclosed around October 21-22, 2026, and AI tools sharply accelerated both its discovery/analysis and subsequent exploitation, forcing emergency patching across government and corporate websites worldwide — researchers estimate the flaws could affect roughly 70% of internet-facing PHP sites. Cloudflare reported exploit attempts scaling from thousands within 10 hours of disclosure to hundreds of thousands within two hours, as attackers used AI to weaponize the two-decade-old bug far faster than in past vulnerability cycles. Wiz researchers noted AI didn't find the bug itself but let a single researcher complete in about a week what previously required a world-class team months to do, compressing the timeline between disclosure and mass exploitation. Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince warned the incident previews a new normal, saying "we're going to have a couple years where every single week it's going to feel like the sky is falling."

Elon Musk said this week during Tesla's earnings call that the boards of SpaceX and Tesla have each formed special committees to explore a potential merger, calling the idea a "lot of merit" but stressing that a deal is "not a certainty." Together the two companies are valued at roughly $3.5 trillion, meaning a combination would eclipse SpaceX's $1.25 trillion absorption of xAI in February and rank as the largest corporate merger in history. The strategic logic Musk cited centers on AI and compute: Tesla's self-driving cars and Optimus robots could draw on the massive data-center and orbital computing capacity SpaceX is building out. Any deal would need approval from both companies' shareholders, and Musk himself is excluded from the special committees due to his conflicts of interest on both sides.

Meta Ordered to Pay $25 Billion to New Mexico in Data-Privacy Case
A New Mexico state judge ordered Meta to pay $25 billion to the state on October 21, 2026, after a Santa Fe jury found in September that the company violated the state's Unfair Practices Act by misleading residents about privacy, tallying 43 million violations tied to the 2018 Cambridge Analytica scandal, in which the data of up to 87 million Facebook users was improperly harvested. The judge calculated that state law could have supported a penalty exceeding $219 billion but reduced it to $25 billion, making it the largest fine ever levied against Meta. Meta's chief legal officer said the suit effectively seeks "to impose a tax on speech" and that the company is confident it will prevail on appeal.

The Financial—and Psychological—Cost of Being the Friend Who Picks Up the Tab
Nearly half of Gen Z consumers—46%—say they currently owe friends or family more than $1,000, with 30% owing $2,500 or more, according to Zelle's "Avoidance Economy Report," based on two national surveys of roughly 1,000 Americans conducted in 2026. Among those who fronted money for a shared expense like a trip or event, 76% say they were never fully repaid, and 55% say settling up has created tension or hurt a friendship. The report also found 47% of Gen Z respondents have gone into debt covering group costs, and about 20% have muted or ignored group chats specifically to dodge repayment requests. Gen Z was also found to be the biggest per-person spender on shared experiences like festivals or bachelorette trips, with 37% reporting spending more than $2,500 on a single event.

Sallie Mae Cuts Full-Year View After Logging Higher Charge-Offs

Meta’s Muse Is Going Gangbusters. But Where Is the Payoff?
I have enough concrete facts to write the brief now. Meta's personal AI assistant Muse, launched in August 2026, has signed up more than 10 million users in the U.S. and Canada, with daily active users hitting 2.5 million on October 16 — a 15% week-over-week jump — and about 25% of registered users engaging daily. Meta charges $20 a month for its "Power" tier and $100 a month for "Maximum," on top of a free tier, while also exploring taking a cut of purchases that Muse helps users complete, a fee Zuckerberg has described as "a very small cut" of money saved, earned, or spent through the agent. Despite the rapid adoption — strong enough that Meta reportedly had to ask Stripe to raise transaction limits to handle shopping volume — Meta's overall Q3 2026 earnings (revenue up 26% to $64.6 billion, net income more than tripling to $9.86 billion) showed capital expenditure and AI infrastructure spending squeezing free cash flow, and analysts have cautioned that monetizing Muse at scale could be "notoriously slow."
