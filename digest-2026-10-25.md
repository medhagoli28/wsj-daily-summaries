## Markets & Finance

- **Nasdaq Futures Steady as AI Selloff Pauses** — The Nasdaq Composite slid 2.6% on October 22, 2026, its worst day since June, as the S&P 500 dropped 1.2% in its worst session since July, driven largely by a nearly 8% plunge in Meta shares after it reported its first quarterly cash outflow in more than a decade amid heavy AI-related capital spending; by October 23, Nasdaq futures edged up about 0.2% as the selloff paused, though sentiment stayed fragile with rising oil prices and Nvidia's CEO warning that capital is growing scarce relative to demand in the AI buildout, particularly for newer cloud providers. _Sources: <https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nasdaq-futures-steady-ai-selloff-084400333.html>, <https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-10-22/nasdaq-futures-drop-over-1-as-ai-worries-resurface-oil-climbs>_
- **NYSE Just Extended Options Trading Hours. It’s Only the Beginning.** — NYSE American began extended-hours options trading on October 19, 2026, adding sessions from 7:30 a.m. to 9:25 a.m. ET and 4:00 p.m. to 4:15 p.m. ET around the standard 9:30 a.m.-4:00 p.m. window, initially covering 29 stocks and ETFs including SPY, QQQ, IWM, GLD, and names like Apple, Nvidia, Tesla, and Amazon. The move follows rival Cboe, which launched similar extended options hours on 24 stocks and five ETFs the same week, as exchanges race to offer round-the-clock trading in response to competition from crypto derivatives and decentralized platforms. NYSE has separately said it plans to extend regular equities trading to 23 hours a day, five days a week, beginning December 6, 2026. _Sources: <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/19/nyse-extends-options-trading-hours.html>, <https://www.thetradenews.com/nyse-goes-live-with-extended-trading-hours-for-options/>, <https://financefeeds.com/nyse-launches-extended-trading-hours-for-nyse-american-options/>_

## Personal Finance

- **Want to Short the AI Boom? She Wants to Give You a Way to Bet on ‘Compute’ Prices** — Carmen Li, founder of Silicon Data and CEO of Compute Exchange, built the GPU benchmarking indexes behind a new market that lets investors go long or short on the price of AI computing power rather than just data-center stocks. On October 5, 2026, CME Group began listing the first two U.S.-regulated compute futures contracts — the Silicon Data H100 Rental Index Futures and B200 Rental Index Futures — in a partnership announced earlier in the year with CME Group CEO Terry Duffy calling compute "the new oil of the 21st century." The underlying rental benchmarks have been volatile: Silicon Data's H100 rental index rose 44% since December 9, 2025 to about $2.88 per GPU-hour even as separate token-price indices (from Silicon Data and Ramp) fell roughly 50% from their 2026 peaks, exposing a split between rising hardware costs and falling AI service prices. DRW founder Don Wilson, who along with firms like Jump Trading is already trading in this space, has predicted compute will become "the largest commodity in the world," underscoring the appetite for tools to hedge — or bet against — the AI infrastructure buildout. _Sources: <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/11/ai-computing-power-becomes-a-tradable-asset-class-as-cme-starts-futures.html>, <https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2026/5/12/cme_group_and_silicondatapartnertolaunchfirstcomputefutures.html>, <https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/jump-drw-bet-on-new-compute-market-as-ai-boom-faces-scrutiny>_
