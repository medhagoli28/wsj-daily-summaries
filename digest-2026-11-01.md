# WSJ Deep Digest — 2026-11-01

_Headlines selected by WSJ; summaries researched from non-WSJ sources._


## Tech

- **AI Spenders Get Spooked** — Big Tech's five largest AI spenders — Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Oracle — have raised a combined $326 billion in debt and stock sales so far in 2026 to fund data-center buildouts, roughly triple the amount raised a year earlier, while Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft outlined over $740 billion in combined 2026 capital expenditures largely tied to AI infrastructure. Investor unease deepened after Democrats won control of Congress in the November 3, 2026 midterms, with the Nasdaq dropping more than 5% on fears of a regulatory crackdown on data centers over energy and water use, erasing roughly $2 trillion in tech-stock value by November 5. Cash-flow strain is already showing: Alphabet posted a second straight quarter of negative free cash flow (-$9.1 billion in Q3) even as its quarterly capex rose to $51.4 billion. European and U.S. tech shares remained volatile through mid-November as investors questioned whether the AI buildout will generate returns to justify the spending. _Sources: <https://www.axios.com/2026/10/29/ai-hyperscalers-stock-debt>, <https://www.npr.org/2026/11/05/nx-s1-6031987/stock-markets-ai-midterm-elections>, <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/ai-data-center-midterms-selloff.html>_
- **The Tangled Web of AI Bets Is Reason to Fear Tech Stocks** — Interlocking financial ties among AI's biggest players — Nvidia, OpenAI, Oracle, Microsoft, AMD and CoreWeave — have become a focal point of investor anxiety, with Oracle alone committing roughly $300 billion in compute purchases tied to OpenAI and Microsoft committing about $250 billion of its own, while Nvidia has pledged up to $100 billion to OpenAI partly to fund chip purchases that flow back into Nvidia's own revenue. Nvidia's market value has swelled to roughly $5.4 trillion even as critics, including investor Michael Burry, warn that this circular financing inflates demand and revenue figures across the chain. The risk became tangible on November 5, 2026, when the Nasdaq fell 5.52% and chip stocks sold off sharply after U.S. midterm election results stoked fears of tighter AI regulation and a slower data-center build-out, with the rout extending into Asian trading the next day. Reuters noted that as of November 3, nearly $32 trillion in S&P 500 market value had been added since ChatGPT's 2022 launch, with more than half of that gain concentrated in just 10 AI-linked companies, underscoring how dependent the broader market has become on this web of AI bets. _Sources: <https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/6/tech-stock-rout-driven-by-us-ai-fears-continues-in-asian-trading>, <https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-bubble-financial-markets/>, <https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/global-ai-stocks-sink-as-nvidia-crisis-deepens-sector-selloff-5021776>_

## Markets & Finance

- **AI Investors Are Racing to Get Insurance on the Biggest Market Bet in History** — AI developers and investors are rushing to insure data-center megaprojects against fire, power failure, and natural-catastrophe losses, with Aon rolling out a dedicated data-center insurance program offering up to $5 billion in coverage, up from $3.5 billion as recently as April 2026. Swiss Re Institute forecasts data-center insurance premiums will total $91 billion between 2026 and 2030, roughly $49 billion of that in property coverage alone, as the asset class balloons toward an estimated $6.7 trillion in cumulative investment. Because a single $10 billion data-center project can require more than 40 insurers to assemble enough capacity, lenders including KKR and Blackstone have reportedly turned down some data-center loans over inadequate natural-disaster coverage, and insurers have begun bundling conventional and specialist policies into new multi-line products to close the gap. The global data-center insurance market itself is projected to grow from about $11 billion today to more than $24 billion by 2030. _Sources: <https://www.investing.com/analysis/ai-data-centers-are-running-into-an-insurance-problem-200691349>, <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/ai-data-centers-investing.html>, <https://beinsure.com/how-the-ai-data-center-boom-is-reshaping-the-re-insurance-landscape/>_
- **Tech Stocks and AI Bonds Are Tumbling. Is the AI Trade in Trouble?** — Tech shares and AI-linked corporate debt have both come under pressure this November: the Nasdaq 100 plunged 6.36% on November 5-6, its worst day since March 2020, after Democrats' midterm win stoked fears of tighter AI regulation and a slower data-center buildout, wiping out more than $650 billion from chip stocks in two days. Credit markets showed matching stress — CoreWeave's credit-default-swap spreads spiked above 1,289 basis points and its 2031 bonds fell to about 79.5 cents on the dollar (yields near 15-16%), while Oracle's implied five-year default probability rose above 20% even as Microsoft ($25 billion), Oracle ($10 billion) and Meta (marketing $20 billion-plus) kept selling bonds to fund AI infrastructure. As of November 16, the Nasdaq 100 was still sliding (down about 1.8% intraday) heading into Nvidia's earnings and Anthropic's expected IPO, with Morgan Stanley telling clients to stick to higher-quality AI-linked bonds given persistent debt worries. _Sources: <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/ai-data-center-midterms-selloff.html>, <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/morgan-stanley-ai-bonds-hyperscalers-credit.html>, <https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/us-futures-drop-as-market-looks-to-nvidia-results-anthropic-ipo>_
