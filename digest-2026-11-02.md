- **The Shopping Bots Are Coming for Amazon** — Amazon is racing to control the rise of autonomous AI shopping agents: it sued Perplexity in November 2026 over the Comet browser agent's ability to make purchases on its marketplace, citing computer-fraud claims and a degraded shopping experience, while also blocking Meta's new "Muse" shopping agent from its site. - **People Are Using AI for Divorce Advice and It’s Driving Their Lawyers Crazy** — Family lawyers say a growing share of clients are showing up to consultations with legal strategies, draft pleadings, or arguments generated by AI chatbots like ChatGPT, often convinced the advice is sound before a professional ever reviews their case; one survey of 133 family-law attorneys found 80% had received client correspondence or filings they believed were AI-generated. Attorneys describe the chatbot-sourced guidance as frequently inaccurate — for example, one business owner was told by a chatbot he had an "easy case" for lowering spousal support without the bot ever reviewing his financial statements, and nearly two years and two law firms later he was still untangling the fallout. Separately, in February 2026 a federal judge (Jed Rakoff, SDNY) ruled that a litigant's chats with a consumer AI chatbot were protected by neither attorney-client privilege nor work-product doctrine, prompting several family-law firms to warn clients that ChatGPT conversations can be discoverable in divorce litigation. Lawyers say the trend is adding cost and friction to cases, as clients often expect their attorney to simply rubber-stamp conclusions they've already reached with a chatbot. _Sources: <https://www.axios.com/2026/02/09/ai-chatgpt-lawyer-legal-help>, <https://www.businessinsurance.com/ai-divorce-advice-burns-business-owner/>, <https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/newsletters/2026/february/23/divorce-and-family-law-insights/what-happens-when-you-ask-ai-about-your-divorce>_

- **This Bond Measure Says Stocks Are Cheap. Don’t Believe It.** — Treasury yields climbing toward 5% in 2026 have narrowed the gap between stock earnings yields and bond yields to levels last seen in the late 1990s, with the S&P 500's equity risk premium compressing to roughly zero or turning negative by some measures, according to Bloomberg-sourced data reported by Fortune on September 29, 2026. As of November 13, 2026, the S&P 500 traded around 18.5 times forward earnings — below its five- and ten-year averages near 19-20x — even as the 10-year Treasury yield pushed to roughly 5.15%-5.24% that week, a combination some analysts read as a signal stocks are undervalued relative to bonds. Critics of that read argue the comparison is flawed because it pits a nominal Treasury yield against a real earnings yield (corporate profits grow with inflation, bond coupons don't), so a fairer gauge would stack earnings yield against inflation-protected Treasury (TIPS) yields, a gap that has also narrowed sharply and no longer signals cheap stocks. Ray Dalio separately warned in October 2026 that the earnings-growth cushion stocks have used to absorb rising bond yields is shrinking, leaving less protection if free cash flow weakens. _Sources: <https://fortune.com/2026/09/29/equity-risk-premium-investing-stocks-versus-bonds/>, <https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/16/the-s-and-p-500-looks-cheaper-than-it-has-in-over-3-years-but-there-s-a-catch/>, <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/08/ray-dalio-stocks-bond-yields.html>_
