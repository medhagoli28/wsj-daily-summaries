Silicon Valley Promises AI Will Cure Disease. Drugmaker CEOs Aren’t Buying It.
Pharmaceutical executives are pushing back on Silicon Valley's bold predictions that AI will soon cure most diseases, with Jeff Leiden, executive chairman of Vertex Pharmaceuticals, calling a tech leader's claim that AI would cure 90% of human disease within seven years "complete BS"; Vertex says it worked with roughly 50 AI companies to mine a dataset of 10,000 drug compounds and produced zero working drugs. AstraZeneca CEO Pascal Soriot echoed the skepticism, noting "disease is complicated, biology is complicated," and executives point out that no FDA-approved drug has yet been designed using AI, with about a quarter of mid-to-late-stage drug failures tied to "off-target" safety issues that current AI models struggle to predict. The pushback comes after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei suggested in September 2026 that AI could help cure most human disease within five to ten years. Still, major drugmakers including AstraZeneca, Bayer, Eli Lilly, and Regeneron continue to use AI for tasks like mining patient records and identifying drug targets.

Google Curbs AI in School Accounts After Pushback
Google turned off Gemini, Gemini in Classroom, and AI-infused Search by default for K-12 student accounts managed through Google Workspace for Education, announcing the reversal on November 3, 2026, after backlash from parents, teachers, and privacy advocates over AI being enabled without consent. School administrators must now actively opt in to restore any of these tools, and the rollout of the changed defaults begins in early 2027. Notably, Google still bundles AI features into Search itself — a district can disable the Gemini chatbot while keeping Search, but it cannot keep Search while stripping out its AI Overviews, since there is no separate toggle for that. Google also said data from these school accounts will be used only to generate responses to a user's prompts and not to train or improve its generative AI models.

Trump Declared War on the Term ‘AI.’ CEOs Are Struggling to Keep Up.
In late September 2026, President Trump signed an executive order directing federal agencies to replace "artificial intelligence" and "AI" with "super intelligence" or "SI" in official communications, calling the original term misleading; the order also gave the White House science adviser 60 days (a deadline landing around November 28) to propose legislation defining "super intelligence" federally. The Pentagon has already renamed its Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office the "Super Intelligence Office," while tech executives have responded unevenly — Elon Musk said he'd rename xAI-linked products to "SI" branding and Salesforce's Marc Benioff said "AIForce" would become "SIForce," even as a YouGov poll found just 9% of the public preferred "super intelligence" to 53% favoring "artificial intelligence." The shift has left many companies scrambling to decide whether to follow the administration's preferred terminology in marketing and government-facing materials without alienating customers who still use "AI."

Hackers Crank the Heat on Europe’s Supermarket Freezers to Above 120 Degrees
Hackers remotely overrode temperature controls on internet-connected supermarket refrigeration systems across Europe in two waves, the first around October 1-2 hitting French blood banks and stores and a second around November 1-2 striking chains including Ahold Delhaize and Colruyt in Belgium and Luxembourg and ICA and Coop in Sweden; attackers triggered defrost cycles that pushed freezer temperatures up to roughly 50 degrees Celsius (about 122°F), spoiling food stocks across more than 100 locations in the latest wave and over 700 locations across nine countries combined since the attacks began. French officials have pointed to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps as responsible and said they would present evidence to EU counterparts and push for sanctions.

U.S. Stocks Mixed Amid Midterm, Iran War Nerves
U.S. stocks traded mixed on Thursday, November 5, 2026, as Wall Street cooled off from the prior day's rally, with Dow Jones futures up about 0.5%, S&P 500 futures up roughly 0.1%, and Nasdaq-100 futures down about 0.7%. The pullback came a day after the Dow posted its best post-midterm-election one-day gain in 44 years, jumping roughly 1,569 points (about 3%), after Democrats swept both chambers of Congress in Tuesday's midterms. Oil prices, which had tumbled on hopes the election results would pressure a resolution to the Iran conflict, stabilized with Brent crude near $96 a barrel and WTI near $89, after Vice President JD Vance publicly suggested the war with Iran cost Republicans the midterms. Investors were also still digesting the fallout for defense and AI-chip stocks, which had sold off sharply on bets that a Democratic Congress would push to wind down the conflict and tighten AI oversight.

Apple Shares Slip, Meta Debt Costs Rise
Apple shares fell roughly 4.5% in premarket trading on November 3, 2026, after the company warned that rising memory-chip costs would squeeze margins, guiding to a holiday-quarter gross margin of 45%-46%, below the 46.9% analysts had expected. Separately, Meta Platforms raised about $25 billion in new debt between November 5-12, 2026 — roughly $15 billion in dollar bonds and about $10 billion in euro-denominated notes — to fund its AI infrastructure buildout, with the new dollar notes carrying rates from 5.85% (2031 maturity) to 7.5% (2056 maturity), higher than any Meta bond sold before 2026. The borrowing spree, part of a 2026 capex plan of $135-145 billion, pushed Meta's quarterly free cash flow negative and prompted S&P Global to revise its outlook on Meta's AA- credit rating to negative; broader US investment-grade tech bond spreads widened to about 110 basis points in November from 80 in October as investors grew wary of the sector's AI-driven debt binge.

Best Pet Insurance for Older Dogs 2026
Pets Best, MetLife, Spot, and Pumpkin are among the pet insurers highlighted for 2026 as offering senior-dog coverage with no upper age limit for enrollment, while Healthy Paws and ASPCA Pet Health Insurance also rank highly for older-dog plans; Pets Best is cited as the most affordable option, averaging about $63 per month, with AKC Pet Insurance noted for covering pre-existing conditions and Embrace for low deductibles. Comparison sites including CNBC, U.S. News, and MoneyGeek evaluated providers on cost, claims speed, and benefits such as prescription, cancer, and end-of-life care coverage specifically for aging dogs. No single insurer topped every list — rankings varied by whether the priority was affordability (Pets Best), claims speed (MetLife), or overall value (Healthy Paws, Pumpkin).

Comprehensive Financial Planning: What Is It, and How Does It Work?
This is an evergreen explainer piece (part of WSJ Buy Side's personal-finance guide series) rather than a breaking news story, so no competing outlet reported the same article. Comprehensive financial planning is generally described as a holistic approach coordinating six core areas — cash flow/budgeting, protection (insurance), tax planning, investment planning, retirement planning, and estate planning — into one integrated strategy rather than addressing goals like retirement or college savings in isolation. The process itself follows a standard sequence: gathering financial facts, defining goals, modeling options, implementing a plan, and revisiting it periodically as circumstances change. Advisors who offer this service are typically recommended to hold fiduciary status and credentials such as CFP certification, since the role involves managing risk, tax efficiency, and life changes across a client's full financial picture rather than a single product or account.

North American Co. Life Insurance Review 2026: Coverage, Cost and Insights
North American Company for Life and Health Insurance, founded in 1886 and part of Sammons Financial, holds an A+ (Superior) rating from AM Best along with A+ ratings from S&P and Fitch, and it paid out more than $547 million in death claims in 2024. The insurer offers term life, guaranteed universal life, and indexed universal life (IUL) policies with coverage up to $5 million; its term life rates run about 9% to 21% above the national average, while its permanent/universal life policies price roughly 13% to 22% below the market average, with every policy including a no-cost accelerated death benefit rider for chronic, critical, or terminal illness. For reference, a 40-year-old nonsmoker buying $500,000 of universal life coverage pays an average of about $295 a month for men and $251 for women.
