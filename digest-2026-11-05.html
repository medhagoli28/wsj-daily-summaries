How the Data Center Backlash Could Play Into the Hands of Big Tech
Voters in at least 38 jurisdictions passed bans or restrictions on new data centers on Election Day in early November 2026, part of a broader backlash driven by concerns over noise, water use, and higher electricity bills — a Washington Post/other polling found roughly 7 in 10 Americans oppose new data centers in their communities, and roughly $64 billion in data center projects have been blocked or delayed over the past two years. Analysts and reporting (Axios, TechCrunch) note this regulatory friction raises the barrier to entry for new projects while established players like Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and Meta — which already have sites permitted, built, or under construction — are better positioned to weather the pushback and even acquire stalled projects cheaply, effectively entrenching their competitive advantage. In response, companies including Google, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft have begun offering communities more money, transparency, and concessions to ease opposition, according to Fortune and Axios.

HP Taps Former GSK Executive Emma Walmsley as Next CEO
HP Inc.'s board named Emma Walmsley as its next chief executive, effective January 4, 2027, succeeding Bruce Broussard, who has been interim CEO since February 2026; the announcement came November 4, 2026, ending a roughly monthlong search. Walmsley led pharmaceutical giant GSK as CEO from April 2017 to December 2025 and has sat on Microsoft's board for seven years, but she arrives at HP with no prior hardware or tech-industry experience. Her mandate includes reviving a sluggish PC business and positioning HP to capture growing demand for AI-enabled devices.

Americans Cut Back on Boats and RVs—but Can’t Stop Buying Harley-Davidsons
New boat sales are forecast to drop 15% in 2026, with retail sales down 8.6% to 215,736 units on a rolling 12-month basis through November 2025, while RV wholesale shipments are projected by the RVIA to fall roughly 8.2% to between 300,000 and 328,100 units for the year amid even steeper year-over-year declines reported earlier in 2026. Harley-Davidson, by contrast, posted a 9% jump in North American retail motorcycle sales in its third quarter of 2026 (reported October 29, 2026), marking a fourth consecutive quarter of year-over-year growth, and the company raised its full-year 2026 guidance to global retail sales and wholesale shipments of 135,500 to 139,000 units. Analysts attribute the boat and RV slump to higher financing costs, inflation, and more cautious consumer confidence around big-ticket discretionary purchases, even as Harley's turnaround—driven by strength in its Trike and Softail lines—bucks that broader pullback.

A Soldier Cut Off His Own Foot to Survive. Then Came a Daring Drone Rescue.
Ukrainian soldier Kostiantyn Vlasenko, 49, amputated his own frostbitten and gangrenous foot to survive after being wounded by Russian drone-dropped munitions that killed his comrade, then spent roughly 491 days stranded near the front line before a six-rotor heavy-lift drone extracted him on October 4, 2026, carrying him about 70 meters into the air and 15 kilometers to safety. Ukrainian commanders called it the first known aerial drone rescue of a wounded soldier in the war; Vlasenko later met Prince Harry and recounted being "on my own for more than 200 days." The operation required coordinated military support to shield the rescue drone from interception by Russian aircraft.

Exclusive | Major Investors Are Skeptical Anthropic IPO Can Achieve $1.5 Trillion Valuation
Major investors told CNBC and Reuters in early November 2026 that Anthropic's hoped-for $1.5 trillion IPO valuation was looking like a "stretch" after a broader AI stock sell-off, with some predicting a more realistic range of $1 trillion to $1.25 trillion. By mid-November, Anthropic formally targeted a lower valuation of up to roughly $1.02 trillion in its IPO filing, about half the $2 trillion figure once floated earlier in the process. Reuters reported investors cited slowing revenue growth and the company's massive infrastructure spending commitments as reasons to push for a reduced price, while the IPO itself is expected to raise over $64 billion at the top of its range. The pullback reflects cooling Wall Street appetite for AI valuations more broadly amid the recent market correction in AI-related stocks.

Wall Street Gets $2.5 Trillion AI Reality Check With Historic Rout of Tech Stocks
A Wall Street sell-off on Thursday, November 5, 2026 wiped out roughly $2.5 trillion in market value as investors reassessed the AI buildout following Democratic gains in Tuesday's midterm elections, including in Texas, a hub for planned data centers; the tech-heavy Nasdaq posted its worst day since April 2025. Nvidia shares fell more than 9%, the "Magnificent Seven" mega-cap tech stocks lost roughly $1 trillion combined in the single session, and the VIX volatility index surged about 70%, its biggest one-day jump in nearly two years, reaching a seven-month high. The rout spread overnight into Asian and European markets, and by Friday, November 6, US stocks staged a fragile, reflex-driven rebound, though analysts said the episode left investors questioning whether the political and regulatory backdrop that fueled the AI rally had shifted.

Opinion | Trump Is the Author of the Ruin
Peggy Noonan's WSJ opinion column "Trump Is the Author of the Ruin," published November 5-6, 2026, argued Trump bears responsibility for the GOP's midterm losses, landing amid exit polling showing roughly 60-65% of voters blamed Trump's policies for making the economy worse and for high prices; an AP-NORC survey similarly found Trump's economic approval hitting a new low, with a separate Reuters/Ipsos poll putting the share of Americans faulting his policies for rising prices at 78%. NBC News' exit poll data showed many voters treated the November 3, 2026 midterms as a direct rebuke of Trump, with 44% saying their personal finances were worse than two years earlier when he returned to office. Noonan's column itself is paywalled on WSJ, but its framing — that Trump squandered a strong economic hand and is now the author of his party's decline — is corroborated by the broader polling picture reported elsewhere.

The 2027 Tax Brackets Are Here. See Where You Land.
The IRS announced new 2027 federal income tax brackets on November 10, 2026, keeping the seven rates unchanged at 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37% while raising the income thresholds roughly 3.2% for inflation, pushing the top 37% bracket to kick in above $768,700 for single filers. The standard deduction rises to $16,600 for single filers, $33,200 for married couples filing jointly, and $24,925 for heads of household. The adjustments touch nearly 70 provisions, including an increase in the unified gift and estate tax exemption to $15.48 million and a rise in the annual gift tax exclusion to $20,000 per recipient. These changes apply to tax year 2027, meaning they'll affect returns filed in early 2028.
