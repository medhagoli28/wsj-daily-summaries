Quantum Computers Are Poised to Crack Encryption. AI Could Beat Them to It.
AI systems are now finding cryptographic weaknesses faster than researchers expected: an Anthropic model set loose on the HAWK lattice-based signature scheme found a hidden symmetry in about 60 hours and turned it into an attack that halved the scheme's effective security, while AI coding agents ported classical factoring methods to GPUs and set two RSA factoring records just sixteen days apart using no new math, only raw speed. Separately, in March 2026 Google's Quantum AI team used AI-assisted algorithm design to cut the number of qubits needed to break elliptic-curve cryptography by roughly 20x compared to prior estimates, accelerating the quantum timeline as well. An Ethereum Foundation researcher has urged crypto holders to adopt "bunker mode" and move funds to safer storage, warning that AI-driven mathematics could break ECDSA signatures—used to secure Bitcoin and Ethereum wallets—before quantum computers do, potentially within months rather than years. Security experts caution that no AI has yet extracted a key or plaintext from a properly implemented modern cipher faster than known theoretical attacks, meaning the risk so far is about accelerating specific structural weaknesses rather than a wholesale break of encryption.

Opinion | There’s No Such Thing as a Stock-Market Bubble
This is a WSJ Opinion column by Jeff Yass (founder of Susquehanna International Group), published November 7, 2026, arguing that the concept of a "stock-market bubble" is flawed because it presumes markets stray from a knowable "true" price; he contends that when share prices fall, as in 1999, it reflects uncertainty resolving rather than a delusion correcting, and that it is "arrogant to believe you know more than the market." As an individual opinion piece rather than a reported news story, it was not covered as news by wire services or other outlets — the only secondary pickup found was a syndication republish on RealClearPolitics, not independent reporting. Separately, the underlying bubble debate it addresses is active in November 2026, with the S&P 500 having added roughly $32 trillion in value since ChatGPT's release (over half from just ten AI-linked companies) and figures like Gary Gensler warning of a bursting AI bubble while Ed Yardeni calls it "self-correcting" with "no irrational exuberance."

Hollywood’s New Superhero Is the Guy Who Tells Moviegoers When to Pee
RunPee, a free app that tells moviegoers the safest moments to slip out for a bathroom break without missing key scenes, was created in 2008 by Dan Gardner, a former Microsoft software developer, after he endured Peter Jackson's 187-minute "King Kong"; he launched it as a website that year and turned it into a mobile app in 2009. The app's team watches each film in advance to flag roughly one safe "Peetime" every 30 minutes, paired with a vibrating alert and a short recap of what was missed, and it now covers more than 2,300 titles with new releases added weekly. RunPee has topped 500,000 downloads across iPhone and Android and continues to see regular updates, including a version released November 2, 2026, with demand spiking around blockbuster releases such as "Avengers: Endgame."

Hospitals Are So Crowded Patients Spend 24 Hours in the E.R. Hallways
Patients admitted to U.S. hospitals from the emergency room spent a median of 14 hours waiting for an inpatient bed as of 2024, with 26% waiting more than 24 hours and nearly 5% of ER-to-inpatient admissions hitting that 24-hour mark during peak months, a level rarely seen before the pandemic. The problem, known as ER boarding, is concentrated in the Northeast, where boarding beyond 24 hours reached 8% during winter peaks, and physicians including UCLA Health's Hashem Zikry describe hallways lined end-to-end with patients on stretchers. Emergency doctors say the crisis now rivals or exceeds the worst strain seen during the early years of the Covid-19 pandemic, driven by hospital bed shortages and staffing gaps rather than patient volume alone. Older adults are especially affected, facing longer waits and higher rates of avoidable harm while stuck in ER hallways awaiting admission.
