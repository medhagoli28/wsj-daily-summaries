My Week With Apple’s Beach-Friendly iPad Mini and Amazon’s Upscale Alexa Tablet
Apple unveiled its new iPad Mini on October 26, 2026, featuring an 8.5-inch OLED display, the A20 Pro chip, IP67 water and dust resistance, and a starting price of $699, which Apple calls the tablet's "biggest update ever." Around the same time, on October 8, 2026, Amazon retired its 15-year-old Fire tablet brand and introduced three Android-based "Alexa Tablet" models built around the Alexa+ assistant and full Google Play access: the 8.7-inch Tablet 8 ($229.99), the 11-inch Tablet 11 ($329.99), and the aluminum-bodied 12-inch Tablet 12 Pro, which starts at $499.99 ($549.99 for the anti-glare Nanomatte version). Amazon says the Tablet 12 Pro offers roughly triple the processing power and nine times the graphics performance of any previous Fire device, positioning it to compete directly with Apple's iPad and Samsung's Galaxy Tab lineup. All three Alexa Tablets began shipping October 14, 2026 in the U.S., Mexico, and Canada.

Nvidia’s Board to Investigate Reports That Jensen Huang Was Spying Target
Nvidia's board of directors announced on Sunday, November 8, 2026, that it was launching an independent investigation into reports that CEO Jensen Huang had been the target of a Chinese intelligence effort, after Huang told staff in an internal email that he'd had an affair with a woman, identified as Alice Huang, a former Singapore-based chip executive allegedly recruited by China's Ministry of State Security; the board said it had "no reason to believe" national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised. The New York Times subsequently reported Huang also had a separate affair with an Nvidia executive who reported to him. Days later, on Thursday, November 12, 2026, Nvidia confirmed Huang's resignation as CEO, president, and board member after 33 years leading the company, with CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO effective immediately.

Democrats Promised to Fix the Economy. What Can They Really Do?
Congressional Democrats, fresh off a midterm sweep that handed them control of the House and Senate, are mapping out a 100-day affordability agenda that includes reinstating expired Affordable Care Act premium tax credits, reversing Medicaid cuts, capping prescription drug costs, rolling back Trump-era tariffs, and a Senate proposal to cap credit card interest rates at 10%. Government funding must be extended by December 11, 2026, and analysts at Goldman Sachs expect Democrats to use that and other must-pass bills as leverage to advance parts of the agenda. Despite the ambitious wish list, the party's actual power is constrained by the Senate filibuster and limited influence over Federal Reserve policy, meaning many of the proposed fixes would shift costs rather than structurally lower them.

Opinion | Israel Can Achieve a Unity Government
Israel's October 27, 2026 legislative election left parliament deadlocked, with a four-party bloc led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot winning 48 of 120 Knesset seats and 67 lawmakers ultimately backing him for prime minister versus 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu; President Isaac Herzog tasked Eisenkot with forming a government and set a December 6 deadline. Netanyahu has since repeatedly called for a national unity government, arguing it's needed to "eliminate the Iranian threat once and for all," but Eisenkot has rejected a unity deal with Netanyahu himself—calling his campaign against Arab parties "racist"—while leaving the door open to a coalition with Likud and Religious Zionism members if their commitment to unity is "sincere." As of November 14-16, 2026, coalition negotiations remain ongoing with no deal reached.

Opinion | It Was the Economy, Not the War
Republicans suffered sweeping midterm losses in elections held the week of November 3, 2026, prompting Vice President JD Vance to attribute the defeat primarily to the war with Iran, while NBC News exit polling found 39% of voters named the economy the most important issue facing the country versus 32% who cited the state of democracy, with 75% saying the economy was in rough shape and 65% blaming Trump's policies for worsening it. The same exit polls showed the Iran war was still a significant factor — 63% of voters said going to war with Iran was the wrong decision, and roughly 74% called the war a "crucial" or "major" factor in their vote — but Republicans themselves ranked the economy above immigration as their top concern, while Democratic voters ranked democracy above the economy. The data underpins a rebuttal published in WSJ Opinion by Mark Penn and Andrew Stein arguing the economy, not the war, was the decisive driver of the GOP's losses.

Children’s Place Files for Bankruptcy With Plans to Sell Assets
The Children's Place filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on November 8, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey, listing roughly $881 million in debt against $742 million in assets. The retailer, which also owns the Gymboree brand, operates 514 brick-and-mortar stores in the U.S. and is now running a competitive bidding process to sell its assets, with interested buyers given until December 9 to submit offers and a sale expected to close by year-end. The company plans to lay off more than 700 New Jersey-based workers by February 2027, but says all of its stores and websites remain open for now.

Iconic Saudi Tower Hit as Yemen’s Houthis Target Riyadh
A Houthi missile barrage struck Saudi Arabia on Sunday, November 8, 2026, with falling debris damaging windows at the Aramco Tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District; Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry reported no injuries or fatalities. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted at least two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh as part of a broader assault that also targeted the Eastern Province's oil facilities, the southern border region, and King Khalid International Airport. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed responsibility, and the coalition said it had intercepted at least 15 ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and drones launched toward Saudi territory since the start of November 2026.
