Alice Huang, the Chinese Woman at the Center of a Spy Probe After Affair With Jensen Huang
Nvidia's board confirmed on November 8, 2026 that it had opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang's affair with Alice Huang (also known as Huang Le), a Chinese businesswoman whose Singapore-based cloud-computing company, Megaspeed International, was a major buyer of Nvidia's advanced chips; U.S. intelligence agencies reportedly believe she passed information to Beijing. Huang disclosed the affair to staff in an email, and the board said at the time it had "no reason to believe" national security or Nvidia trade secrets were compromised. On November 10, Huang resigned as CEO, and two days later the board announced it had retained a former federal prosecutor and a top Wall Street law firm to conduct a formal probe into the matter.

Nvidia's board launched an independent investigation, led by outside counsel Wachtell Lipton, into CEO Jensen Huang after he admitted in a note to staff that he had an affair with Alice Huang, a Chinese tech executive whom U.S. intelligence agencies determined had been recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to spy on him; the probe examined whether company secrets or national security were compromised, though the board said it had no reason to believe they were. Jensen Huang Doesn’t Believe in Succession Planning. It’s Become a Big Risk for Nvidia.
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, 63, remains in charge after acknowledging on November 8, 2026, an affair with a woman the company says may have been a Chinese intelligence operative tied to the Ministry of State Security, a disclosure that sent Nvidia shares down more than 4%. Nvidia's board said it found no evidence that national security or company secrets were compromised and affirmed it has confidence in Huang, who will keep leading the company during an ongoing investigation. Analysts flagged a deeper structural risk exposed by the episode: Nvidia has no clear successor, with Huang's direct reports shrinking from 60 in September 2025 to 33 more recently, and Melius Research noted the market had expected him to stay at least another decade. Huang owns just over 3% of Nvidia's outstanding shares, and the board's probe could still push the company toward more formal succession and governance planning.

Supermarket-Fridge Hacks Show Risk of Linking Everything to the Internet
Starting October 11, 2026, hackers remotely hijacked internet-connected refrigeration controllers at supermarkets across multiple European countries, including roughly 40 Delhaize stores, at least 15 Carrefour locations, five Spar shops and a Belgian butchery chain, by triggering defrost cycles that kept heating elements running until units warmed up; Belgian retailers alone discarded about €100,000 worth of spoiled food. The attacks exploited internet-exposed controllers made by Danish manufacturer Danfoss and US firm Copeland (and its Dixell brand); researchers at Midnight Blue separately disclosed six pre-authentication remote-code-execution flaws in Danfoss's AK-SM800A controller that could let an attacker take over a unit in about an hour. Security researchers found nearly 13,000 such refrigeration controllers openly reachable on the internet across 99 countries, many still using default passwords or outdated firmware, and France's cybersecurity agency ANSSI later attributed a related wave of attacks on supermarket fridges to Iran. The incidents prompted the EU's cyber chief to warn that routers and other internet-connected home and commercial appliances face similar risks.

AI Companies Scramble to Navigate a World Hostile to Data Centers
More than 300 U.S. cities, towns, and counties have enacted bans or moratoriums on new hyperscale data centers as of 2026, with 173 moratoriums passed in June alone compared to just 8 in June 2025, according to Axios. In the first three months of 2026, roughly 75 major data center projects worth over $130 billion were delayed or canceled amid organized local opposition, and a June Reuters poll found only 14% of Americans are comfortable having a data center built near them. New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed an executive order creating the state's first moratorium on new hyperscale data centers, while Pennsylvania, Michigan, and South Carolina are weighing similar pauses, and OpenAI has said it will stop using broad non-disclosure agreements with public officials on data center projects to address transparency concerns. Voters in 10 states also passed 40 of 41 local ballot measures in November 2026 aimed at constraining data center development.

Exclusive | NYSE to Settle Tokenized Stocks on Avalanche Blockchain
NYSE said on Monday, November 9, 2026, that it has selected Avalanche's blockchain to handle clearing and settlement for a planned 24/7 tokenized-securities trading platform, pending regulatory approval. Under the arrangement, NYSE will run a custom blockchain built on Avalanche's technology — tailored to its regulatory needs rather than using Avalanche's public chain — while NYSE's existing matching engine continues to pair buyers and sellers; Avalanche's chain takes over post-trade record-keeping to confirm ownership once a trade settles. Ava Labs said the deal cost NYSE nothing and has opened doors with institutional players, and AVAX's token price rose following the announcement. NYSE is targeting a fourth-quarter 2026 launch for the platform, contingent on regulatory approval.

Skydance Plans to Cut $6 Billion in Costs Following Warner Bros. Deal
Skydance Media detailed plans to cut at least $6 billion in annual costs within three years of closing its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, which was completed on October 6, 2026 using roughly $47 billion in new equity and $57 billion in new secured debt, leaving the combined company with about $87 billion in gross debt. The savings target breaks down into roughly $2.2 billion from corporate overhead and real estate consolidation, $1.4 billion from unifying streaming and broadcast technology, $1.4 billion from consolidating third-party vendor spending, and over $1 billion from streamlined marketing. Skydance expects to incur about $4 billion in one-time costs to achieve these synergies and aims to reduce net leverage to 3.0x by the end of 2029.

Jensen Huang’s Affair Rings Alarm Bells for Nvidia Investors
A proposed shareholder class-action lawsuit was filed against Nvidia and CEO Jensen Huang on November 9, 2026, in federal court in San Jose, alleging the company misled investors by failing to disclose Huang's alleged affair with Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, and its reported national-security implications. The suit, brought by retail investor Mark Kistenmacher, seeks to represent shareholders who bought Nvidia stock between October 9, 2025, and November 8, 2026. Nvidia shares fell $7.97, or 4.27%, to close at $178.67 around the time the suit was filed, wiping out roughly $190 billion in market value. Huang subsequently resigned as CEO, president, and board member amid the fallout, as Nvidia's board separately investigated reports of a second affair involving an employee.

Anthropic Prepares to Push Ahead With IPO Despite Market Jitters
Anthropic has launched its IPO roadshow and is proceeding despite a broader AI-stock selloff, offering roughly 825 million shares at an estimated $70 to $78 apiece, with pricing expected Wednesday, November 18, and Nasdaq trading set to begin Thursday, November 19, 2026 under the ticker "ANT." At the top of that range the deal would raise more than $64 billion and value the company at about $1.4 trillion fully diluted, down sharply from the $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion range floated earlier in the fall as investors pushed back on valuation. The company's filing showed revenue of $16.3 billion for the first six months of 2026, up roughly 1,300% year-over-year, alongside a large net loss tied to heavy AI infrastructure spending.

Exclusive | U.S. Balks at China’s Demand for Tightly Scripted Trump-Xi-Putin Summit
Washington is pushing back against Beijing's insistence that a planned Nov. 19 trilateral meeting between President Trump, China's Xi Jinping, and Russia's Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen follow a tightly scripted format, with each leader reading prepared statements rather than engaging in open discussion; the Trump administration wants a freer exchange instead. The summit, which would mark the first-ever three-way meeting between US, Chinese, and Russian leaders, is expected to touch on nuclear-arms issues (notably relevant given the lapse of the US-Russia New START treaty) as well as the wars in Ukraine and Iran, though Beijing has resisted folding nuclear talks into the trilateral format since its arsenal is far smaller than those of the US and Russia. The APEC summit runs Nov. 18-19, 2026, with Trump due to meet Xi before the joint session with Putin; Reuters has described the odds of a substantive breakthrough emerging from the sidelines encounter as "slim."
