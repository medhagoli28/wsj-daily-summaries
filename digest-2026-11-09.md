- **Supermarket-Fridge Hacks Show Risk of Linking Everything to the Internet** — Starting October 11, 2026, hackers remotely hijacked internet-connected refrigeration controllers at supermarkets across multiple European countries, including roughly 40 Delhaize stores, at least 15 Carrefour locations, five Spar shops and a Belgian butchery chain, by triggering defrost cycles that kept heating elements running until units warmed up; Belgian retailers alone discarded about €100,000 worth of spoiled food. The attacks exploited internet-exposed controllers made by Danish manufacturer Danfoss and US firm Copeland (and its Dixell brand); researchers at Midnight Blue separately disclosed six pre-authentication remote-code-execution flaws in Danfoss's AK-SM800A controller that could let an attacker take over a unit in about an hour. Security researchers found nearly 13,000 such refrigeration controllers openly reachable on the internet across 99 countries, many still using default passwords or outdated firmware, and France's cybersecurity agency ANSSI later attributed a related wave of attacks on supermarket fridges to Iran. The incidents prompted the EU's cyber chief to warn that routers and other internet-connected home and commercial appliances face similar risks. _Sources: <https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/02/why-are-supermarket-fridges-connected-to-the-internet>, <https://cybersecuritynews.com/supermarket-refrigeration-systems-0-day>, <https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-hackers-are-turning-grocery-store-freezers-into-heaters/>_

Voters in 10 states also passed 40 of 41 local ballot measures in November 2026 aimed at constraining data center development. _Sources: <https://www.axios.com/2026/10/31/ai-data-center-backlash>, <https://www.governing.com/infrastructure/data-centers-are-spreading-so-are-moratoriums>, <https://www.forbes.com/sites/zacharyfolk/2026/11/04/heres-where-voters-are-rejecting-data-centers-at-the-ballot-full-list/>_

- **Exclusive | NYSE to Settle Tokenized Stocks on Avalanche Blockchain** — NYSE said on Monday, November 9, 2026, that it has selected Avalanche's blockchain to handle clearing and settlement for a planned 24/7 tokenized-securities trading platform, pending regulatory approval. Under the arrangement, NYSE will run a custom blockchain built on Avalanche's technology — tailored to its regulatory needs rather than using Avalanche's public chain — while NYSE's existing matching engine continues to pair buyers and sellers; Avalanche's chain takes over post-trade record-keeping to confirm ownership once a trade settles. Ava Labs said the deal cost NYSE nothing and has opened doors with institutional players, and AVAX's token price rose following the announcement. NYSE is targeting a fourth-quarter 2026 launch for the platform, contingent on regulatory approval. _Sources: <https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/09/nyse-picks-avalanche-to-settle-tokenized-stocks-amid-wall-street-s-blockchain-race>, <https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-11-09-nyse-settle-tokenized-stocks-avalanche-planned-24-7-market-436208>_
