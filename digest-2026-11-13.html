Exclusive | Meta Testing New Prediction-Market App With Employees
Meta Platforms employees have begun internally testing a prediction-market app called "Takes," which was previously known internally as "Arena" when Mark Zuckerberg directed a small team to build it this past summer; the app uses a virtual currency called "cred" (short for credibility) rather than real money, letting employees stake points on the outcomes of questions or events and gain or lose cred based on accuracy. As of mid-November 2026, the app is not available to the public and Meta has not said whether or when it might launch more broadly, with the internal trial fitting the company's routine practice of "dogfooding" new products before any public release. The move signals Meta's interest in the prediction-markets space as platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket have grown in popularity, especially among younger users Meta is trying to attract.

Widespread Microsoft Outage Roils Businesses, Governments
A severe Microsoft 365 outage beginning late Thursday, November 12, 2026, knocked out Outlook, Teams and Copilot for more than 12 hours and dragged into a second day, affecting organizations in over 70 countries. Governments including those of Japan, Singapore, Australia and the UK reported disruptions, with the NHS, London's Metropolitan Police, and the U.S. House of Representatives among those hit, and Microsoft said the cause was a technical/server issue with no sign of malicious activity. The Anderson Economic Group estimated the outage could cost up to $7 billion in lost productivity, and Microsoft confirmed by Saturday, November 14, that services had been fully restored. The disruption forced some U.S. workers home early, pushed local governments to tell residents to call instead of email, and in Australia stopped some businesses from printing invoices and some schools from holding online classes.

ChatGPT Launches Wallet Feature Allowing Users to Pay With Agents
OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on November 13, 2026, letting its AI agent "dots" make purchases on a user's behalf without the agent or merchant ever accessing the underlying card number, with the user approving each transaction via Face ID or fingerprint; the feature is initially available only to ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18+ in the US with eligible US-issued cards, built in partnership with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa. Users add a payment card once and keep their existing card rewards and dispute protections, while OpenAI said it is not taking a cut of transactions to start. Longer term, OpenAI plans to let users set spending limits and pre-approve purchase categories so agents can act more autonomously without per-purchase approval.

Uber Says Personal Data Was Exposed in Third-Party Data Breach
Uber disclosed that an unauthorized actor used compromised credentials belonging to third-party AI firm Fireworks AI to access data Uber had shared with that vendor, affecting 149,346 U.S. drivers; the intrusion occurred around October 7, 2026, and Fireworks AI notified Uber on October 9, with Uber's investigation confirming the access by October 13. The exposed information included drivers' names, driver's license numbers, and driver's license images. Uber said it immediately severed its data connection to the vendor and, as of its notification letters sent around November 11, had found no evidence that the stolen data had been misused.

Our New Cybersecurity Reality: If It’s ‘Smart,’ It’s Hackable
Attackers seized remote control of Danfoss refrigeration controllers in hundreds of French supermarkets overnight on October 11-12, 2026, forcing units into continuous defrost mode that pushed internal temperatures as high as 50°C (122°F) and spoiled fresh and frozen stock at chains including Intermarché, Netto, Carrefour, and Monoprix. CERT-FR, France's national cyber emergency response team, confirmed widespread compromises of two Danfoss controller series (AK-SM and AK-SC), many running outdated or end-of-life firmware no longer receiving security patches. Two supermarkets in Alsace alone reported losing roughly €270,000 in spoiled stock, with nationwide losses potentially reaching into the millions of euros. The attack, followed weeks later by similar fridge-hacking incidents in Belgium, Sweden, and Ireland, has prompted EU cybersecurity officials to warn that internet-connected industrial and consumer devices — from fridges to routers to home appliances — remain a largely unsecured and expanding attack surface.

European Stock Indexes Turn Positive as Oil Drops
European markets reversed earlier losses to close higher on November 10, 2026, as the pan-European Stoxx 600 index rose about 0.9% to roughly 662.54, nearing its early-August record and recovering from a weaker open. Exclusive | AI Bots Are Using Credit Cards. Banks Are Racing to Keep Up.
JPMorgan Chase has begun blocking AI shopping agents from completing purchases with customers' Chase credit card numbers, even when the customer has handed the agent the card details, while Bank of America, Capital One and Citigroup cards are already being used by such agents when customers permit it; JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon warned on the bank's October 2026 third-quarter earnings call against letting "rogue agents" — "there could be thousands of them" — into its systems. The dispute centers partly on card classification, since the virtual cards many AI agents use get coded as commercial cards, prompting workarounds like Stripe's Link wallet, which issues one-time virtual cards that merchants say cost more to accept and complicate returns. Meanwhile card networks are racing to build rails for this activity: Mastercard introduced "Verifiable Intent" on March 5, 2026, and Visa launched "Intelligent Commerce Connect" on April 8, 2026, with Visa and OpenAI announcing on June 10, 2026 a deal letting ChatGPT agents pay with linked Visa cards within user-set limits. Consumer research cited by PYMNTS found 52% of consumers want to choose how they pay when an AI agent lines up even a $300 purchase.
