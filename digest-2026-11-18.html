Nvidia Reports Faster Growth, Says Jensen Huang Won’t Return as CEO
Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion for the period ended October 25, 2026, up 103% from a year earlier and ahead of a CNBC-polled forecast of $109.1 billion, with data-center revenue climbing 110% to $107.6 billion; GAAP and non-GAAP diluted EPS came in at $2.70 and $2.68, respectively. The results were delivered by Colette Kress, Nvidia's CFO who became interim CEO after Jensen Huang resigned effective November 12, 2026, following disclosure of personal conduct inconsistent with the company's code of conduct. On November 16, Board Chairman Harvey Jones confirmed the board does not intend to reinstate Huang and has begun a search for a permanent successor, considering both internal and external candidates.

Jensen Huang Breaks Silence, Signals Willingness to Return as Nvidia CEO
Jensen Huang, 63, said in his first public statement since resigning as Nvidia CEO on November 12, 2026 — a departure triggered by his admitted affair with a woman U.S. intelligence officials believe was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security — that he would be "honored" to return as CEO if Nvidia's board asked him to, citing an "outpouring of calls" from employees, investors, partners and customers. The statement, reported November 17, 2026, came as three House committees have summoned Huang for a closed-door interview and the Senate Banking Committee chairman has asked him and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify over national-security concerns tied to Nvidia's chip sales to China. Despite his openness to returning, a source on Nvidia's board told The Washington Post there are no plans to reinstate him, and Nvidia's board has said it has no reason to believe national security was compromised.

OpenAI Partners With NATO On Cyber Defenses
OpenAI announced it is giving NATO's Communications and Information Agency (NCIA) access to its advanced cyber defense AI models, part of a program called Daybreak, to help allied cyber defenders find and fix network vulnerabilities before attackers exploit them. NATO has used up to 300 billion input and output tokens per month through the partnership, according to OpenAI's head of national security policy, Sasha Baker. OpenAI also supported the 2026 Crossed Swords exercise run by the NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence, which for the first time included a track where participants built and evaluated AI agents for cyber operations using Daybreak. The company has extended similar cyber-model access to other European defenders, including the EU's ENISA and national cyber agencies in France, Germany, and Poland.

Temu Gets Fresh EU Warning Over Efforts to Curb Sale of Illegal Products
On November 17, 2026, the European Commission issued preliminary findings that Temu breached the EU's Digital Services Act by failing to adequately prevent the sale of illegal and unsafe products, citing ineffective product-compliance checks, weak content moderation, and a lax sanctions system that let traders commit repeated serious infringements before being suspended. Regulators noted Temu requires test reports for only a small share of high-risk goods—such as toys, electronics, and cosmetics—and often doesn't review those reports even when they are submitted. This follows a €200 million ($220 million) fine the Commission already levied on Temu in May 2026 for allowing sales of dangerous baby toys and defective chargers; if Temu cannot convince regulators it is doing enough to fix the problem, it could face a fine of up to 6% of its global annual turnover. Temu has said it disagrees with the Commission's preliminary findings.

U.S. Stocks Extend Slide on Nvidia, China Fears
The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 849 points, or 1.64%, on November 17, 2026, while the S&P 500 dropped 1.03% and the Nasdaq Composite slid 0.74%, marking a third straight day of losses as investors braced for Nvidia's earnings report due after the closing bell; at its session low the Dow had been down more than 1,000 points. The sell-off was fueled by scandal and controversy surrounding Nvidia — including reports tied to CEO Jensen Huang — plus renewed China fears after the Commerce Department moved to close a loophole that let Chinese firms remotely rent Nvidia's computing power abroad to evade chip export controls. Nvidia shares have fallen roughly 35% since their October 6, 2026 record high, erasing about $2 trillion in market value, even though the company's results, released that evening, beat expectations and included a $150 billion buyback announcement.

European Stock Indexes Lower Amid Market-Wide Falls
European stocks posted their worst session in eight months on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, with the pan-European STOXX 600 closing down 1.75% at 650.72 — its biggest one-day drop since March 20 — as 92% of its constituent stocks finished in the red. Germany's DAX fell as tech stocks led declines on renewed AI-trade jitters ahead of Nvidia's earnings, with ASML dropping 5.3% and SAP sliding 5.6% after its Chief Customer Officer resigned; the UK's FTSE 100, France's CAC 40, and Germany's DAX closed down 0.79%, 0.92%, and 0.85% respectively (on an intraday basis DAX losses exceeded 2%). Deutsche Bank also slid about 6% after media reports, based on leaked documents, detailed its dealings with clients including Russian oligarchs, Donald Trump, and the late Jeffrey Epstein, adding to the broad risk-off move alongside new US chip-export curbs and Middle East tensions.

Stocks Dip Ahead of Nvidia Earnings
Stocks fell for a fourth straight session on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, as Wall Street braced for Nvidia's first quarterly earnings report since CEO Jensen Huang resigned the previous Thursday amid an espionage scandal tied to alleged leaks of US export-controlled chips to Chinese AI firms. The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 848.75 points (1.64%) to close at 50,759.42, the S&P 500 fell 1.03% to 7,340.53 (its lowest close since late July), and the Nasdaq Composite slid 0.74% to 24,328.38 (lowest since April); Nvidia shares themselves were down roughly 16.5% since the scandal broke. Chipmakers broadly underperformed, with AMD and Micron each falling more than 3%, while investors also weighed Anthropic's upcoming IPO later in the week, reportedly targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion.

U.S. Stocks Fall as Nvidia Drama Continues
Nvidia shares fell further on November 16-17, 2026, as Wall Street continued to digest the fallout from Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation as CEO on November 12 amid reports of extramarital affairs, including one allegedly tied to a woman U.S. intelligence suspects of spying for China; CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor. Nvidia’s Finance Chief Kress to Lead First Earnings Call Following Huang’s Ouster
Nvidia CFO Colette Kress, who was named interim CEO after Jensen Huang resigned on November 12, 2026 amid revelations of extramarital affairs including one with a suspected Chinese spy, led her first earnings call on November 17 (Nvidia's first in its 33-year history without Huang). Nvidia reported fiscal Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion, up sharply from $57 billion a year earlier, with Kress emphasizing it was "business as usual" for customers and operations. The Four-Person Agency Cooking Up Kalshi Ads With Martha Stewart and Shane Gillis
Kalshi, the federally regulated prediction-markets exchange co-founded by Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara, has built its advertising around a small boutique shop called Es Muy Good, led by founder and executive creative director Steve Gonzalez alongside Steve Nasopoulos; the agency previously produced Kalshi's World Cup campaign starring Timothée Chalamet and a Marshawn Lynch "Beast Mode" spot directed by Craig Gillespie. In November 2026 the agency rolled out a Martha Stewart ad recreating her 1990s cooking-show style to warn against insider trading, alongside a four-minute spot called "The Weathermen" featuring Shane Gillis and Danny McBride as feuding TV meteorologists trading on weather and novelty markets like Costco hot-dog prices and cricket. Kalshi says the Stewart campaign grew out of a meeting between Stewart and Lopes Lara at this year's World Cup final, and the push coincided with the company tightening insider-trading controls across roughly 98% of its trading volume.

Volatility Has Fundamentally Rewired the AI Trade
Nasdaq's "no-brainer" AI trade unraveled over a volatile six-week stretch: the index peaked on Oct. 6, 2026, then by Nov. 5 the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 12.2% in a single session, and roughly $2.5 trillion in market value was erased in Nasdaq's worst day in nearly two years. Volatility intensified after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation, with Nvidia shares falling another 4.1% and Intel and Micron dropping 7% and 7.6% respectively on Nov. 16; the Nasdaq is now down more than 10% from its October high, putting it in correction territory, while the VIX jumped 18.7% to 22.91 and one-month realized volatility hit 22.5%, its highest since May 2025. Stocks briefly roared back on Nov. 11 with some of the strongest single-day AI gains in months, only for selling to resume days later ahead of Nvidia's earnings and the Anthropic IPO, underscoring how swings — not steady gains — now define trading in AI-linked names.

‘Protestants and Patriots’ Review: Good and Godly Resistance
D. G. Hart, a history professor at Hillsdale College, published "Protestants and Patriots: Presbyterians in the Age of Revolution" through the University of Notre Dame Press in April 2026. The book traces Presbyterianism's transatlantic political history from roughly 1560 to 1870, covering England, Scotland, Ireland, the United States, and Canada, with a focus on the 1630s-40s religious and parliamentary wars in Britain—about 150 years before the American Revolution—as the movement's most disruptive period. Hart's central argument pushes back on the common historical claim that Presbyterian church polity inherently breeds rebellion, instead tracing how these Protestants eventually accommodated liberal democracy. Christianity Today reviewed the book in March 2026, and Notre Dame Press published an author interview and excerpt in April 2026 to accompany its release.

It’s Hard to Protect Your Nest Egg from Nvidia
I now have solid, concrete numbers from legitimate outlets (CNBC, Motley Fool) to write the brief. Nvidia alone makes up roughly 6% to 7% of the S&P 500's market value, and together with Apple and Microsoft, the three stocks accounted for 21.59% of the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust as of October 2, 2026 — the highest three-stock concentration in the index's history, surpassing the mid-1980s peak of 13.4% held by IBM, AT&T, and ExxonMobil. A Motley Fool analysis published November 5, 2026 found that just six companies — Nvidia, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta — made up 30.8% of the index. Baird Strategas chief ETF strategist Todd Sohn noted Nvidia alone is now larger than the S&P 500's entire consumer discretionary sector, meaning a single disappointing earnings report or guidance cut from the chipmaker can move the broader index and leaves investors in "diversified" index funds far more exposed to one company's fortunes than they may realize. Advisors have responded by pointing clients toward equal-weight funds like the Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF (RSP), which caps each S&P constituent at roughly 0.2% of the fund, as a way to cut single-stock exposure.
