Thousands of Anthropic Employees and Investors Are Set to Reap Fortunes
Anthropic launched an employee tender offer in February 2026 sized at $5 billion to $6 billion, allowing current and former employees who had been with the company at least a year to sell shares to outside investors at a valuation of roughly $350 billion, up from $61.5 billion about ten months earlier. The company ultimately completed the secondary sale but fell short of the full $6 billion target as some employees chose to hold their shares ahead of a potential IPO. By comparison, Anthropic's subsequent primary funding round valued the company at $380 billion (and a later $65 billion Series H reportedly pushed the post-money valuation to $965 billion), with investor assessments suggesting at least 400 early employees could be worth $100 million or more, including several potential billionaires. Anthropic publicly filed its IPO registration statement on October 26, 2026, with the IPO price reportedly set at more than three times the tender offer price.

I Tried New York City’s New High-Tech Public Bathrooms—and Saw the Future
New York City is rolling out 17 free, touchless public restrooms from startup Throne Labs across all five boroughs under a $4 million, yearlong pilot launched by Mayor Zohran Mamdani; units sit near high-traffic spots like Yankee Stadium and Cooper Square, run 7 a.m. to 10 p.m., and are entered via text message, QR code, an app, or a tap card. Each self-contained, solar-powered unit needs no water, sewer, or electrical hookup, uses 21 sensors to monitor cleanliness and trigger automatic cleaning after roughly 10-15 visits, and includes a baby-changing station and ADA accessibility. For privacy and to limit misuse, the door automatically unlocks and opens after a 10-minute time limit, with warnings issued in English and Spanish before it does.

Uber Co-Founder Kalanick Teases Protest at Nvidia as Calls for Huang’s Return Continue
Travis Kalanick, co-founder of Uber, said at the TomorrowX summit in Austin on November 18, 2026 that he would show up at Nvidia's headquarters with a "wafer-encrusted" megaphone to lead a walkout of employees demanding Jensen Huang's reinstatement as CEO, a campaign that has reportedly gathered more than 7,500 signatories identified as Nvidia staff (about 18% of the workforce). Huang resigned as president, CEO and board member effective November 12, 2026, following board findings of personal conduct inconsistent with company standards. On Nvidia's November 17 earnings call, the first since Huang's exit, new board chair Harvey Jones said directors had unanimously accepted the resignation and do not intend to reinstate him, while reporting quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion and expressing confidence in interim CEO Colette Kress as the company searches for a permanent chief executive.

What the Blue Wave in Texas Means for Elon Musk’s Tesla and SpaceX
Democrats swept Texas's November 3, 2026 statewide elections for the first time since 1994, with Gina Hinojosa defeating Gov. Greg Abbott by roughly 7 points and state Rep. James Talarico winning the U.S. Senate race by about 12 points, while Democrats also flipped the Texas House. The results rattled markets tied to Elon Musk, whose companies are based in Texas: shares of Tesla and SpaceX slumped 3.3% and 3.8% respectively, erasing nearly $130 billion in combined value, despite Musk having spent close to $500 million trying to keep the state red. Analysts flagged that the incoming Democratic administration, which campaigned partly on data-center regulation, could slow permitting and impose new guardrails on the AI-infrastructure buildout SpaceX and Tesla are counting on, including a planned joint chipmaking venture.

Two Big Debuts Fail To Boost Stocks
Anthropic's shares surged roughly 17-34% above their $83 IPO price on their November 19, 2026 trading debut, pushing the company's fully diluted valuation to about $1.76 trillion in the largest IPO in history, but the enthusiasm failed to lift the broader market. The Dow Jones Industrial Average instead sank about 1,100 points (roughly 2.1-2.2%) to fall below 50,000, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each dropped around 2-2.3%, with more than 90% of S&P 500 stocks closing lower. The S&P 500's decline marked a sixth straight losing session, its longest losing streak in two and a half years, as a price war between Anthropic and OpenAI and broader doubts about the AI buildout weighed on sentiment. The pattern echoed other recent high-profile debuts that likewise failed to translate into gains for the wider market.

U.S. Stocks Slide as AI Bust Continues Despite Anthropic IPO
US stocks fell sharply on Thursday, November 19, 2026, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average sinking about 1,084 points (roughly 2.2%) below 50,000, the S&P 500 dropping about 2% for its sixth straight losing session (its longest such streak since 2024, leaving it down 8.2% from last month's record), and the Nasdaq Composite falling about 2.2-2.3%. The selloff came even as Anthropic's IPO — the largest on record — priced at $83 a share and opened near $111.50, briefly spiking as high as 38.6% above offer before paring gains to close up about 17.1%; the muted follow-through failed to revive broader AI-trade enthusiasm. Chipmakers and AI-linked names reversed early gains to close lower, with Nvidia erasing a 4.7% advance to finish down 2.7%, CoreWeave swinging from a 10.5% gain to a 5.5% loss, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closing 2.4% lower, while more than 90% of S&P 500 constituents fell across all 11 sectors.

What to Know About Anthropic’s Record-Breaking IPO
Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on November 19, 2026, selling 980 million shares to raise roughly $81.3 billion — the largest capital raise in IPO history, surpassing SpaceX's $75 billion offering from June. The deal valued the Claude maker at about $1.5 trillion at pricing; shares then jumped as much as 35% intraday before closing up 17% at $97.17, pushing its fully diluted market cap to roughly $1.76 trillion, making it worth more than companies like JPMorgan Chase, Visa, and Walmart. The listing delivered a massive windfall to Anthropic's co-founders, whose stakes are now worth roughly $20 billion each on paper, and minted many employee millionaires.

Babcock Shares Climb on Earnings Beat, Push Into Drones
Babcock International's shares jumped as much as 8% on November 19, 2026, after the UK defense contractor reported first-half results and outlined a push into autonomous drone technology as part of a new growth strategy. Underlying operating profit rose 13% to £227 million, with the underlying margin improving to 8.4% from 7.9%, beating consensus of 8.3%, while underlying earnings per share climbed 15% to 32.7 pence from 28.5p. Defense, which makes up roughly three-quarters of group revenue, grew 8% organically, led by nuclear and aviation, even as marine revenue fell 7% due to lower civil activity and Type 31 frigate program timing. The company also raised its interim dividend 12% to 2.8p, generated £186 million in free cash flow, and expects to complete an approximately £80 million share buyback by year-end.

European Stock Indexes Rise as Tech Sentiment Improves
European shares rose Thursday, November 19, 2026, snapping three straight sessions of losses, with the pan-European STOXX 600 index up about 0.2% to 645.07 points by 09:58 GMT after closing at a three-week low the prior day. Technology stocks led the gains, climbing roughly 1.6%, with chipmakers ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon rising between 3% and 5.5%, while France's Soitec jumped 12.7% after beating expectations with its fiscal 2027 forecast. The rally followed Anthropic's stock-market debut, in which the company raised $81.3 billion — a record for a market debut — valuing it at roughly $1.5 trillion and easing concerns about AI-sector fundraising. Separately, a November Bank of America fund manager survey found 67% of respondents expect European stocks to rise over the next one to three months, up from 44% in October.

Five Takeaways From This Year’s Tax Data
The IRS collected a record $5.6 trillion in gross taxes during fiscal year 2026, while issuing larger refunds to taxpayers following the Working Families Tax Cuts legislation signed by President Trump. Average refunds rose roughly 11% year-over-year, with total refund dollars up about 17-19.7% compared to 2025, reaching nearly $373 billion by mid-October 2026. New deductions for tips, overtime pay, and car-loan interest boosted many filers' refunds, while the agency's individual online accounts grew to about 37.6 million users, up 34% from the prior year. Despite workforce cuts of more than 25% under DOGE, the IRS reported no major disruptions to the 2026 filing season, though paper returns took about twice as long to process as the year before.

Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Customer Data Leaked by Hackers
Hackers published customer data tied to Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Chase on Thursday, November 19, 2026, after breaching third-party vendors rather than the companies' own networks, with a ransomware group calling itself "n0n" claiming responsibility. The leaked JPMorgan Chase file contained roughly 16,000 customer email addresses, some phone numbers and Chase account IDs, plus 124 internal staff email addresses, while Visa's leak involved data on more than 6,000 premium cardholders and Mastercard disclosed a limited cardholder subset; none of the three said card numbers or payment systems were exposed. The hackers had reportedly set a 48-hour deadline threatening to publish the data unless demands were met, and all three firms said their core networks remained uncompromised.

Trump’s Personal Lawyer to Step Down as Sullivan & Cromwell Co-Chair
Robert Giuffra, co-chair of Sullivan & Cromwell and one of President Trump's personal appellate lawyers, will step down from the firm's leadership on January 1, 2028, alongside fellow co-chair Scott Miller. The two will be succeeded by litigation partner Sharon Nelles, who will become the first woman to lead the 147-year-old firm, and capital markets partner Carsten Berrar; the firm announced the succession plan in November 2026. After the transition, Giuffra and Miller will remain at Sullivan & Cromwell as senior chairs and continue their client work, with Giuffra expected to keep representing Trump in pending appeals, including the hush-money criminal case and the E. Jean Carroll defamation matter.

Nvidia Says It’s ‘Business as Usual’ After Huang’s Ouster. Wall Street Doesn’t Buy It.
Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO, and board member effective November 12, 2026, following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy; CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement. On Nvidia's first earnings call without Huang, Kress told analysts "from a business perspective, it's business as usual," noting customers, suppliers, and partners remained "100 percent behind" the company, and Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue while raising its sales outlook. Board chair Harvey Jones directly rejected the possibility of reinstating Huang, rebutting Huang's own earlier comment that he'd be "honored" to return. Despite the reassurances, Wall Street reacted with skepticism about the leadership transition and governance fallout from the scandal.
