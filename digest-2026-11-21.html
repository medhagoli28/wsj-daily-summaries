Anthropic Mathematician Says AI Has Solved the Most Famous Problem in Math
Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpoge announced on Friday, November 20, 2026, that he used the company's Claude chatbot to develop a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, a prime-number conjecture dating to 1859 and one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems carrying a $1 million reward. Alpoge said the AI-assisted proof runs more than 250,000 pages and claims to resolve the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, with the Extended Riemann Hypothesis potentially solvable with additional work. The announcement, posted online rather than published in a peer-reviewed journal, has not yet been verified by the broader mathematical community. This follows other recent AI mathematics claims from Anthropic, including Claude Mythos reportedly solving the Erdős unit-distance conjecture earlier in 2026.

The Brother-Sister Duo Who Want to Keep Anthropic Weird
Dario Amodei and his sister Daniela Amodei co-founded Anthropic in January 2021 after leaving OpenAI, with Dario serving as CEO and Daniela as president and board chair; the pair, along with five other co-founders, built the company into an AI giant that recently went public, making each of the seven founders worth roughly $20 billion in the IPO payday, according to CBS News. Coverage from Yahoo Finance and CNN notes that despite his CEO title, Dario handles comparatively little day-to-day management, with Daniela running daily operations, and describes the founding team as including a college dropout, several PhDs, a former journalist, and the sibling pair who grew up squabbling over toys.

Exclusive | Nestle Eyes Autonomous Factory Lines and Looks to Google, Microsoft as Partners
Nestlé announced on November 20, 2026 that it is partnering with Microsoft and Boston Dynamics to expand AI use in its manufacturing operations, while also holding late-stage talks with Google about automating production lines, as the food giant pushes toward fully autonomous factory operations. The work centers on Nestlé's new Deep Tech Center in Orbe, Switzerland, where AI, smart sensors, and robotics—including Boston Dynamics' Spot robots running hourly thermal and acoustic inspection routes on packaging lines—are being tested to free up maintenance technicians for predictive work. No financial terms, contract length, or specific Microsoft product details have been disclosed.

China’s AI Firms Are Addicted to Nvidia Chips. Now They Are Facing Withdrawal.
Chinese AI companies are rapidly cutting their reliance on Nvidia, with Beijing's internet regulator ordering top tech firms to halt purchases of Nvidia AI chips and cancel existing orders as part of a broader push toward domestic semiconductors. Nvidia's share of China's data-center AI chip market fell to 34% in the first half of 2026, while Huawei's Ascend chips surged to 28% share after shipping 572,000 AI accelerators in the same period, and Huawei now claims to have outsold Nvidia inside China as of October 2026. Analysts project China-based vendors like Huawei, Cambricon, and Hygon will control roughly 90% of the domestic AI accelerator market in 2026, with Chinese executives saying they plan to allocate about 46% of their AI budgets to domestic products within a year. Even so, many of China's top AI developers still depend on Nvidia hardware to train their most advanced models, so the transition away from Nvidia remains incomplete.

California Attorney General Issues Investigative Subpoena to Nvidia
California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia on November 19, 2026, probing whether the company's advanced AI chips were illegally diverted to China in violation of U.S. export restrictions. The inquiry is tied to former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation amid allegations of an affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset, who had ties to Megaspeed, a Singapore-based firm under investigation for funneling restricted Nvidia chips into China. Bonta's office is examining whether Nvidia or Huang's conduct let China obtain advanced chips or confidential information, and whether the company misled investors and the public about that risk. Huang is set to testify before Congress on December 1, with Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter following on December 2.

The Drama Over Drone Delivery
Walmart and drone-delivery startup Zipline announced a multi-year deal on November 5, 2026 to expand to roughly 300 U.S. delivery sites, a sharp jump from the 66 stores the program covered as recently as May, with Walmart having logged more than 2 million drone deliveries since the partnership began in 2021; the same day, Zipline disclosed a $1.5 billion funding round valuing it at $21.5 billion, nearly tripling its valuation in under a year. The expansion intensifies competition with Amazon, which is pushing Prime Air toward nearly 500 U.S. communities by the end of 2026 while also raising seller fulfillment fees by an average of 19 cents per item starting in 2027. Rivals are also moving fast: Uber Eats and Zipline launched drone delivery in Spring, Texas on November 17, and Alphabet's Wing is expanding its own Walmart partnership toward 270 stores by 2027.

Tech Outages Are Inevitable. Business Chaos Doesn’t Have to Be.
A string of outages in November 2026—including disruptions to Microsoft Outlook, the FAA, and national security infrastructure—has shaken confidence in U.S. digital infrastructure and pushed "digital resilience" into investor focus, with one firm identifying a basket of 29 resilience-related stocks in the S&P 1500 spanning backup/recovery, cybersecurity, multi-cloud infrastructure, and data-center power. The July 2024 CrowdStrike outage remains the benchmark case: it cost Fortune 500 companies a combined $5.4 billion, with Delta Air Lines alone reporting roughly $500 million in losses, after a faulty Falcon sensor update bricked an estimated 8.5 million Windows endpoints. Broader industry data shows the stakes rising—Splunk's 2026 downtime resilience report put the annual cost of outages at $600 billion across the Global 2000, up 50% in two years, while New Relic's 2026 survey found high-impact IT outages now average $1.85 million per hour. In response, 84% of companies are diversifying or considering diversifying their software and service providers, and Morgan Stanley has flagged SpaceX as an "off-the-grid" beneficiary of the resilience push.

Even Personal-Finance Devotees Struggle With Roth Conversions and IRS Rules
Roth IRA conversions carry no IRS income limit and no annual dollar cap, but several technical rules trip up even experienced savers: the pro-rata rule requires aggregating all traditional, SEP, and SIMPLE IRA balances (using the December 31 year-end value, not the conversion-date balance) to determine what portion of a conversion is taxable, so a $100,000 combined balance with $80,000 pre-tax would make 80% of a backdoor conversion taxable. A 2026 conversion must leave the IRA by December 31, 2026 to count for that tax year, with no April filing extension, and estimated tax on the converted amount is generally due by the next quarterly deadline (mid-January) or a penalty applies retroactive to the prior April. Since 2018, conversions can no longer be undone through recharacterization, and savers who pay the conversion tax out of the IRA funds themselves (rather than from outside cash) reduce what actually reaches the Roth while still owing tax on the full converted amount.

TPG, Corpay to Buy Finance Platform Bill for About $6 Billion
BILL Holdings has agreed to be taken private by TPG and Corpay in a cash deal valuing the financial operations software company at about $6 billion, with shareholders set to receive $63.25 per share, a roughly 31% premium to BILL's 60-day volume-weighted average price through November 16, 2026. Houthi Attack Damages Saudi Capital’s Tallest Tower
A Houthi missile strike on November 19, 2026 damaged the Public Investment Fund (PIF) Tower, Riyadh's tallest skyscraper at roughly 385 meters and 80 stories, located in the King Abdullah Financial District; Saudi Civil Defence reported "material damage" across several floors of the tower and two adjacent buildings, including shattered windows on up to eight floors, with no casualties reported. The strike came after an earlier Houthi attack this month damaged a tower belonging to oil giant Aramco, marking the second hit on Riyadh's financial hub in November. Saudi air defenses continued intercepting Houthi projectiles in the following days, downing five drones and a ballistic missile on November 20 and three more ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh on November 21, as the Yemen-based group escalated strikes on Saudi financial, military, and oil assets.
