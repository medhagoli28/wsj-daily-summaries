- **The 11 Retirement-Planning Milestones Everyone Over 50 Needs to Know** — Key retirement-planning milestones begin at age 50, when savers can make catch-up contributions to 401(k)s, 403(b)s, and IRAs, adding $1,100 to the 2026 IRA limit for a total of $8,600 and at least $8,000 extra to 401(k) plans; those aged 60-63 qualify for an even larger "super catch-up" of $11,250 in 401(k) plans in 2026, allowing total contributions up to $35,750. At 55, workers who leave a job can tap that employer's 401(k) penalty-free under the Rule of 55, and HSA holders become eligible for a $1,000 catch-up contribution. - **How to Support Your Grandchildren Without Straining Your Finances** — This is a general personal-finance advice topic rather than a breaking news story, so there's no single underlying event with wire-service confirmation — it's covered independently across many personal-finance outlets. Grandparents can give grandchildren up to $19,000 per recipient in 2026 ($38,000 for married couples) without triggering gift-tax reporting, and can "superfund" a 529 plan with up to five years of gifts at once — $95,000 per individual or $190,000 per married couple per grandchild. Direct tuition payments made straight to a school are exempt from gift-tax limits entirely.
