- **Texas Pacific Land to Buy EagleRock Land in $3 Billion All-Stock Deal** — Texas Pacific Land Corporation agreed to acquire EagleRock Land in an all-stock deal valued at roughly $3 billion, with EagleRock shareholders receiving 0.0831 TPL shares per share held, implying a value of about $23.56 per EagleRock share. The combined company will control approximately 1 million fee-owned surface acres in the Permian Basin plus 105,000 acres dedicated to water services, with water-handling capacity rising 49% to about 1.5 million barrels per day and storage capacity increasing 79% to 68 million barrels. - **Asian Regulators Step Up Efforts to Calm AI-Driven Market Turmoil** — South Korea's KOSPI index triggered two consecutive circuit breakers in late 2026 as a global AI-driven selloff hammered chip heavyweights Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, with Korean regulators launching a "special intensive monitoring period" starting November 24 to track margin lending, forced liquidations, and illegal short-selling, while also relaxing daily share-buyback order limits for listed companies for three months. Taiwan's financial regulator tightened short-selling rules, and China's stock exchanges eased margin-financing requirements to cushion the rout. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell as much as 3.5% and the TOPIX dropped 2.6% in the same selloff, as chip-related and export-oriented stocks gave back recent gains. The moves came after roughly $2.5 trillion was wiped off U.S. markets amid fears that AI investment spending is overheating, with contagion spreading rapidly across Asian equity markets by November 23, 2026. _Sources: <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/asia-markets-kospi-taiwan-china-csi300.html>, <https://asia.nikkei.com/business/markets/japan-south-korea-stocks-plummet-as-ai-selloff-accelerates>, <https://us.cnn.com/2026/11/05/markets/asian-markets-us-rout-ai-investment-intl-hnk>_
- **Volatility Dents Consumer Mood of Wealthier Americans** — The University of Michigan's November 2026 consumer sentiment survey found a sharp wealth-based divergence: sentiment improved among lower- and middle-wealth households but fell roughly 5% among consumers with the largest stock portfolios, who reported weaker personal finances amid continued AI-related equity volatility. The pullback coincided with a turbulent stretch for markets — the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) jumped 26.2% to close at 35.89, its first close above 30 since late March 2026 — and with nearly $9 trillion wiped off global stock values since August peaks on AI-bubble and interest-rate worries. - **European Chip Stocks Surge After Nvidia Reinstates Huang as CEO** — Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive effective immediately on November 23, 2026, weeks after his unexpected departure had rattled the semiconductor sector, with the company saying it received input from senior U.S. government officials indicating the move posed no national-security concern. Nvidia shares surged roughly 33-35% to around $170 on the news, and President Trump called the board's decision "correct and proper." The rally spread to Europe, where the STOXX 600 closed up about 1.6-1.87% at 637.32, snapping five straight days of losses, as chipmakers including ASML and ASM International posted some of the index's strongest gains following the prior week's more-than-10% semiconductor selloff. _Sources: <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo.html>, <https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/nvidia-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-ceo-igniting-tech-surge-5045917>, <https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/european-stocks-rally-nvidia-reinstates-144744580.html>_
- **Nasdaq Jumps, Nvidia Surges After Huang Returns as CEO** — Nvidia's board unanimously voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO, and board director on November 23, 2026, just 11 days after he resigned on November 12 amid a scandal involving extramarital affairs, including with a woman identified as a suspected Chinese spy, and related national-security concerns; the board said it received input from senior U.S. government officials, including President Trump, and concluded his reinstatement posed no security risk.
