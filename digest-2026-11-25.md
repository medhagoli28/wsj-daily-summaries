## Tech

- **Huang Tells Staff He Is in Therapy, Apologizes in Nvidia Town Hall** — Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologized to employees during an all-hands meeting on November 24, 2026, a day after being reinstated to the top job, calling it the "hardest company meeting" he'd ever done. He told staff he has been in mental health therapy daily, roughly 90 minutes a session, and that it was helping him "a lot," while acknowledging he had violated Nvidia's code of conduct. The apology followed his brief ouster tied to reports of an extramarital affair with a woman U.S. officials suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence, as well as relationships with two other Nvidia employees. - **The AI Build-Out Is Sucking Up Resources and Crowding Out the Rest of the Economy** — AI-related capital spending is on pace to hit roughly $600 billion in the U.S. in 2026, equal to about 2% of GDP, with Goldman Sachs economists estimating that AI-linked investment categories accounted for a median 35.5% of quarterly U.S. GDP growth over the past six quarters, effectively crowding out other forms of business investment, especially in rate-sensitive sectors. The buildout is also straining physical resources: data-center electricity consumption is projected to roughly double from about 485 terawatt-hours in 2025 to near 950 terawatt-hours by 2030, and in Virginia, the top data-center market, facilities already consume more than a quarter of the state's power. Construction labor, equipment, and semiconductor fabrication capacity are similarly being diverted toward AI infrastructure projects, which carry gross margins more than twice those of typical non-tech construction, at the expense of housing and other building activity. Some economists, including those at Goldman Sachs, caution that the scale of this crowding-out effect may be overstated relative to its true drag on broader economic capacity. _Sources: <https://www.axios.com/2026/08/12/ai-boom-goldman-sachs>, <https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/ai-spending-boom-gdp-growth-crowding-out-housing-market-electricity-bills>_
