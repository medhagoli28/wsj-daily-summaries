Saudi Engineer Accused of Bugging OpenAI to Be Released on $1 Million Bond
Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national who had worked as an OpenAI engineer since July 2025, was released on $1 million bond after U.S. Magistrate Judge Alex Tse in San Francisco granted a defense motion on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, with Alfozan expected to wear an ankle monitor. Prosecutors allege Alfozan secretly recorded conversations in an OpenAI executive conference room, with investigators finding 540 recordings on one of his devices, dozens of which captured OpenAI meetings; he was arrested October 9, 2026. U.S. District Judge Vince Chhabria denied a last-minute government request to keep Alfozan jailed, ruling on Wednesday, November 25, that prosecutors' bid came too late since he had already been released.
MARKETS
Stocks Finish Lower After Wild Session | Markets P.M. for Nov. 25
The Dow Jones Industrial Average erased an intraday plunge of more than 1,100 points to close up 0.33% (164.25 points) at 50,182.95 on Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq-100 fell 0.64% to 26,046.90 after a session that saw the S&P swing roughly 3.2% from its low to its high. The volatility was driven by a renewed AI-stock selloff that hit chip and AI-related names hardest — Anthropic shares fell as low as $73.85, about 11% below their $83 IPO price — compounding a 5.9% drop in Nvidia the prior day, while hotter-than-expected inflation data added to the pressure even as Nvidia's CEO returned to the spotlight. The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) spiked to an intraday high near 35 before settling at 28.03, underscoring the choppy, whipsaw nature of trading ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

Worse Than the Dot-Com Crash: What an AI Bust Could Mean for the Economy
A new analysis by White House Council of Economic Advisers alumni Jared Bernstein and Ryan Cummings finds that a bursting AI bubble could cut real GDP by more than three times as much as the 2001 dot-com-driven downturn did, citing a stock market that has swelled to roughly 240% of U.S. GDP versus about 160% at the dot-com peak; separately, former SEC chair Gary Gensler told CNN on November 16, 2026 that he expects the AI boom to end in a bust and recession before the 2028 election, comparable in scale to the dot-com bust but not as severe as the 2008 financial crisis. The warnings followed a rough stretch for AI stocks, with Nvidia and Anthropic-linked shares sliding and credit-default-swap costs on tech firms rising as of November 23-25, 2026 amid investor doubts about AI infrastructure returns.
MARKETS
Jensen Is Back. But AI’s Problems Haven’t Gone Away.
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang returned to the top job this week after resigning days earlier amid revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a Singapore-based executive reportedly scrutinized for possible ties to Chinese intelligence; interim CFO Colette Kress had briefly taken over before Huang's reinstatement, which sent Nvidia shares up more than 30%. Despite the stock pop, analysts say deeper structural worries remain unresolved, including heavy reliance on "circular financing" deals in which Nvidia extends credit or guarantees to customers like CoreWeave that then use the money to buy its chips, and rising bond yields among heavily indebted AI data-center firms. Nvidia shares had fallen roughly 33% from their October peak amid the scandal, and economists such as RSM's Joe Brusuelas have warned the episode could still trigger a broader unwinding of AI-related valuations even with Huang back at the helm.
MARKETS
Anthropic Had the Biggest IPO Ever. The Trade Is Already Unraveling.
Anthropic priced the largest IPO in history on November 18-19, 2026, selling shares at $83 each — above its $70-$78 target range — to raise over $81 billion and value the company near $1.5 trillion, before shares opened at $111.50 (up 34%) and briefly touched a near-$2 trillion market cap. The rally reversed quickly: by Friday, November 20, shares had fallen roughly 20% from that opening trade, pulling the implied valuation down to about $1.63 trillion, amid a broader tech selloff described as Wall Street's worst week since 2020. The slide drew scrutiny because the IPO price already marked a huge markup from Anthropic's last private valuation of $965 billion just months earlier, fueling investor doubts about whether the public price was sustainable.
MARKETS
European Stock Indexes Mostly Higher as Industrials, Luxuries Gain
The pan-European STOXX 600 rose 0.48% to 635.78 on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, with the DAX up 0.27%, the CAC 40 up 1.03%, and the FTSE 100 up 0.55%. Gains were led by automakers and luxury names, as autos jumped 3.3% after J.P. Morgan reiterated its preference for truckmakers, tyre makers and luxury carmakers, while household goods stocks gained 1.6%, with Swatch Group up 6.2% and Richemont up 1.5%, and the broader luxury index up 2.5%. Investors were also looking ahead to incoming U.S. inflation (PCE) data.
FINANCE
The Retail Investors Who Believed Nvidia’s Board—and Got Burned by Its U-Turn
Nvidia's board publicly ruled out Jensen Huang's return as CEO on last week's earnings call, only to reverse that decision roughly 11 days later and reinstate him amid pressure from major investors including Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker. Some retail investors who sold shares after the board's initial statement, including one investor/content creator identified as Kukreja, took losses and had to buy back into the stock once Huang's return was announced, with Nvidia shares falling in premarket trading after the saga unfolded. Separately, retail buyers poured a record $111 million net into NVDA in the first 30 minutes of trading on the day Huang was initially ousted, per Vanda Research data cited by Bloomberg, while board director Mark Stevens had sold roughly 4.2 million shares across three transactions in late August and September for over $900 million combined. The episode has drawn scrutiny over the board's credibility given how quickly it reversed a decision it had firmly stated just days earlier.
