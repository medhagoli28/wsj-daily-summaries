- **Nvidia Tells Partners to Pause Shipments to Southeast Asia** — Nvidia has directed manufacturing partners, including Dell and Super Micro, to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia as of November 26, 2026, while it reviews whether regional partners meet U.S. export requirements. The move follows a Commerce Department rule change this month that closed a loophole allowing Chinese firms to remotely access Nvidia chips via data centers in those countries — a channel the Institute for Progress estimates supplied about 24% of China's AI computing power. The case of Singapore-based Megaspeed, which became the region's top Nvidia buyer before being accused of smuggling chips to China, has heightened scrutiny. - **A Game-Changing Move Just Gave America the Edge Over China in AI** — I have enough confirmed from NBC News, Fortune, and the Commerce Department rule details to write the brief. The U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on November 16, 2026, closing a loophole that let Chinese companies remotely rent access to advanced Nvidia AI chips housed in overseas data centers rather than importing them directly. The rule now requires export licenses for shipping advanced chips to Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore, and Brazil, and bars foreign data centers from giving remote access to companies headquartered in China, Russia, or Iran once they supply more than 12 million units of "aggregate total processing performance" to those customers. Investigators flagged a Malaysian firm, A201, which had built data centers specifically to funnel compute to a Chinese tech company, as a key target of the crackdown. The rule was developed jointly with the Treasury Department and National Security Council and follows reporting that remote cloud access to chips abroad had become a significant source of China's AI computing capacity. _Sources: <https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/us-closes-loophole-let-chinese-companies-use-top-ai-chips-rcna609634>, <https://fortune.com/2026/11/16/new-commerce-rule-nvidia-chip-singapore-malaysia-china/>, <https://www.techspot.com/news/114605-us-closes-ai-chip-loophole-china-rent-nvidia.html>_
- **The Mad Dash for Memory Chips—and the Pain It’s Causing Holiday Shoppers** — Memory chip prices have surged dramatically in 2026 as AI data center demand outstrips supply, with a 16Gb DDR5 chip's contract price climbing from $8.70 in October 2025 to roughly $48 by September 2026, and global DRAM revenue jumping 369% year-over-year in the third quarter to nearly $200 billion. Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron have sold out their high-bandwidth memory (HBM) production to AI customers months in advance—SK hynix has said its DRAM and HBM output is sold out through 2029—squeezing supply for the general-purpose memory used in laptops, phones, and other consumer devices. Analysts at Gartner and TrendForce project PC prices rising roughly 17% and conventional DRAM contract prices climbing 90-95% in early 2026 as manufacturers pass costs to shoppers this holiday season, with Costco among retailers warning customers of higher electronics prices. Industry watchers expect the shortage to persist into 2027 or later, since new fabrication capacity takes years to come online. _Sources: <https://www.techradar.com/pro/memory-price-skyrockets-as-demand-for-ai-data-centers-drives-369-percent-year-over-year-surge>, <https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/10/micron-ai-memory-shortage-hbm-nvidia-samsung.html>, <https://finance.biggo.com/news/72b434c6-44f9-4118-8922-d7e5874d305a>_
- **Jensen Huang’s Vision for the New Nvidia Is a Lot Like the Old One** — I have enough to write the brief with concrete facts on the reinstatement, stock move, and strategic continuity. Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia's president, CEO, and board member on November 23, 2026, just 11 days after resigning on November 12 amid a scandal involving extramarital affairs, one with a subordinate and another with a woman U.S. officials reportedly believe may have been spying for China; Nvidia shares jumped more than 31% on the reinstatement news. - **Pensions Trusted by Millions of Industrial Workers Make a Surprising Comeback** — Multiemployer pension plans, which cover millions of unionized industrial and trucking workers, ended 2025 with an aggregate funded ratio of 103%, the highest level recorded in Milliman's 18-year funding study and up from 97% at the end of 2024, driven by strong equity returns and continued employer contributions. About 69% of the 1,180 plans tracked (812 plans) are now funded at 100% or more, and 90% are at least 80% funded, a sharp turnaround from the funding crisis that hit plans like the Teamsters' Central States Pension Fund, which required a roughly $36 billion federal bailout to project solvency through 2051. Separately, corporate single-employer pensions have also rebounded, with Milliman's index showing the 100 largest U.S. corporate plans funded at 112.2% in August 2026, up from just 77% in 2012 and the highest level since 2001, prompting companies such as IBM, JBS Foods and Northwell Health to reopen or launch new defined-benefit pension plans to attract workers. _Sources: <https://us.milliman.com/en-GB/insight/multiemployer-pension-funding-study-year-end-2025>, <https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/milliman-analysis-corporate-pension-funded-174000001.html>, <https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/ibm-saves-billions-by-reopening-pension-plan>_

- **Braskem Agrees to Plan Restructuring $10.9 Billion in Debt** — Braskem's board approved pursuing an out-of-court "extrajudicial reorganization" to restructure $10.9 billion in unsecured financial debt, filing the petition in Brazilian court on August 24, 2026, after securing support from creditors holding 39.6% of the affected claims. Under Brazilian law, the Brazilian petrochemical producer now has 90 days from the filing date to win approval from the required majority of creditors so the updated plan binds 100% of the subject claims.
