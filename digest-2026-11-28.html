They Pledged to Give Away 10% of Their Income. Now They’re AI Billionaires.
Dario Amodei, Amanda Askell, Jack Clark, and Tom Brown are among more than 60 current and former Anthropic employees who had signed the Giving What We Can pledge to donate at least 10% of their income (or wealth) to effective charities, a commitment made before the company's AI boom made many of them wealthy on paper. Anthropic's seven co-founders separately pledged at the company's founding to give away 80% of their initial equity stakes, a commitment now reportedly worth more than $110 billion given the company's rising valuation. Across roughly half of Anthropic's employees who have joined some form of the pledge, total committed future donations are estimated around $279 billion. OpenAI's leadership has not made a comparably public or widespread 10%-income or wealth-pledge commitment.

Americans Are More Exposed to the Stock Market Than Ever Before
Corporate equities made up a record 48.2% of U.S. household financial assets in the second quarter of 2026, according to Federal Reserve Z.1 data, surpassing the prior quarter's 44.66% and eclipsing the dot-com-era peak. Households also directly own 42.1% of the U.S. stock market, the highest share since late 1998, as roughly $11 trillion in gains pushed total household equity holdings to about $74 trillion and overall household net worth to roughly $183 trillion. For context, equity allocation bottomed at just 9.5% of household financial assets in Q2 1982. Fed distributional data also show the wealthiest 10% of households hold about 87% of all household equity, with the top 1% alone owning just over half.

Despite the rout, Wall Street consensus still calls for S&P 500 corporate earnings to climb 33% in 2026 and 16% in 2027, and markets got a brief boost in early November after Trump's conciliatory tone following Democratic midterm gains. The Fed's next policy decision lands December 9, 2026, which analysts are calling a pivotal date for whether the market stabilizes or extends its decline.

Dubai’s Benchmark Stock Index Erases Wartime Losses as Banks, Property Shares Rise
Dubai's DFM General Index has erased all losses suffered since the Middle East war began on February 28, climbing roughly 14% in November to post its biggest monthly gain since April 2020 and rank as the Gulf's second-best market this year behind Oman. Banks and real estate led the rebound: Dubai Islamic Bank gained about 11% on one day and nearly 10% on another this week, Emaar shares are up 33% from their March low, and Aldar rose 28% after nine-month net profit climbed 19% to Dh7.1 billion. The index had been almost 19% below its pre-war close at its mid-March low, and JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley both upgraded UAE equities to overweight in November on improving earnings revisions and foreign inflows.

Asian Markets Rise as AI Sentiment Stabilizes

Stock Futures Jump as AI Trade Set for Rebound
US stock futures surged on Friday, November 27, 2026, as investors returned from the Thanksgiving break and bought back into beaten-down AI-linked shares; Nasdaq 100 futures rose as much as 2.86%, S&P 500 futures gained about 1.96%, and Dow futures added 0.33%. AI chip stocks including Nvidia, Micron, Intel, Broadcom, and SanDisk climbed as much as 10% in premarket trading after a sharp prior sell-off. Chinese Shares Lifted as Beijing Signals More Market Support
China's stock benchmarks posted their biggest one-day gains in two years on Friday, November 27, 2026, after Premier Li Qiang chaired a State Council meeting that called for a bigger role by "national team" investors and government-backed funds like Central Huijin to stabilize a market that had fallen to 16-month lows. The Shanghai Composite rose 3.73% to 3,606.49 and the CSI 300 jumped 4.17% to 4,063.06, their strongest session since October 2024, while the rally also lifted the yuan and spread to Hong Kong, where the Hang Seng gained 1.9% and the Hang Seng Tech Index jumped 6%. Tech and chip shares led the surge, with the STAR 50 Index up 15% and semiconductor sub-indexes soaring as much as 17-19%, both record one-day moves. The State Council also urged listed companies to boost dividend payouts and share buybacks as part of the broader push to shore up investor confidence.

Gold, Silver Rally as Market Sentiment Improves
Gold rallied to about $3,643–$3,650 per ounce on November 27, 2026, up roughly 1.5%–3%, while silver surged even more sharply, jumping around 5.7% to 8% to near $43.5–$43.75 per ounce, compressing the gold-silver ratio to about 83 from roughly 86.7 the prior close. The rebound followed a 15-month low in both metals and was driven by improved global stock-market sentiment, a weaker U.S. dollar, and recovering physical demand, which eased the forced selling pressure that had been hitting precious metals. Additional support came from China's commodities-boosting market measures, with the Shanghai Composite up 3.7% and the ChiNext index soaring 11.6%. Jay Puri, Nvidia's former global sales chief, carried on a multiyear romantic relationship with Cenly Chen, chief growth officer at Super Micro Computer, and the two married in March 2026, just days before federal prosecutors indicted Super Micro co-founder Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw and others for allegedly smuggling roughly $2.5 billion worth of Nvidia chips to China via a Southeast Asian intermediary. Wall Street's AI trade swung wildly through the week of November 23-27, 2026: the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) spiked from roughly 20 on Monday to above 37 by Wednesday's close, putting it on pace for one of its biggest monthly jumps since the COVID crash of February 2020, before markets staged a late-week rebound. The turmoil followed Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's abrupt November 12 resignation amid reports of extramarital relationships, his reinstatement by the board on November 23 after direct involvement from President Trump, and lingering doubts over AI infrastructure spending that pushed the Nasdaq Composite into negative territory for the year and dragged the S&P 500 and Dow into correction territory. On November 25 alone, the Nasdaq-100 fell more than 4% and the S&P 500 slid over 2% as AI-linked names were dumped, but stocks reversed sharply on November 27, with the S&P 500 posting its best week since April 2025 as battered chip and AI stocks rallied back.

The Car Industry Has a Seat-Belt Problem It Can’t Fix
I have enough solid, non-WSJ facts (NHTSA's own federal register notice, plus independent reporting from vehicle-safety.org) to write the brief. About 10,000 Americans die each year in crashes while unbuckled, roughly half of all vehicle-occupant fatalities, a toll that has barely moved in over a decade despite decades of automaker safety investment, and U.S. seat-belt use of about 90% still trails Canada and most of Europe, where rates run 95-99%. In July 2026 the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration said it would seek public comment on letting automakers use seat-belt interlock or "Seat Belt Assurance Systems," which limit vehicle function until front occupants buckle up, as an alternative compliance path that would exempt them from the current unbelted crash-test requirement under FMVSS No. 208. The Alliance for Automotive Innovation, representing most U.S. automakers, has petitioned NHTSA for this change, reviving a technology Congress banned in 1974 after a public backlash to a one-year interlock mandate that had driven seat-belt use up roughly sevenfold. A separate NHTSA rule set to take effect in 2028 would require seat-belt reminder chimes to keep sounding until the driver buckles up, rather than stopping after a few seconds.

Jensen Huang, Trump and the 11 Days That Rocked Nvidia
Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on November 12, 2026, after acknowledging an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, a former Singapore-based Megaspeed executive who was reported to be a target of Chinese intelligence recruitment, with CFO Colette Kress stepping in as interim CEO; four days later, on November 16, the Commerce Department issued a rule closing a loophole that had let Chinese firms access restricted American AI chips remotely via third-country data centers. President Trump told CNBC he personally pressed Nvidia's board to reverse course, saying "I made the deal," and Huang was reinstated as CEO on November 23, 2026 — exactly 11 days after he stepped down. The reinstatement drew immediate pushback from Congress, which said Huang, Nvidia executives, and the board still owe lawmakers answers about the company's compliance with U.S. chip export controls to China.

Treasury Yields Extend Fall After U.S. Data Flurry
The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 7 basis points on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, to near 4.86%, marking its eighth straight daily decline, while the 2-year yield dropped roughly 8 basis points to around 4.59%. The slide came after a flurry of delayed economic reports: the Commerce Department's PCE price index showed inflation accelerating to 3.8% year-over-year in October (core PCE at 3.3%), third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to a 4.3% annualized rate, and weekly jobless claims fell to 189,000, among the lowest readings since the 1960s. Despite the stronger-than-expected growth and hotter inflation data, investors piled into Treasuries for safety as a tech-led stock selloff pushed the Nasdaq 100 toward bear-market territory, pulling yields lower rather than higher.
