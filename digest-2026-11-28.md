- **The Car Industry Has a Seat-Belt Problem It Can’t Fix** — I have enough solid, non-WSJ facts (NHTSA's own federal register notice, plus independent reporting from vehicle-safety.org) to write the brief. About 10,000 Americans die each year in crashes while unbuckled, roughly half of all vehicle-occupant fatalities, a toll that has barely moved in over a decade despite decades of automaker safety investment, and U.S. seat-belt use of about 90% still trails Canada and most of Europe, where rates run 95-99%. In July 2026 the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration said it would seek public comment on letting automakers use seat-belt interlock or "Seat Belt Assurance Systems," which limit vehicle function until front occupants buckle up, as an alternative compliance path that would exempt them from the current unbelted crash-test requirement under FMVSS No. 208. The Alliance for Automotive Innovation, representing most U.S. automakers, has petitioned NHTSA for this change, reviving a technology Congress banned in 1974 after a public backlash to a one-year interlock mandate that had driven seat-belt use up roughly sevenfold. A separate NHTSA rule set to take effect in 2028 would require seat-belt reminder chimes to keep sounding until the driver buckles up, rather than stopping after a few seconds. _Sources: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/03/2026-11081/federal-motor-vehicle-safety-standards-no-210-seat-belt-assembly-anchorages>, <https://vehicle-safety.org/stories/interlock-52-year-detour>, <https://www.nhtsa.gov/node/138851>_
